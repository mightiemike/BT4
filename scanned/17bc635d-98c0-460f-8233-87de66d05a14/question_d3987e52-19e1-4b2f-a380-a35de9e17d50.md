In stackslib/src/chainstate/nakamoto/tenure.rs, check_first_nakamoto_tenure_change() synthesizes a `last_epoch2_tenure` NakamotoTenureEvent purely from the epoch2 parent header (coinbase_height = epoch2_header_info.total_work.work) with no cross-check against the sortition DB's own record of how many epoch2 sortitions actually occurred before the Nakamoto transition. Can an attacker who mines the very first Nakamoto tenure-change block choose a `previous_tenure_end` pointing to an epoch2 block whose total_work.work over/under-counts actual sortitions (e.g., due to a missed-sortition burn block that epoch2 accounting treats differently), causing the synthesized coinbase_height fed into get_matured_miner_reward_schedules to diverge from the coinbase_height an honest node independently derives by replaying epoch2 sortition history, so that MINER_REWARD_MATURITY-based maturation triggers at different heights on different nodes for the transition tenure? Equality broken: coinbase_height derived at the epoch2/Nakamoto boundary on node A == coinbase_height on node B for the same previous_tenure_end block. Impact: Critical, reward-maturation schedule divergence at the hard-coded epoch boundary, causing one node to pay a matured reward a block before/after another node, i.e., a state-root mismatch at that height. Proof idea: integration test booting two peers through the epoch2->Nakamoto transition with a deliberately inserted missed-commit burn block near the boundary and asserting get_scheduled_block_rewards_at_block returns different coinbase heights or reward totals between the two peers' chainstate.
