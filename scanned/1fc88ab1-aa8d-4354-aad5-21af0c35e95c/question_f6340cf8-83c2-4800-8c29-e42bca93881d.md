Attacker action: fund any Bitcoin wallet, take a genuine council-signed `BatchProofMethodId{body, signatures_with_index}` observed from any public source (testnet explorer, leaked draft, prior mainnet tx), and re-inscribe it verbatim from an attacker-controlled Bitcoin address before the intended broadcaster's transaction confirms. Preconditions: attacker can pay Bitcoin fees to outbid the legitimate broadcaster's transaction for block inclusion (e.g. higher feerate RBF or first-seen at a mining pool). Call sequence: attacker's inscription is included in an earlier block/position -> `run_l1_block` processes it identically to the legitimate one -> `BatchProofMethodIdAccessor::insert` runs against the attacker's inscription, not the authority's. Binding: `blob.sender()` used to gate every other `DataOnDa` variant == the sender actually checked for `BatchProofMethodId`, which is None here. Impact: High (not Critical on its own since the content is still council-authorized) - it degrades the intended AUTHORITY audit trail (the accepted transaction is not sent by the party who was supposed to broadcast it), but combined with the same-height race above, is the enabling primitive for the permanent-skip attack.
