check_normal_coinbase_tx (referenced in stackslib/src/chainstate/nakamoto/tests/mod.rs against nakamoto_block.check_miner_signature) validates the coinbase's VRF proof against the block's sortition_hash and vrf_pubkey, but does not itself validate that the coinbase's embedded recipient principal is authorized by the sortition winner's registered mining key. Can an attacker who legitimately wins a sortition with VRF key K submit a block whose coinbase recipient field is a principal controlled by a collaborating unprivileged third party who did not participate in signing or mining, such that check_normal_coinbase_tx returns Ok (since it only checks VRF binding to sortition_hash/vrf_pubkey, per the test assertions at nakamoto/tests/mod.rs lines 2118-2126) while make_scheduled_miner_reward then routes the ENTIRE coinbase+fee reward to that third party, breaking the identity that reward recipient == the specified recipient of the SORTITION WINNER (not an arbitrary third party with no stake in the sortition)? Precondition: attacker wins sortition and sets coinbase recipient_opt to any principal. Equality broken: recipient credited == recipient the sortition winner explicitly designated as *their own* payout target (this is by protocol design allowed, but if the recipient field is taken from an unauthenticated/unsigned portion of the coinbase payload rather than one covered by the block's signature, an attacker who does NOT own K could still forge a coinbase claiming to be K's tenure and redirect funds). Impact: Critical if the coinbase recipient field is not covered by the miner's block signature (check_miner_signature) -- confirm whether recipient_opt is included in the signed hash. Proof idea: build a Nakamoto block with a tampered coinbase recipient field distinct from the original signed payload, run check_miner_signature and check_normal_coinbase_tx together, and assert the block is rejected specifically because recipient tampering invalidates the block signature hash.
