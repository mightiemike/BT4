`BurnSamplePoint::make_min_median_distribution` (distribution.rs) builds `cur_commits_map: HashMap<_, _>` and `cur_missed_map: HashMap<_, _>` keyed by `txid`, then does `.remove(end.op.spent_txid())` to resolve chaining. If an unprivileged attacker submits two block-commits in the same relative window with colliding `spent_txid`/`spent_output` (e.g., two commits both claiming to chain from the same parent UTXO due to a permitted double-spend race at the Bitcoin-tx level being observed differently pre/post reorg by two nodes), can the `HashMap::remove` resolve to a different candidate on each node depending on which of the two commit txids populated the map first, breaking burn_dist(nodeA) == burn_dist(nodeB) and hence the sortition winner? Impact: Critical, chain split. Proof: construct both commits referencing the same `(spent_txid, spent_output)`, feed to two `SortitionDB`s in different arrival order, diff resulting `BurnSamplePoint.candidate.txid`.
