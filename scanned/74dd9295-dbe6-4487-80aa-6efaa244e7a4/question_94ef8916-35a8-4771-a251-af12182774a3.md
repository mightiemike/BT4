Target: `check_normal_coinbase_tx` (mod.rs:1808-1856) VRF verification only, no recipient/fee binding. Attacker action: attacker who wins sortition submits a coinbase tx with a VALID VRF proof (satisfying `check_normal_coinbase_tx`) but crafts the accompanying tenure-change/coinbase pair such that `coinbase_tx.get_origin()` (used in `make_scheduled_miner_reward` for `miner_addr`) differs from the `pubkey_hash` validated in `check_tenure_tx` against the sortition winner's leader key. Preconditions: attacker controls the coinbase tx's signing key independently of the tenure-change miner key (two different keys, both attacker-owned, no other party involved). Call sequence: `validate_normal_against_burnchain` calls `check_miner_signature`+`check_tenure_tx` (binds tenure to leader-key-recovered hash160) and separately `check_normal_coinbase_tx` (binds VRF only) -- neither cross-checks that `coinbase_tx.get_origin()`'s address equals the recovered miner pubkey hash. Equality: reward's `MinerPaymentSchedule.address`/`.recipient` == the SORTITION WINNER's identity (the leader key that won), not an arbitrary key the attacker also controls but that never won any sortition. Impact: Critical -- lets an attacker route a matured reward to an unrelated address that never won VRF/sortition, breaking the 'paid to the winner' identity while the block still validates. Proof idea: craft a Nakamoto block whose coinbase tx origin key != tenure-change miner key, submit through `validate_normal_against_burnchain`, and check whether block acceptance rejects the mismatch or whether `make_scheduled_miner_reward`'s `miner_addr` silently becomes the coinbase-tx signer instead of the sortition winner.
