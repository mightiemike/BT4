Target: `calculate_paid_rewards` and `dispatcher_announce_burn_ops` (stackslib/src/chainstate/coordinator/mod.rs) versus the *actual* winning block-commit determined by sortition. Attacker action: attacker submits a `LeaderBlockCommit` with `commit_outs` splitting `burn_fee` across multiple PoX addresses including one attacker-controlled minority address, while a *different*, higher-burn attacker-owned commit at the same height ultimately wins the VRF-weighted sortition; `calculate_paid_rewards` is computed over all `ops` in the burn block regardless of which one wins. Preconditions: attacker submits two of their own block-commits (self-competing) in the same burn block, only one of which wins sortition. Call sequence: handle_new_epoch2_burnchain_block -> calculate_paid_rewards(&ops) (iterates *all* LeaderBlockCommit ops, not just the winner) -> dispatcher_announce_burn_ops. Equality that breaks: reward paid (as announced/dispatched) == reward earned by the sortition winner only; since calculate_paid_rewards aggregates PoX payouts from every commit_outs in the block (losers included, since Bitcoin already paid the PoX addresses regardless of sortition outcome), a losing commit's payout is reported alongside the winner's, and downstream consumers relying on this event for reward accounting could double count if not filtered by winning txid. Scoped impact: High (bounded to event-dispatcher/off-chain accounting, not on-chain STX balances) unless the same aggregate is used to gate a Clarity-side reward-maturation equality, in which case Critical. Proof idea: submit two self-owned block-commits with different commit_outs in one burn block, only one wins; assert `calculate_paid_rewards` output includes both commits' payouts and does not equal `paid_rewards` restricted to `tenure_burn_chain_tip.winning_block_txid` alone.
