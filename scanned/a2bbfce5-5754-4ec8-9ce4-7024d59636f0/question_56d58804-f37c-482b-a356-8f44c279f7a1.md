Can an attacker who mines two competing block-commits in sequential sortitions (S_n, S_n+1) manipulate `parent_block_ptr`/`parent_vtxindex` in S_n+1's commit to point at a losing (non-winning) commit in S_n instead of the actual sortition winner, so `get_parent_vrf_proof` derives its 'parent tenure' VRF proof from a tenure that never actually won a sortition and thus never produced a canonical block, given preconditions of at least one losing commit existing in sortition S_n? Equality that must hold: the `parent_sortition_id` resolved for VRF-proof lookup purposes must equal the sortition that actually won and had its block accepted, i.e. `parent_sn.winning_block_txid` must correspond to a processed tenure-start block, not merely 'some sortition the pointer names.' Scoped impact: Critical - if a losing-commit's tenure (which produced no canonical block, hence no coinbase, hence no VRF proof) is targeted, `get_block_vrf_proof` returns `None`/errors, but if a *different* winning tenure that isn't actually this block's true ancestor is targeted, the seed check would validate against an unrelated tenure's randomness, breaking the block<->committed-randomness identity and enabling a form of seed-grinding across tenures. Exposed: all nodes; deterministic static validation.
