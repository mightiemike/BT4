Target: `NakamotoChainState::get_parent_vrf_proof` (nakamoto/mod.rs), specifically its dependency on `SortitionDB::get_block_commit_parent_sortition_id`. Can an attacker craft a `LeaderBlockCommitOp.parent_block_ptr`/`parent_vtxindex` pointing to a sortition that did NOT produce the block referenced by `header.parent_block_id`, such that `get_parent_vrf_proof` retrieves the VRF proof of a *different* tenure than the one the Nakamoto block header actually claims as its parent, given preconditions of at least two candidate parent tenures at the same or nearby block heights? Call sequence: `validate_vrf_seed` calls `get_parent_vrf_proof(chainstate_conn, header.parent_block_id, sortdb_conn, header.consensus_hash, block_commit.txid)` -> internally resolves `parent_sortition_id` via the block-commit's on-chain pointer, independent of `header.parent_block_id` -> looks up VRF proof by `parent_sn.consensus_hash`. Equality that must hold: the sortition resolved from `block_commit`'s `(parent_block_ptr, parent_vtxindex)` chain == the sortition that actually produced `header.parent_block_id`'s tenure. Scoped impact: Critical - if these two resolutions diverge, a block could pass `validate_vrf_seed` using a VRF proof from a tenure unrelated to its claimed parent, letting an attacker mint a syntactically 'valid' seed-binding while stitching together non-contiguous tenures, corrupting the reproducible tenure history nodes rely on. Exposed: all nodes; this is a consensus-critical mismatch, not a UI bug.
