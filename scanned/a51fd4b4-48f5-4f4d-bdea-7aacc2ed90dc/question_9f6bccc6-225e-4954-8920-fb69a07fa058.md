Target: `handle_poison_microblock`'s `sender_principal` (derived from `invoke_ctx.sender`, transactions.rs ~735) versus the actual entity that detected/constructed the fork proof. Attacker action: an unprivileged attacker who merely relays a poison-microblock pair discovered by someone else (or by the honest miner's own node broadcasting via `load_and_vet_parent_microblocks`) front-runs by submitting the identical `PoisonMicroblock(h1,h2)` payload from their own address with higher fee/priority before the original detector's transaction is mined. Preconditions: honest node has locally detected the fork (`validate_parent_microblock_stream` or `load_descendant_staging_microblock_stream_with_poison`) and queued its own poison tx in the mempool, but has not yet been mined. Call sequence: attacker copies the public payload bytes from the mempool/P2P broadcast, wraps it in their own `StacksTransaction` with their own auth, and gets it mined first; `handle_poison_microblock` uses `invoke_ctx.sender` (the attacker) as `reporter_principal`. Equality that breaks: reward paid == reward earned by the party whose CPU/detection work found the offense, but instead it is paid to whichever principal wins the mempool fee race, regardless of who actually detected it. Impact: High, reward mis-payment bounded to the coinbase commission fraction (front-running theft of a legitimate detector's reward, not a chain-split). Proof idea: mempool integration test submitting the identical poison payload from two different signers and confirming only fee/nonce ordering (not detection priority) determines the recorded `reporter_principal` in `get_poison_microblock_report`.
