Target: `BitcoinVerifier::verify_transactions` `Complete`-kind blob push omitting compression validation (`// complete.body is compressed, but we'll leave the compression to circuit logic`) versus native `extract_relevant_zk_proofs`'s eager `self.decompress_chunks(&compressed_zk_proof)` call which *does* decompress and drops the proof entirely (`continue`) on decompression failure. Attacker action: inscribe a `Complete`-kind reveal tx signed by the real prover's key is not attacker-reachable (requires the prover's private key to pass `get_sig_verified_hash`), but an attacker can still inscribe a `Complete`-kind tx with an *arbitrary unsigned/attacker-signed* body that fails `get_sig_verified_hash` and is dropped identically on both sides at the signature check - so no divergence from this angle. However, note the asymmetry itself: native extraction eagerly attempts decompression as a filter (dropping malformed-but-signed bodies before ever reaching `verify_transactions`, which does not decompress at the verifier layer at all) - since decompression failure requires the real signature to matter, and that requires the prover key, this remains unreachable to an unprivileged attacker. Flag as not attacker-reachable under the stated threat model; no question generated for the decompression-timing asymmetry itself.
