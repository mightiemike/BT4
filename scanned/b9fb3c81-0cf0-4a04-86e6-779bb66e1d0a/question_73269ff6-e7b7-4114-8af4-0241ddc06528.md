pox_5_make_signer_set (stackslib/src/chainstate/nakamoto/signer_set.rs) apportions weight via `weight: stacked_amt / threshold` plus a largest-remainder ('Hare') round that hands leftover slots to signers sorted by `b.remainder.cmp(&a.remainder).then_with(|| a.signing_key.cmp(&b.signing_key))`. Can an unprivileged attacker with a minority stake craft their stacked_amt so their remainder exactly ties with another signer's remainder, and manipulate their own signing_key's byte value (which they control, since it's their own public key) to sort lower and thus deterministically win the leftover slot ahead of a legitimate signer with equal remainder but a higher-valued key, effectively stealing one reward_slot's worth of signing weight from another signer through key-selection rather than stake? Preconditions: an attacker who can choose/generate their own signing key to have a favorable low byte pattern, combined with a stake chosen to create a remainder tie with a target signer. Equality broken: not directly SIGNING's threshold equality, but the reward-set weight allocation must equal 'each signer's weight is a function of their stake, not their key's sort order' -- if this is by-design deterministic tie-breaking (as the code comments state 'ties broken by signing_key ascending... deterministic'), it is *not* a bug since determinism holds identically on all nodes; frame this as verifying determinism (DETERMINISM: sortition/weight-allocation verdict on node A == node B) rather than a fairness defect, since the tie-break itself doesn't break consensus, only fairness. Impact: not in scope as a consensus/chain-split bug -- explicitly confirm the tie-break is deterministic and does not create divergence. Proof idea: property test (extending pox_5_make_signer_set_props in stackslib/src/chainstate/nakamoto/tests/signer_set.rs) with an attacker-chosen key colliding on remainder with a victim key, asserting the allocation is identical across repeated independent runs/nodes (i.e., deterministic, hence out-of-scope as a split vector) -- included here to explicitly rule it out as a Critical/High finding.
