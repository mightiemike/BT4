`StacksChainState::make_scheduled_miner_reward` (blocks.rs, epoch-2 path) and `NakamotoChainState::make_scheduled_miner_reward` (tenure.rs, Nakamoto path) are two independently-maintained implementations of the identical recipient-selection logic (`recipient_opt.cloned().unwrap_or(miner_addr.to_account_principal())` gated on `epoch_id >= StacksEpochId::Epoch21`). Since these are hand-duplicated rather than shared, can a future or existing divergence between the two functions' `coinbase_reward_ustx`/fee-accounting parameters (e.g., differing treatment of `parent_fees` vs `anchored_fees`/`streamed_fees`) cause the *same* logical tenure, when straddling the epoch-2-to-Nakamoto transition, to be scheduled with a reward under one function's accounting that doesn't equal coinbase+fees actually earned under the other's definition — and is there a test enforcing that both implementations agree at the transition boundary? Equality: reward scheduled for the boundary tenure == coinbase(tenure) + fees(tenure) under a single, consistent accounting regardless of which `make_scheduled_miner_reward` variant executed. Impact: Critical if the mismatch causes an over-credit at the boundary. Proof idea: integration test that mines the exact tenure that triggers the epoch-2->Nakamoto switch, capturing whichever `make_scheduled_miner_reward` variant is invoked, and diff the resulting `MinerPaymentSchedule.coinbase + tx_fees` against manually reconstructed coinbase+fees for that tenure from the raw transactions.
