### Title
Pausing `accrue` freezes the index but not `last-update`, causing the entire paused interval to be retroactively charged at the post-unpause rate - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar] and the identical pattern in v0-vault-sbtc.clar, v0-vault-ststx.clar, v0-vault-usdc.clar, v0-vault-usdh.clar, v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar)

### Summary
Each vault's `accrue` function skips updating `last-update` while the `accrue` pause flag is set, but resumes computing `time-delta` from that stale `last-update` once unpaused. The interest/liquidity rate used for the whole elapsed interval — including the frozen/paused window — is the rate in effect at the moment `accrue` is finally called, not the (possibly very different) rates that would have applied during the paused period. This mirrors the reported bug class: a single rate is applied uniformly across a period that should have been split into differently-rated sub-periods, misallocating yield between users who transact before/after the pause.

### Finding Description
`accrue` reads the pause flag and, when paused, returns the current index unchanged without ever touching `last-update`: [1](#0-0) 

When unpaused, `next-index` / `next-liquidity-index` compute: [2](#0-1) 

`time-delta = stacks-block-time - last-update` (var-get last-update)`, and this delta is fed straight into `calc-multiplier-delta` together with `interest-rate` computed from the *current* utilization — i.e., the rate sampled at (or after) unpause time. Because `last-update` was never advanced during the pause, the very first `accrue()` call after unpause treats the *entire* paused duration as if it accrued continuously at the post-unpause rate: [3](#0-2) 

The `var-set last-update stacks-block-time` line only fires when the index actually changed, further reinforcing that the timestamp bookkeeping is coupled to "did the index move" rather than "did time pass" — the same "clock advanced only on change" pattern that lets a stale value get retroactively reinterpreted under a different rate later. [4](#0-3) 

This is a single-call (single accrue invocation) mathematical mixing of two distinct rate periods (the paused/frozen window and the live window) into one multiplier, exactly analogous to `_notifyReward`'s `leftover` being re-spread over the new `rewardsDuration` at a blended rate rather than being kept separate.

### Impact Explanation
Because the pause is a pass-through (`ok {...}` unchanged) rather than a revert, deposits/redemptions and borrows/repays can still occur while `accrue` is paused, changing `total-supply`, `principal-scaled`, and utilization. When the pause is later lifted, the very next `accrue()` call applies one uniform rate (the rate implied by utilization at/after unpause) to the *entire* elapsed real-world duration, including the paused window during which the "true" accrual should have been zero (or governed by whatever rate existed then). Depending on the direction of the rate/utilization change across the pause window, this either:
- Grants suppliers/borrowers who are present at the moment of the catch-up `accrue()` call unclaimed yield they should never have received (windfall paid at the expense of the shared interest/liquidity index), or
- Permanently deprives suppliers who held through the pause of yield they should have accrued, because it gets computed at a different (lower) rate than what should have applied incrementally.

Since suppliers/borrowers can enter or exit between the unpause and the next `accrue()` call, this is a real misallocation of unclaimed yield among vault participants tied to the exact accrual mechanics of these contracts — matching the in-scope High-severity class "theft of unclaimed yield ... or permanent freezing of unclaimed yield," not a two-user-griefing scenario, since the root cause is the protocol's own timestamp bookkeeping rather than one user interfering with another.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires the DAO/admin to toggle the `accrue` pause flag off after it was on for some duration and utilization/rate to have shifted meaningfully in between (a routine operational event, e.g. temporary pause during an incident or upgrade). No attacker privilege is needed to trigger the mis-accrual itself — only ordinary users transacting around the pause/unpause boundary are needed to realize the misallocated yield, making this readily reachable whenever an operational pause of `accrue` is used, which the pause-state struct explicitly supports as first-class functionality.

### Recommendation
When toggling the `accrue` pause flag off (or on), snapshot `last-update` to `stacks-block-time` immediately so that no elapsed time is ever attributed across the pause boundary; alternatively, track `last-update` unconditionally on every `accrue()` invocation (regardless of whether the index actually changed) so paused time never gets silently absorbed into a single post-pause rate calculation. If desired, split the elapsed interval into pre/post-pause segments and apply a stored rate to each, analogous to the C4 report's queue-of-rewards mitigation.

### Proof of Concept
1. At `t0`, DAO sets `pause-states.accrue = true`. `last-update` remains `t0`.
2. Between `t0` and `t1`, borrowers/suppliers still deposit/borrow/repay/redeem (all other pause flags remain `false`), shifting `principal-scaled`/`total-borrowed`/utilization, while `accrue()` returns the frozen `idx`/`lidx` unchanged and `last-update` stays at `t0`. [1](#0-0) 
3. At `t1`, DAO sets `pause-states.accrue = false`. Interest rate for the new utilization is now, say, materially different from what it was at `t0`.
4. The next call that triggers `accrue()` (any deposit/redeem/borrow/repay) computes `time-delta = t1 - t0` (the whole paused span, plus any further delay) and applies the interest rate sampled at that moment across the *entire* interval via `calc-multiplier-delta`: [2](#0-1) 
5. `index`/`lindex` jump by an amount corresponding to `(t1 - t0)` at the post-pause rate, crediting/debiting that yield to whichever principal-scaled balances exist at the moment of that call — not to the balances/utilization that actually existed during `[t0, t1]`. Users who entered right before this catch-up call (after unpause but before the triggering transaction) can capture yield attributable to the paused period they were never exposed to; users who exited during the pause lose yield they should have earned had the rate genuinely applied incrementally.
