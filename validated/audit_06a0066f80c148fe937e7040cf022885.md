### Title
Reward-sandwiching via stale liquidity index during an `accrue`-paused window - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar])

### Summary
When a vault's `pause-states.accrue` flag is set, `next-index()` and `next-liquidity-index()` return the *current* (frozen) index/lindex instead of advancing them, and the public `accrue` function is a no-op pass-through that also skips updating `last-update`. Because `last-update` is not reset when the pause begins, the elapsed-time delta used by the interest formula keeps growing silently. The first `accrue()` call after the pause is lifted then applies the *entire* pause-duration's worth of interest in one instantaneous jump. A user who deposits while the freeze is active (buying shares at the stale, undervalued price) and redeems immediately after the pause is lifted (selling at the post-catch-up price) captures interest that should have accrued gradually to the depositors who actually held capital in the vault during that window — the exact "reward sandwiching" pattern described in the source report, but triggered by a pause/resume cycle instead of a discrete reward deposit.

### Finding Description
Each vault (e.g. `v0-vault-usdc.clar`) stores a `pause-states` record with an independent `accrue` boolean field: [1](#0-0) 

`next-index` and `next-liquidity-index` short-circuit to the stored (stale) index when `accrue` is paused, without touching `last-update`: [2](#0-1) 

The public `accrue()` entry point (invoked at the top of every `deposit`/`redeem`/borrow/repay call) only advances `last-update` when the computed `next`/`nliq` values actually differ from the stored ones — which never happens while paused: [3](#0-2) 

Because `last-update` is frozen at the pre-pause timestamp, when the pause is lifted the very next `accrue()` call computes `time-delta = stacks-block-time - last-update`, which spans the *entire* pause duration plus any time since. This produces one large index jump instead of the gradual, continuously-compounded growth the model is designed to produce. `deposit` and `redeem` both call `accrue()` first, then price shares off `total-assets-preview` / `total-supply-preview`, which in turn depend on `next-index()`: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Sequence:
1. Governance (or an emergency responder) pauses `accrue` for a vault (e.g., during a maintenance window). `last-update` stops advancing even though `stacks-block-time` keeps moving.
2. While still paused, the attacker calls `deposit`. Their `accrue()` call is a pass-through, so `convert-to-shares-preview` prices their deposit at the stale (frozen) index — the price does not yet reflect the interest that is formulaically "owed" for elapsed time.
3. Governance unpauses `accrue`.
4. The next `accrue()` invocation (triggered by any actor's operation, or by the attacker's own subsequent call) computes `time-delta` back to the pre-pause `last-update`, retroactively realizing the full pause-window interest in a single index jump. `treasury-lp` shares are minted to the DAO treasury for its reserve cut, and the remainder of the jump raises the redemption value of *all* currently outstanding zTokens, including the ones the attacker just bought cheaply.
5. The attacker immediately calls `redeem`, which again calls `accrue()` first (now already caught up) and converts their shares at the new, inflated price via `convert-to-assets-preview`.

### Impact Explanation
The attacker captures a pro-rata share of interest that accrued (per the protocol's own formula) during a period in which their capital was not actually locked in the vault, diluting the yield of legitimate suppliers who held shares throughout the pause. This is theft of unclaimed yield — an in-scope High-impact class — extracted from other zToken holders without any economic activity or risk on the attacker's part, purely by timing entry/exit around the pause/resume boundary.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation only requires: (a) knowledge that a pause on `accrue` is in effect or about to be lifted (publicly observable on-chain), and (b) a deposit followed by a redeem — both permissionless vault operations available to any account, with no minimum holding period enforced. No DAO compromise is required; pausing/unpausing `accrue` is a normal, intended operational lever, and the two transactions can be placed by an ordinary user around the resume event. The size of the payout is proportional to the pause duration and the interest rate at time of resume, so longer maintenance pauses are more lucrative.

### Recommendation
Reset `last-update` to `stacks-block-time` at the moment `accrue` is paused (or immediately upon un-pausing, before allowing any deposit/redeem), so that no interest accrues for time elapsed while the vault was intentionally frozen. Alternatively, disallow `deposit`/`redeem` operations while `accrue` is paused for a given vault, since allowing entry/exit at a deliberately stale price is precisely what enables the sandwich.

### Proof of Concept
1. `dao-executor` (or authorized admin) sets `pause-states.accrue = true` on `v0-vault-usdc` for maintenance; `last-update` is now stuck at `T0`.
2. Attacker calls `deposit(amount, min-out, attacker)` at time `T1 > T0`. `accrue()` returns the unchanged `{index, lindex}` pass-through; `convert-to-shares-preview` prices the deposit using the stale index, minting shares cheaply relative to the "true" formulaic value.
3. Admin sets `pause-states.accrue = false` at time `T2`.
4. Any transaction touching the vault (or the attacker's own next call) triggers `accrue()`; `next-index()`/`next-liquidity-index()` now compute `time-delta = T2(or later) - T0`, jumping the index up by the entire pent-up interest in one step; `treasury-lp` is minted to `.dao-treasury` and the rest of the appreciation is reflected in the redemption value for all current zToken holders, including the attacker.
5. Attacker calls `redeem(shares, min-out, attacker)` immediately, receiving underlying tokens priced at the post-jump index — realizing yield they never truly earned, at the expense of suppliers who held shares through the whole pause window.
