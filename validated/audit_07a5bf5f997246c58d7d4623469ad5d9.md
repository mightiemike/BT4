Based on the analysis, protobuf-go already implements consistent recursion-depth protection across every decode path that could encounter nested/recursive structures, which is exactly the defense that libksba's `ber-decoder.c` was missing prior to the fix in CVE-2016-4353.

- The core wire-format group/field decoder enforces a depth limit via `consumeFieldValueD`, which returns `errCodeRecursionDepth` when `depth < 0` before recursing into nested groups [1](#0-0) .
- The reflection-based slow-path unmarshaler (`proto.Unmarshal`) initializes `RecursionLimit` to `protowire.DefaultRecursionLimit` and decrements/checks it on every message and map entry [2](#0-1) , and on nested map values [3](#0-2) .
- The generated-message fast path (`internal/impl`) enforces `opts.depth` on every `unmarshalPointer` call (covering both eager and lazy decode) [4](#0-3)  and on map-of-message entries [5](#0-4) .
- The lazy-decode validation pass (`MessageInfo.validate`) also decrements `opts.depth` on every nested message, group, or map before recursing [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) .
- `encoding/prototext` and `encoding/protojson` decoders both maintain their own `RecursionLimit` counters, checked on every nested message (including inside `Any` and skip-value paths) [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) .
- Test coverage explicitly exercises recursion-limit enforcement for nested messages, maps, and discarded unknown fields [11](#0-10) .

Since every reachable decode entry point (binary wire, generated fast path, lazy path, ProtoText, ProtoJSON) already tracks and bounds recursion depth before descending into nested/group/map structures, there is no equivalent of the libksba "decoder stack overflow without depth check" flaw on a reachable, unprivileged request path with a trusted schema.
