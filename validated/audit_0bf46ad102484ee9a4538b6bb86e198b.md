### Title
Full withdrawal of disabled collateral can spuriously revert with `ERR-INSUFFICIENT-COLLATERAL` due to opposite rounding of the same balance in `collateral-remove` - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
In `collateral-remove`, when a user removes a **disabled** collateral asset while holding other debt, the contract computes two USD notional values derived from the *same* underlying token amount using opposite rounding directions, then compares them in a health/sufficiency guard. When a user withdraws their *entire* disabled-collateral balance (`amount == user-amount`), the round-down value can be strictly less than the round-up value of the identical quantity, causing the guard to fail and the whole withdrawal to revert — even though the user is not actually over-withdrawing.

### Finding Description
In the disabled-collateral branch of `collateral-remove`: [1](#0-0) 

```
(let ((oracle-data (get oracle asset))
      (price (unwrap! (price-resolve oracle-data) ERR-DISABLED-COLLATERAL-PRICE-FAILED))
      (decimals (get decimals asset))
      (user-amount (find-collateral-amount (get collateral pos-full) asset-id))
      (disabled-notional (normalize (* user-amount price) decimals false))
      (removal-notional (normalize (* amount price) decimals true))
      (total-collateral-value (+ collateral-value disabled-notional)))
  (asserts! (>= total-collateral-value removal-notional) ERR-INSUFFICIENT-COLLATERAL)
  (is-healthy (- total-collateral-value removal-notional) debt-value current-ltvb)))
```

- `disabled-notional` is the USD value of the user's **full** disabled-collateral balance, rounded **down** (`false`).
- `removal-notional` is the USD value of the amount being **removed**, rounded **up** (`true`).
- When a user withdraws exactly their entire disabled-collateral position, `amount == user-amount`, so both values are computed from the identical token quantity and price/decimals — but with opposite rounding directions. `removal-notional` (rounded up) can therefore be strictly greater than `disabled-notional` (rounded down) by up to 1 unit of USD precision.
- The guard `(>= total-collateral-value removal-notional)` checks `(collateral-value + disabled-notional) >= removal-notional`. If the position has little or no enabled collateral (`collateral-value` small/zero) and the rounding delta exceeds it, the assertion fails and `ERR-INSUFFICIENT-COLLATERAL` is thrown, reverting the entire transaction — for a user attempting a valid, complete withdrawal of an amount they actually hold.

This mirrors the reported `forceUnstakeAll` bug class: a value bound from one rounding direction (`maxStakeDebt`/`disabled-notional`, rounded down) is compared against a second value derived from the same source with an opposite rounding direction (`removal-notional`, rounded up) inside a single-transaction guard, and the mismatch can force a spurious revert of a legitimate full-exit operation.

The identical logic is duplicated in `local-testing/contracts/market/market.clar` at the same relative position: [2](#0-1) 

### Impact Explanation
This is a temporary freezing of funds: a user with debt who tries to withdraw 100% of a disabled-collateral asset can have the transaction revert due to rounding, even though they are entitled to withdraw it (assuming health is otherwise satisfied). They are forced to retry with a slightly smaller amount, or add more collateral, before they can extract their tokens — a denial-of-service on the legitimate full-exit path. This is most impactful for small-balance or thin-collateral-cushion positions, exactly the class of edge case rounding bugs typically target.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires no attacker: it is triggered by any user calling `collateral-remove` on a disabled collateral asset for their full balance in a position that has debt and where the excess `collateral-value` cushion is smaller than the 1-unit rounding delta (e.g., a single-collateral, thin-margin, or dust-level position, or an asset with few decimals where the notional rounding delta is proportionally larger). This is a realistic single-transaction scenario reachable by any normal borrower.

### Recommendation
Compute `disabled-notional` and `removal-notional` with the same rounding direction (both rounded down, or both computed from an authoritative single quantity), or special-case a full-removal (`is-eq amount user-amount`) to bypass the notional comparison and directly use the already-known `user-amount`/`disabled-notional` value rather than re-deriving `removal-notional` from `amount` with a different rounding mode.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower has an enabled collateral asset A with `collateral-value` just barely satisfying LTV, and a small amount of a disabled-collateral asset B, plus outstanding debt.
2. Borrower calls `collateral-remove` on asset B with `amount` equal to the borrower's full balance of B (`user-amount`).
3. Inside the disabled-collateral branch: `disabled-notional = normalize(user-amount * price, decimals, round-down)`, `removal-notional = normalize(amount * price, decimals, round-up)`, with `amount == user-amount`.
4. Due to rounding, `removal-notional > disabled-notional` by up to 1 unit; if `collateral-value + disabled-notional < removal-notional`, the assertion `(>= total-collateral-value removal-notional)` fails, and `ERR-INSUFFICIENT-COLLATERAL` reverts the entire withdrawal — even though the borrower is not withdrawing more than they hold.
