### Title
Single failing per-asset vault call inside `socialize-debt-asset` fold permanently blocks bad-debt liquidation for the entire borrower position - ([File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar])

### Summary
`liquidate()` in `v0-4-market.clar` writes off a borrower's remaining bad debt across all of that borrower's debt assets in a single `fold` (`socialize-debt-asset`) once collateral has been exhausted. If the per-asset vault call for **any one** of the borrower's debt assets fails, the fold's `unwrap!` short-circuits with `success: false`, the post-fold `asserts!` fires `ERR-BAD-DEBT-SOCIALIZATION-FAILED`, and the **entire** `liquidate()` transaction reverts — including the collateral seizure that had already been computed for other, healthy debt assets. This is the same bug class as the external report's FundsAllocator issue: a single malfunctioning component inside a loop/fold over multiple sub-systems causes total denial of service for the whole operation, rather than being isolated or bypassed.

### Finding Description
In `call-liquidate`/`liquidate`, once a position has no collateral left, bad debt is socialized across the borrower's full debt list via a fold: [1](#0-0) 

The fold body is: [2](#0-1) 

Each iteration:
1. Skips work if a prior iteration already failed (`success` is `false`), but still returns the failed accumulator.
2. Otherwise calls `vault-socialize-debt`, `vault-accrue`, and `.market-vault debt-remove-scaled` in sequence with `unwrap!`, each defaulting to `failed-status` on error.

`vault-socialize-debt` itself routes to one of six independent vault contracts based on asset id: [3](#0-2) 

Because the fold's final result is gated by a single `asserts!` after it completes: [4](#0-3) 

a revert or unexpected error from **any one** of up to 64 possible debt-asset vault calls (e.g., `vault-usdh` reverting due to a bug, an unexpected internal invariant violation, or any state that makes `debt-remove-scaled`/`vault-accrue`/`socialize-debt` fail for that specific asset) aborts the whole `liquidate()` call. This mirrors the external report's root cause: a loop/fold iterating over multiple independent sub-modules (adapters there, per-asset vaults here) where one failing module blocks the entire batch operation rather than being skipped or isolated.

### Impact Explanation
When a borrower's position has debt spread across multiple assets/vaults and hits zero collateral, liquidators must go through this bad-debt socialization path to close out the position. If one of the borrower's debt assets routes to a vault that reliably errors on `socialize-debt`/`accrue`/`debt-remove-scaled` for that specific state, **no liquidator can ever complete liquidation of that borrower's position**, even though the other debt assets and their vaults are perfectly healthy. The result is:
- The undercollateralized position can never be closed, leaving bad debt permanently on the books (protocol insolvency exposure for the affected debt asset).
- Collateral and other, unrelated debt for that same borrower become permanently stuck/unliquidatable because the whole transaction — not just the failing leg — reverts.

This lands in the **temporary-to-permanent freezing of funds** impact class (borrower's collateral/debt position becomes permanently unresolvable), and in the worst case (permanently broken vault call) escalates to **protocol insolvency** for that asset since the bad debt can never be socialized away.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires a genuine failure condition in the routed vault's `socialize-debt`, `accrue`, or in `.market-vault`'s `debt-remove-scaled` for a specific asset (e.g., a bug, an edge-case revert on a boundary value, or the vault being intentionally/accidentally paused for that operation). I was not able to fully verify inside this session whether any of the six vault implementations (`v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, etc.) or `v0-market-vault.clar`'s `debt-remove-scaled` contain such a revert path — this needs direct inspection of those functions to confirm a concrete trigger. The structural DOS risk (fold + single `asserts!` blocking the whole liquidation), however, is confirmed directly from the code cited above.

### Recommendation
Do not let a single per-asset failure inside `socialize-debt-asset` abort the entire bad-debt socialization / liquidation. Either:
- Process each debt asset independently and only skip (not fail the whole batch for) the asset whose vault call errors, recording it for later retry/governance intervention, or
- Provide a governance/emergency path to force-write-off or isolate a specific asset's bad debt without requiring all debt-asset vault calls in the position to succeed atomically.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower opens a position with debt in two assets, e.g. STX and USDH, backed by insufficient collateral.
2. Collateral price drops (or repeated partial liquidations occur) until `no-collateral-left` becomes true in `liquidate()` [5](#0-4) .
3. `liquidate()` builds `fresh-debt-list` containing both STX and USDH debt entries and calls `fold socialize-debt-asset fresh-debt-list {...}` [4](#0-3) .
4. Suppose the USDH vault's `socialize-debt` call errors for this specific scaled amount (due to a bug or edge case in `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, not independently verified in this session). The fold's `unwrap!` short-circuits that iteration to `{ borrower, success: false }`.
5. Even though the STX-debt iteration (processed earlier or later in the list) may have succeeded independently, the final `asserts! (get success socialization-result) ERR-BAD-DEBT-SOCIALIZATION-FAILED` fails because `success` is `false`, reverting the entire `liquidate()` transaction — including the already-computed collateral seizure.
6. No liquidator can ever successfully call `liquidate()` on this borrower while the USDH vault call keeps failing, permanently freezing the position (collateral + all debt legs, not just USDH).
