### Title
Meta-Transaction Relayers Can Be Gas-Griefed and Drained by a Malicious Sender's Receiver Contract - ([File: docs/architecture/how/meta-tx.md])

### Summary
NEAR's meta-transaction mechanism (NEP-366, `Action::Delegate`) lets an unprivileged "sender" author a `DelegateAction` that a relayer wraps, signs, and pays for. The relayer bears **all** gas and token costs for the sender-chosen `receiver_id` and its called method, while the sender retains full control over what that receiver contract does with the attached gas. This is structurally identical to the reported delegation issue: an untrusted party (the delegator/sender) chooses the address that receives control of the caller's gas ("enforcer"/receiver contract), and can use it to grief or extract value from the gas payer (relayer), with no protocol-level limit beyond the relayer's own chosen `prepaid_gas`.

### Finding Description
In a meta transaction, `apply_delegate_action` builds a new receipt whose `predecessor_id` is the `sender_id` and whose actions are exactly what the sender signed; all gas/deposit costs are charged to the relayer, not the sender [1](#0-0) . The relayer purchases the gas for all inner actions plus the delegate wrapper [2](#0-1) , and the sender fully controls the receiver (which can be the sender's own account/contract) and the method invoked.

Because the receiver of the inner action can be an account controlled by the sender, the sender can:
1. Deploy an arbitrary, gas-intensive WASM contract as the `receiver_id` and have the relayer's `DelegateAction` call it, burning the relayer-purchased `prepaid_gas` on computation that benefits the sender (analogous to "enforcer" contracts in the original report doing gas-intensive work paid for by someone else).
2. Cause the burnt gas to accrue as a **contract reward** (`burnt_gas_reward`, 30% of gas burnt on the function-call execution) to the sender's own account, effectively "stealing" a fraction of the gas value the relayer paid for [3](#0-2) .
3. Simply exhaust all attached gas to force `GasExceeded`/failure, griefing the relayer's transaction (gas griefing), since the sender chooses the receiver and its behavior is not restricted by the relayer beyond the receiver id/method embedded in the `DelegateAction` itself.

This exact risk class is already called out by the nearcore documentation itself for the general relayer trust model: "The relayer could waste NEAR gas without compensation... Some level of trust between the relayer and its user is therefore required," and explicitly warns of "faucet-draining attacks where an attacker extracts funds from the relayer by making calls to a contract they own" [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) .

### Impact Explanation
A relayer that is not extremely careful about which senders/receivers it will relay for can have gas it purchased burned entirely for the sender's benefit (via contract reward) or wasted entirely (griefing), causing direct, unauthorized value extraction from the relayer with each accepted `DelegateAction`. This is a repeatable drain: nothing at the protocol level caps how much of the relayer-purchased gas ends up rewarded to a sender-controlled contract, short of `max_total_prepaid_gas` (300 Tgas) per transaction.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any unprivileged account holder can act as the "sender," sign a `DelegateAction` naming a contract they control as `receiver_id`, and submit it to any relayer willing to relay it. No special privilege, validator, or node compromise is required — only relayer participation, which is the intended usage pattern for meta transactions. This mirrors the original report precisely: it is a known, acknowledged design property rather than a memory-safety bug, requiring off-chain trust/allowlisting by relayers rather than a protocol fix.

### Recommendation
As nearcore's own documentation already states, this is treated as an inherent trust/design tradeoff of the meta-transaction (NEP-366) model rather than a bug to be "fixed" at the protocol layer. Consistent with the original report's "Acknowledged" resolution, the practical mitigations are the same category the report suggests: relayers should restrict which `receiver_id`/`method_names` combinations they are willing to relay for (an allowlist), cap attached gas conservatively, and monitor gas usage/contract-reward outcomes per sender rather than expecting protocol-level enforcement.

### Proof of Concept
1. Sender deploys a contract `evil.near` (self-owned) whose method burns as much gas as possible doing "useful" (to the sender) computation, or simply loops until `GasExceeded`.
2. Sender signs a `SignedDelegateAction` with `receiver_id = "evil.near"`, `actions = [FunctionCall { method_name: "burn", gas: <large>, ... }]` following the flow in `apply_delegate_action` [6](#0-5) .
3. Relayer wraps and submits it, paying gas per the cost-accounting shown in the meta-tx test helper `check_meta_tx_fn_call` / `meta_tx` [7](#0-6) .
4. The relayer's balance decreases by the full gas cost; a fraction of the burnt gas (per `burnt_gas_reward`) is credited to `evil.near`, controlled by the sender — demonstrating value extraction from the relayer without the relayer's consent to that specific computation.

pub(crate) fn apply_delegate_action(
    state_update: &mut TrieUpdate,
    apply_state: &ApplyState,
    action_receipt: &VersionedActionReceipt,
    sender_id: &AccountId,
    signed_delegate_action: VersionedSignedDelegateActionRef<'_>,
    result: &mut ActionResult,
) -> Result<(), RuntimeError> {
    // The inner delegate signature is verified below, here on the receiver shard.
    // Meter its verification compute against this shard's `compute_limit`; the gas
    // for it was already burnt at tx conversion on the signer shard. Without the
    // fix the compute is instead mis-charged on the signer shard (which never runs
    // this verify), letting the work escape the receiver shard's budget. See
    // `signature_verification_cost`.
    if apply_state.config.wasm_config.fix_ml_dsa_cost_charging {
        let verify_compute = delegate_signature_verification_compute(
            &apply_state.config.fees,
            signed_delegate_action.delegate_action().public_key(),
        );
        result.compute_usage = safe_add_compute(result.compute_usage, verify_compute)?;
    }
    if !signed_delegate_action.verify() {
        result.result = Err(ActionErrorKind::DelegateActionInvalidSignature.into());
        return Ok(());
    }
    let delegate_action = signed_delegate_action.delegate_action();

```markdown
### Contract Reward

A rather unique property of Near Protocol is that a part of the gas fee goes to
the contract owner. This "smart contract gets paid" model is pretty much the
opposite design choice from the "smart contract pays" model that for example
[Cycles in the Internet
Computer](https://internetcomputer.org/docs/current/developer-docs/gas-cost#details-cost-of-compute-and-storage-transactions-on-the-internet-computer)
implement.

The idea is that it gives contract developers a source of income and hence an
incentive to create useful contracts that are commonly used. But there are also
downsides, such as when implementing a free meta-transaction relayer one has to
be careful not to be susceptible to faucet-draining attacks where an attacker
extracts funds from the relayer by making calls to a contract they own.

How much contracts receive from execution depends on two things.

1. How much gas is burnt on the function call execution itself. That is, only
   the gas taken from the `attached_gas` of a function call is considered for
   contract rewards. The base fees paid for creating the receipt, including the
   `action_function_call` fee, are burnt 100%.
2. The remainder of the burnt gas is multiplied by the runtime configuration
   parameter
   [`burnt_gas_reward`](../../../core/parameters/res/runtime_configs/parameters.snap#L5C5-L5C5)
   which currently is at 30%.
```

    /// Wrap the given actions in a delegate action and execute them.
    ///
    /// The signer signs the delegate action to be sent to the receiver. The
    /// relayer packs that in a transaction and signs it .
    fn meta_tx(
        &self,
        signer_id: AccountId,
        receiver_id: AccountId,
        relayer_id: AccountId,
        actions: Vec<Action>,
    ) -> Result<FinalExecutionOutcomeView, CommitError> {
        let inner_signer = create_user_test_signer(&signer_id);
        let user_nonce = self
            .get_access_key(&signer_id, &inner_signer.public_key())
            .expect("failed reading user's nonce for access key")
            .nonce;
        let delegate_action = DelegateAction {
            sender_id: signer_id.clone(),
            receiver_id,
            actions: actions
                .into_iter()
                .map(|action| NonDelegateAction::try_from(action).unwrap())
                .collect(),
            nonce: user_nonce + 1,
            max_block_height: 100,
            public_key: inner_signer.public_key(),
        };
        let signature = inner_signer.sign(delegate_action.get_nep461_hash().as_bytes());
        let signed_delegate_action = SignedDelegateAction { delegate_action, signature };

        self.sign_and_commit_actions(
            relayer_id,
            signer_id,
            vec![Action::Delegate(Box::new(signed_delegate_action))],
        )
    }
