## Title
Order-Matching Rounding-Direction Flaw Systematically Underpays the Fully-Filled Counterparty in the TRC10 Market — (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/MarketSellAssetActuator.java`)

### Summary
The TRC10 order-book market's trade-matching logic in `matchSingleOrder` always rounds the *payment amount* owed to the counterparty **down** (floor division via `MarketUtils.multiplyAndDivide`), while crediting the fully-consumed side its **full** remaining quantity with no corresponding rounding-up of what it receives in return. This is the same class of bug as the ERC-4626 report: a conversion between two economically-linked quantities (here, sell/buy token amounts instead of shares/assets) is rounded in the direction that favors the caller instead of the party whose balance is being fully liquidated, allowing systemic underpayment/value leakage on every partially-divisible trade.

### Finding Description
`MarketUtils.multiplyAndDivide` performs `floorDiv(a*b, c)` with no rounding-up variant: [1](#0-0) 

In `MarketSellAssetActuator.matchSingleOrder`, when the taker's demand exceeds the maker's remaining supply ("taker > maker" branch), the taker is credited the **entire** remaining sell quantity of the maker order, while the amount the taker must pay is computed with this floor-dividing helper and the maker order is marked fully consumed (`sellTokenQuantityRemain` set to 0): [2](#0-1) 

The same floor-rounded helper is used for the exactly-matching ("taker == maker") case where both sides are fully consumed: [3](#0-2) 

In both cases, the side whose order is being *fully* liquidated (`sellTokenQuantityRemain` forced to `0`) is paid `floor(remainingSellQty * buyQty / sellQty)` instead of the mathematically-correct `ceil(...)` amount required to make them whole for giving up their entire remaining balance. Because the division is not exact whenever `remainingSellQty * buyQty` is not a multiple of `sellQty`, the fully-consumed party is paid strictly less than the value of what it gave away, and the counterparty captures the rounding remainder for free. This mirrors the reported bug exactly: `convertToShares`/`convertToAssets`-style helpers must round in favor of the party being made whole, not in favor of the calling side — `deposit`-style debits round down, but any operation that fully drains a balance (analogous to `withdraw`) must round up.

### Impact Explanation
Any unprivileged user can create TRC10 sell orders via `MarketSellAssetActuator`/`MarketSellAssetContract` (no privileged role required). By carefully choosing `sellTokenQuantity`/`buyTokenQuantity` ratios that don't divide evenly, an attacker acting as taker can repeatedly consume maker orders (including their own orders placed from a second address, or unsuspecting victims' resting orders) and systematically underpay by the floor-rounding remainder on every fill. While the loss per trade is bounded by the token's price granularity, it is a real, repeatable, protocol-level accounting shortfall extracted from counterparties' token balances with no compensating credit anywhere in the system — i.e., value is destroyed/misallocated on-chain, which is a concrete underpriced-settlement/accounting defect rather than a theoretical rounding curiosity.

### Likelihood Explanation
The order-matching path is reachable by any account with TRC10 tokens or TRX, requires no special permissions, and the rounding condition (non-exact division) is common for arbitrary user-chosen price ratios and quantities, so the underpayment triggers routinely rather than in rare edge cases. An attacker can also amplify the effect deliberately by controlling both the maker and taker sides across two of their own accounts to farm the rounding differential from any resting orders that get matched against them, or by crafting quantities designed to maximize the truncated remainder against a victim's maker order.

### Recommendation
Round in favor of the side whose order is being fully consumed: when `sellTokenQuantityRemain` is being set to `0` (full fill), compute the payment amount with ceiling division (`ceilDiv`) instead of `floorDiv`/`multiplyAndDivide`, mirroring the ERC-4626 guidance of rounding up whenever a party's balance is being drawn down to zero, and only rounding down when computing amounts still left outstanding (partial fill remainder). Add explicit tests asserting that fully-filled counterparties always receive at least the exact proportional value, with no systematic underpayment across many small trades.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker controls Account A (maker) and Account B (taker), or simply targets any victim maker order.
2. Maker (A) places a sell order via `MarketSellAssetActuator` with `sellTokenQuantity = 3`, `buyTokenQuantity = 2` (i.e., wants 2 buyToken for 3 sellToken) — `MarketSellAssetActuator.java:99-163` (order creation path).
3. Taker (B) places a matching order that ends up in the "taker > maker" branch of `matchSingleOrder`, so `makerOrderCapsule.getSellTokenQuantityRemain()` (say `= 5`) is fully consumed:
   - `makerBuyTokenQuantityReceive = multiplyAndDivide(5, 2, 3) = floor(10/3) = 3` (see `MarketUtils.java:264-277` and `MarketSellAssetActuator.java:461-463`).
   - Correct/fair amount owed should be `ceil(10/3) = 4`.
4. Maker's order is marked fully filled (`sellTokenQuantityRemain = 0`) yet only receives `3` buyToken instead of the fair `4` — `MarketSellAssetActuator.java:479-482`.
5. Taker (B) effectively pays `1` unit less than the value of tokens it received, repeatable across many trades to accumulate value taken from counterparties without an equivalent debit anywhere in the ledger.
