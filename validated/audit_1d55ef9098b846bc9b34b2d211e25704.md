## Analysis

The Rubicon report describes a privileged actor (the strategist) using access to pooled user funds to execute two atomic, self-referential trade actions in one transaction to redirect value to themselves. The closest reachable analog in nearcore that an unprivileged transaction signer can trigger is in the **NEP-366 meta-transaction / `DelegateAction`** flow, where the relayer funds a `sender`-controlled action that can be crafted to fail on purpose so that the attached deposit refund goes back to the sender instead of the relayer who paid for it.

### Title
Meta-transaction sender can redirect relayer-funded deposits to themselves by forcing inner-action failure - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`)

### Summary
A `DelegateAction` sender (an ordinary, unprivileged account with no NEAR balance of its own) can craft delegated actions with an attached deposit that predictably fail on the receiver's shard. Because deposit refunds are always routed to the receipt's `predecessor_id` (which becomes the sender/"Alice", not the relayer who paid the deposit), and because `apply_delegate_action` always constructs a legacy `Receipt::V0` that has no `refund_to` override capability, the relayer's funds are unconditionally refunded to the sender rather than to the relayer.

### Finding Description
When a relayer submits a meta-transaction, `apply_delegate_action` unwraps the `SignedDelegateAction` and spawns a brand-new action receipt for the inner actions using `predecessor_id: sender_id.clone()`: [1](#0-0) 

This receipt is built as a plain `Receipt::V0`, which has no `refund_to` field — that field only exists on the newer `ActionReceiptV2` variant: [2](#0-1) 

Deposit and gas refunds are computed in `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits`, and the deposit refund is explicitly sent to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()`, which defaults to `predecessor_id` unless `refund_to` is set: [3](#0-2) 

Since the delegate-action receipt is a `Receipt::V0` with no `refund_to`, `balance_refund_receiver()` always resolves to `sender_id` (the delegate-action sender), never the relayer, even though the relayer is the one who actually paid for the attached deposit at transaction-conversion time. This is the exact scenario the protocol's own documentation flags: [4](#0-3) 

The `refund_to` mechanism does exist elsewhere in the codebase and is specifically designed to let a fund-attaching predecessor redirect its own refund (e.g. used by `promise_batch_action_state_init`/`promise_set_refund_to` flows and tested in `test_refund_to`): [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

However, `apply_delegate_action` never uses this mechanism to protect the relayer, meaning relayers have no protocol-level way to guarantee they get their deposit back if the sender's inner action is designed to fail.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged account holder with zero NEAR balance ("Alice") can act as the `sender_id` of a `DelegateAction` containing an action with an attached deposit (e.g. a `Transfer` or `FunctionCall` deposit) targeting a receiver/method that will deterministically fail (nonexistent method, insufficient gas for execution, a contract that always panics, etc.). The relayer purchases and prepays that deposit believing it will reach the receiver; instead, on failure, the entire deposit is refunded to Alice, the very party who orchestrated the failure — a direct transfer of relayer-owned value to an unprivileged party who contributed no funds of her own. This constitutes concrete unauthorized value movement from the relayer to the sender.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires only a single signed `DelegateAction` from any account and depends on a relayer's willingness to sponsor deposits for meta-transactions, which is the entire premise of NEP-366. The protocol currently gives relayers no built-in mechanism (no `refund_to` support on delegate-spawned receipts) to defend against this, so the risk is present for any relayer sponsoring deposit-carrying meta-transactions unless the relayer implements its own off-chain safeguards (e.g., pre-simulating execution, restricting to zero-deposit actions, whitelisting receivers/methods). This matches the acknowledged, but unmitigated-in-protocol, risk already called out in nearcore's own documentation.

### Recommendation
Extend `apply_delegate_action` to construct the inner receipt as an `ActionReceiptV2`-capable receipt with `refund_to` set to the relayer (the outer transaction's signer/predecessor) whenever the relayer, not the sender, is the party funding attached deposits — mirroring the existing `refund_to`/`promise_set_refund_to` mechanism already used elsewhere for exactly this purpose. At minimum, document/require relayer-side simulation before sponsoring deposit-bearing delegated actions, and consider protocol-level opt-in for relayer-safe refund routing.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer `R` receives a `SignedDelegateAction` from sender `Alice` (who holds no NEAR) with `actions = [Transfer { deposit: X }]` (or a `FunctionCall` with attached deposit) targeting `receiver_id = Bob`, where `Bob` is configured/chosen so the action will fail (e.g., insufficient gas, nonexistent method, or account state that trips `LackBalanceForState`).
2. `R` wraps it in a transaction and submits it, paying gas and the deposit `X` upfront.
3. On Alice's shard, `apply_delegate_action` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:453`) builds a `Receipt::V0` with `predecessor_id = Alice` and forwards the inner action to Bob's shard.
4. Bob's shard executes the inner action, which fails as designed.
5. `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` (`runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:1284`) issues a deposit refund via `Receipt::new_balance_refund(receipt.balance_refund_receiver(), deposit_refund)`, and `balance_refund_receiver()` resolves to `Alice` (no `refund_to` set on the `Receipt::V0`), so the deposit `X` — paid for by `R` — lands in Alice's account instead of being returned to `R`.

```markdown
## Balance refunds in meta transactions

Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor
(a.k.a. sender) of the receipt. This makes sense, as we deposit the attached
balance to the receiver, who has to explicitly reattach a new balance to new
receipts they might spawn.

In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an
inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then
this balance is taken from the relayer.

The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction
and agrees to pay for it, so nothing wrong so far. But what if the transaction
fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and
therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is
something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial
incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached
but will fail on Bob's shard.
```
