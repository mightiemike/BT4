The code clearly checks buffer authority matching and signer status before the Upgrade proceeds.

### Analysis

The `Upgrade` instruction handler in `programs/bpf_loader/src/lib.rs` explicitly validates that the supplied buffer's `authority_address` matches the `authority_key` (the same account used as the ProgramData upgrade authority), and additionally requires that authority to be a signer: [1](#0-0) 

Specifically:
- `authority_key` is derived from instruction account index 6, the account that must also match/sign as the ProgramData's `upgrade_authority_address` [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 
- Before any lamports are merged/drained, the buffer's `authority_address` must equal that same `authority_key`, or the instruction fails with `InstructionError::IncorrectAuthority` [4](#0-3) 
- The authority must also be a signer on the transaction, or it fails with `InstructionError::MissingRequiredSignature` [5](#0-4) 

This means an attacker cannot supply a victim's buffer (whose `authority_address` is the victim) unless the victim also signs the transaction as instruction account 6 — which contradicts the attacker-only, unprivileged threat model. If the attacker tries to substitute their own pubkey as `authority_key` while the buffer's stored authority is the victim's, the check at line 415 fails with `IncorrectAuthority` before any lamport merging occurs (lamport merge/drain happens later at lines 535-544, well after this check).

This exact scenario is also covered by existing unit tests asserting `IncorrectAuthority` is returned for mismatched buffer/program authority: [6](#0-5)  and for the `DeployWithMaxDataLen` variant: [7](#0-6) 

The `DeployWithMaxDataLen` instruction (which also consumes a buffer) similarly enforces this check: [8](#0-7)
