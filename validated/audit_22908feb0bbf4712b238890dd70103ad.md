### Title
Protocol reserve fee (treasury-lp) permanently lost to rounding when `accrue` is called frequently on low-decimal-asset vaults (USDC/USDH) - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar`)

### Summary
The reserve-fee (protocol/DAO-treasury cut of interest) computed inside `accrue` rounds down to zero for small interest deltas, yet the accrual checkpoint (`last-update`) is advanced whenever the borrow or liquidity index changes at all — even when the corresponding fee rounds to zero. Anyone can call the public `accrue` function (or trigger it implicitly via `deposit`/`redeem`/`transfer`) very frequently and cheaply, permanently destroying the DAO treasury's fee share on 6-decimal-asset vaults such as `v0-vault-usdc.clar` and `v0-vault-usdh.clar`.

### Finding Description
`accrue` computes the interest-fee ("reserve") to be minted to `dao-treasury` from the delta between old and new debt: [1](#0-0) 

```
(old-debt (mul-div-down scaled-principal idx INDEX-PRECISION))
(new-debt (mul-div-down scaled-principal next INDEX-PRECISION))
(debt-delta (if (> new-debt old-debt) (- new-debt old-debt) u0))
(reserve-inc (mul-div-down debt-delta (var-get fee-reserve) BPS))
...
(if (or (not (is-eq idx next)) (not (is-eq lidx nliq)))
    (var-set last-update stacks-block-time)
    false)
```

`old-debt`/`new-debt`/`debt-delta` are expressed in raw underlying-asset units (6 decimals for USDC/USDH, since `mul-div-down` divides back by `INDEX-PRECISION`), matching the exact class of the referenced report where `interestAccrued.mulDiv(pool.interestFee, 1e18)` truncates for low-decimal assets. `reserve-inc = mul-div-down(debt-delta, fee-reserve, BPS)` truncates to `0` whenever `debt-delta * fee-reserve < BPS (10000)`. This routinely happens for short time intervals or modest positions because `debt-delta` itself is derived from a per-second interest rate multiplier (`calc-multiplier-delta`) that grows the index by a tiny amount per second.

Crucially, `last-update` — the checkpoint from which future interest is computed — is advanced as soon as `idx` (or `lindex`) changes at all, **independent of whether `reserve-inc` rounded to zero**. This is the "clock advanced only on change" pattern: the index does move (so borrowers/depositors get their principal-level interest, since `debt-delta` feeds directly into debt), but the protocol's fee slice on that same delta is silently dropped because of the separate floor-division, and the checkpoint used to compute the *next* fee slice starts fresh from the new index — the lost fee delta can never be recovered on a subsequent call.

`accrue` is a `define-public` function with no caller-authorization check (unlike `system-borrow`, which requires `check-caller-auth`), and it is also invoked as the first step of `deposit`, `redeem`, and `transfer`: [2](#0-1) 

This means any external account can call `accrue` directly, or interleave trivial deposits/redeems, at very short intervals (e.g. once per block) essentially for free.

### Impact Explanation
Every time `accrue` is called with a `debt-delta` small enough that `debt-delta * fee-reserve < BPS`, the DAO treasury's protocol-fee mint (`treasury-lp`) is silently zeroed while the checkpoint (`last-update`) is still advanced. Because the checkpoint reset is not conditioned on whether the fee was actually captured, this loss is unrecoverable and can be repeated indefinitely by anyone. Over time this results in permanent loss of the DAO treasury's fee revenue ("unclaimed yield" belonging to the protocol) on the USDC/USDH vaults, which use only 6 decimals and are therefore most exposed to this truncation.

### Likelihood Explanation
`accrue` is unauthenticated and callable directly by anyone, and is also triggered implicitly by cheap operations like `deposit`/`redeem`/`transfer`. No minimum-borrow/minimum-interval guard prevents rapid successive calls. For any vault with modest total borrowed principal and a realistic reserve-factor (e.g. 5–10%), calling `accrue` once per block (or once every few seconds) keeps `debt-delta` small enough that `reserve-inc` floors to zero on most calls, while the index checkpoint still advances — making the attack both cheap and repeatable without limit.

### Recommendation
Do not silently drop the un-minted fee remainder when the checkpoint advances. Options:
- Track a running remainder of `debt-delta * fee-reserve` (or of `debt-delta` itself) that carries over between `accrue` calls instead of relying on `last-update` alone, so unminted fee dust accumulates instead of resetting to zero.
- Alternatively, store internal debt/index accounting at a higher fixed precision (e.g. scale raw asset amounts up to 18 decimals for the fee computation) so `debt-delta * fee-reserve` does not truncate to zero for realistic time intervals on 6-decimal assets.
- At minimum, only advance `last-update` when the resulting `reserve-inc`/`treasury-lp` was successfully realized, or account for the residual precision loss explicitly.

### Proof of Concept
Using `v0-vault-usdc.clar` constants (`INDEX-PRECISION = 1e12`, `BPS = 10000`, `SECONDS-PER-YEAR-BPS = 31536000*10000`):

1. Vault has `scaled-principal = 2_000_000_000` (2000 USDC borrowed, 6 decimals), `fee-reserve = 1000` (10%), and a borrow rate of `500` bps APY.
2. A caller (needs no authorization) calls `accrue` once per second.
3. Per call: `time-delta = 1`; multiplier delta portion `= (500 * 1 * 1e12) / 3.1536e11 ≈ 1585`; `debt-delta ≈ scaled-principal * 1585 / 1e12 ≈ 3` (raw USDC units, i.e. $0.000003).
4. `reserve-inc = mul-div-down(3, 1000, 10000) = 0` (floors to zero) — no `treasury-lp` shares minted to `dao-treasury`.
5. Because `idx` still changed (`next != idx`), `last-update` is advanced to the current block time regardless.
6. Repeating this every second (or every block) means the DAO treasury's fee share is zeroed on essentially every `accrue` call while borrowers'/depositors' index still compounds — the protocol permanently loses its reserve-fee revenue on this vault, and the attack costs only the gas of a public-function call.
