## Title
Energy overcharge in TVM `batchvalidatesign`/`validatemultisign` precompiles: fee charged for full requested signature count while verification work is capped/aborted at `MAX_SIZE` - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/vm/PrecompiledContracts.java)

### Summary
The `BatchValidateSign` and `ValidateMultiSign` TVM precompiled contracts compute their energy charge from the raw calldata size (i.e., the number of signatures the caller *requests* to be verified), but their `execute()` methods abort immediately and perform **zero verification work** whenever the decoded signature count exceeds a hard-coded `MAX_SIZE`. Because the energy debit happens in `Program.callToPrecompiledAddress` *before* `execute()` runs and is never adjusted to the amount of actual work performed, a caller supplying more signatures than `MAX_SIZE` is charged full price for a call that does nothing but return `false`.

### Finding Description
`Program.callToPrecompiledAddress` computes and spends the energy fee for a precompile call strictly from `contract.getEnergyForData(data)`, prior to invoking `contract.execute(data)`: [1](#0-0) 

For `BatchValidateSign`, the energy formula is derived purely from the byte length of calldata (i.e., how many signatures/addresses the caller declares), independent of any cap: [2](#0-1) 

But `doExecute()` enforces `MAX_SIZE = 16` and, if the decoded signature/address count exceeds it, returns immediately with `DATA_FALSE`/no result — no ECDSA recovery, no comparison, no useful work at all: [3](#0-2) 

The identical pattern exists in `ValidateMultiSign`, where `MAX_SIZE = 5`: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

This is structurally identical to the reported `beam-contract`/`oracle` bug: a fee-charging layer (`getEnergyForData`, evaluated before `execute()`) bills for the number of records/signatures requested by the caller, while the actual data-processing layer (`execute()`/`doExecute()`) silently caps the real work performed to a fixed maximum (`MAX_SIZE`). Unlike the original report where excess requests still return a partial (20-record) result, here the mismatch is even more severe: once the requested count exceeds the cap, **no verification whatsoever is performed**, yet the caller is charged the full `cnt * ENGERYPERSIGN` energy for the entire (unprocessed) request.

### Impact Explanation
Energy debited via this path is real TRX cost to the calling account (paid by the contract caller/creator per the normal TVM fee-bill flow), and once spent it is not refunded — the call simply pushes `0`/`false` onto the stack and the transaction proceeds. Any externally-callable smart contract that forwards user-supplied signature arrays into `batchvalidatesign` or `validatemultisign` (e.g., multisig wallets, DApps built on these precompiles) can cause its unprivileged callers to be systematically overcharged energy proportional to `cnt` (up to attacker-influenced sizes) while getting zero signature-verification work in return whenever `cnt > MAX_SIZE`. This is a genuine underpriced/overpriced-public-work accounting bug at the TVM layer, not merely a caller-side logic error, since the energy formula and the cap live in the same precompile class and are inherently out of sync.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any contract or user can trigger this by calling the `batchvalidatesign(bytes32,bytes[],address[])` or `validatemultisign(address,uint256,bytes32,bytes[])` precompiles with a signatures/addresses array larger than `MAX_SIZE` (16 or 5 respectively). No privileged role or special configuration is required — only that the calldata be crafted so the decoded array length exceeds the cap. The vulnerability is triggered by simple public precompile calls, making it directly and easily reachable by any unprivileged user or contract that uses these standard TVM precompiles.

### Recommendation
Reject calls whose declared signature/address count exceeds `MAX_SIZE` *before* computing `getEnergyForData`, or cap the energy formula itself to `min(cnt, MAX_SIZE) * ENGERYPERSIGN` so the charge tracks the amount of work `execute()` will actually attempt. Alternatively, have `getEnergyForData` and `execute()`/`doExecute()` share the same bounds-check helper so the two can never diverge.

### Proof of Concept
1. Craft ABI-encoded calldata for `batchvalidatesign(bytes32,bytes[],address[])` with 17 (or more) signature/address pairs (any valid or dummy bytes, since verification never runs).
2. Call the precompile at its fixed address via a `CALL`/`STATICCALL` from a test contract, providing sufficient `energyLimit`.
3. Observe: `Program.callToPrecompiledAddress` computes `requiredEnergy = contract.getEnergyForData(data)` = `17 * 1500 = 25500` [6](#0-5)  and that full amount is spent from the caller's energy/TRX.
4. `doExecute()` detects `cnt (17) > MAX_SIZE (16)` and returns `Pair.of(true, DATA_FALSE)` immediately [7](#0-6)  — no signature recovery/comparison is executed for any of the 17 entries, yet the full 25500 energy (and corresponding TRX/bandwidth cost) has already been irrecoverably charged.
