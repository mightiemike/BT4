### Title
Deposit Refund Misdirected to Sender Instead of Relayer on Failed Inner Action in Meta-Transactions — (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`)

---

### Summary

In NEAR's meta-transaction (NEP-366) implementation, `apply_delegate_action` constructs the inner action receipt with `predecessor_id` set to the **sender** (`sender_id`, i.e. Alice), not the **relayer** who actually paid the attached deposit. When the inner action fails on the receiver's shard, the protocol's deposit-refund mechanism sends the full deposit back to `predecessor_id` — Alice — rather than to the relayer who funded it. An unprivileged user can exploit this to drain a relayer's NEAR balance by deliberately crafting meta-transactions with large deposits that are guaranteed to fail.

---

### Finding Description

**Root cause — `apply_delegate_action` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:455-469`)**

When a `DelegateAction` is processed on Alice's shard, a new inner receipt is emitted:

```rust
let new_receipt = Receipt::V0(ReceiptV0 {
    predecessor_id: sender_id.clone(),   // ← Alice, NOT the relayer
    receiver_id: delegate_action.receiver_id().clone(),
    ...
    receipt: ReceiptEnum::Action(ActionReceipt { ... }),
});
``` [1](#0-0) 

The relayer's identity is only preserved in `signer_id` (used for gas refunds), not in `predecessor_id` (used for deposit refunds).

**Refund dispatch — `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` (`runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:1281-1285`)**

When the inner receipt fails, the runtime computes `deposit_refund = total_deposit` and calls:

```rust
result.new_receipts.push(Receipt::new_balance_refund(
    receipt.balance_refund_receiver(),
    deposit_refund,
));
``` [2](#0-1) 

**`balance_refund_receiver` (`core/primitives/src/receipt.rs:428-430`)**

```rust
pub fn balance_refund_receiver(&self) -> &AccountId {
    self.refund_to().as_ref().unwrap_or_else(|| self.predecessor_id())
}
``` [3](#0-2) 

Because the inner receipt is `Receipt::V0` / `ReceiptEnum::Action` (not `ActionV2`), `refund_to()` always returns `&None`: [4](#0-3) 

The fallback is therefore `predecessor_id` = Alice. The relayer, who paid the deposit per the NEP-366 spec, receives nothing.

**The asymmetry is acknowledged in the codebase itself:**

```
// Note, Relayer prepaid all fees and all things required by actions: attached deposits and attached gas.
// If something goes wrong, deposit is refunded to the predecessor, this is sender_id/Sender in DelegateAction.
// Gas is refunded to the signer, this is Relayer.
// Therefore Relayer should verify DelegateAction before submitting it because it spends the attached deposit.
``` [5](#0-4) 

And in the architecture docs:

> "there is a financial incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached but will fail on Bob's shard." [6](#0-5) 

The protocol provides no guard against this. The comment and doc treat it as a relayer-side responsibility, but the broken invariant lives entirely in nearcore's runtime.

---

### Impact Explanation

The relayer pays the deposit for all inner actions (this is enforced by the protocol — the relayer's balance is debited at transaction processing time). When the inner receipt fails, the deposit is refunded to Alice (`predecessor_id`), not the relayer. Alice gains the full deposit amount; the relayer loses it. This is a direct, protocol-level transfer of funds from the relayer to Alice with no authorization from the relayer.

Scope match: **stealing or loss of funds** — the relayer's NEAR balance is permanently reduced by the deposit amount, and Alice's balance is increased by the same amount, with no legitimate basis.

---

### Likelihood Explanation

- Alice needs no special privilege — she only needs to sign a `DelegateAction` and find any relayer willing to submit it.
- The attack is repeatable: Alice can submit many such meta-transactions in sequence.
- The failing condition is easy to guarantee: call a non-existent method on any contract, or target a receiver that has no contract deployed.
- The relayer cannot distinguish a legitimate meta-transaction from a malicious one purely by inspecting the `DelegateAction` content, because the failure may depend on contract state that changes between submission and execution.
- The documentation explicitly acknowledges the financial incentive for Alice to do this, confirming the attack is known and reachable.

---

### Recommendation

Replace the `Receipt::V0` / `ReceiptEnum::Action` inner receipt in `apply_delegate_action` with a `Receipt::V0` / `ReceiptEnum::ActionV2` receipt that sets `refund_to: Some(relayer_account_id)`, where `relayer_account_id` is the `predecessor_id` of the outer action receipt (i.e., the relayer's account). The `ActionReceiptV2.refund_to` field exists precisely for this purpose: [7](#0-6) 

With `refund_to` set to the relayer, `balance_refund_receiver()` will return the relayer on failure, correctly routing the deposit refund to the party who paid it. Successful execution is unaffected: contracts that explicitly refund the predecessor (Alice) do so via their own `Transfer` actions, not via the protocol's deposit-refund path.

---

### Proof of Concept

1. Alice creates a `DelegateAction` targeting `bob.near` with an inner `FunctionCall` action carrying a large deposit (e.g., 10 NEAR) to a method that does not exist on `bob.near`.
2. Alice signs the `DelegateAction` and sends it off-chain to a relayer.
3. The relayer wraps it in a transaction, paying the 10 NEAR deposit from its own balance.
4. On Alice's shard, `apply_delegate_action` emits an inner receipt with `predecessor_id = alice.near` and the 10 NEAR deposit.
5. On Bob's shard, the function call fails (`MethodNotFound` or similar).
6. `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` fires: `deposit_refund = 10 NEAR`, `balance_refund_receiver() = alice.near`.
7. `Receipt::new_balance_refund(&alice.near, 10 NEAR)` is pushed.
8. Alice's balance increases by 10 NEAR; the relayer's balance is permanently reduced by 10 NEAR.
