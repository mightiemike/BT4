### Title
Contract-reward faucet-drain: a permissionless per-call gas reward lets any account drain a meta-transaction relayer that pays for calls into an attacker-owned contract - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
NEAR's `burnt_gas_reward` mechanism credits 30% of the gas burnt for any `FunctionCall` back to the *receiver* account, unconditionally and permissionlessly, on every receipt. This is functionally the same "permissionless round-trip incentive" primitive that the Splits report exploited: any party that pays gas on behalf of a contract call (a meta-transaction relayer sponsoring users, in the NEAR case) can be looped against by an attacker who owns the receiving contract, extracting a fixed percentage of every gas-paying call as real NEAR balance, draining the payer over repeated calls. NEAR's own architecture docs explicitly flag this exact attack as a known "faucet-draining" risk for relayers, and the underlying reward path is still fully active on stable protocol version 86 (the feature that removes it, `RemoveGasRewards`, is not yet enabled).

### Finding Description
During `apply_action_receipt`, after a `FunctionCall` receipt executes, the runtime computes a reward for the receiving account equal to `burnt_gas_reward` (mainnet `3/10`) of `gas_burnt_for_function_call`, and unconditionally credits it to the receiver account if that account still exists: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

This credit is subtracted from `tx_burnt_amount` (i.e., it is diverted from validators/burn to the receiver) rather than sourced from the caller's own unspent gas - it is real value paid out of the fee the caller/payer already paid for the call: [3](#0-2) 

The reward parameter `burnt_gas_reward` is a `RuntimeFeesConfig` field currently set to `3/10` on mainnet, and there is no restriction on who the receiving contract account is or how many times the mechanism can be invoked: [4](#0-3) 

NEAR's own architecture documentation explicitly calls out this exact class of attack against a relayer that pays gas on behalf of others: [5](#0-4) 

The protocol feature that would eliminate this payout (`RemoveGasRewards`, setting `burnt_gas_reward` to 0%) exists in the codebase but is not yet active on the stable protocol version used by this tree: [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

This is structurally the same bug class as the Splits H-01 report: a permissionless, unbounded, incentive-bearing operation (there: `warehouse.withdraw()` claiming an incentive; here: any `FunctionCall` receipt claiming 30% of its own gas burn as a contract reward) that a passive fund-holder (there: the Splits wallet; here: any account that pays for calls into a contract it does not control, most notably a meta-transaction relayer sponsoring gas for users) unintentionally funds every time the operation is triggered, and which an attacker can trigger in a tight loop to extract value cycle by cycle.

### Impact Explanation
Any account that regularly pays gas for `FunctionCall` receipts landing on a contract it does not control - most concretely a meta-transaction relayer that sponsors gas fees for its users' calls (explicitly one of the reachable actor types: "meta-transaction sender") - can be drained by an attacker who deploys a trivial contract and repeatedly triggers cheap, gas-heavy calls into it via the relayer. Each call transfers 30% of the burnt gas cost from the relayer's fee budget directly into the attacker's account balance, with no cap on repetition. Over enough iterations this is a genuine, unauthorized, permissionless transfer of value from the sponsoring account to the attacker, matching the "concrete unauthorized value movement" impact bar.

### Likelihood Explanation
The primitive requires no special privilege: an ordinary account can (a) deploy a contract, and (b) either call it directly while paying its own gas, or - to actually harm a third party - get a relayer/meta-transaction sponsor to pay gas for calls into that contract. The mechanism is unconditional in `apply_action_receipt`, active on every `FunctionCall` receipt on stable protocol version 86, and is explicitly documented by the NEAR team as an accepted risk that relayer implementations must defend against by application-level controls (e.g., allow-listing which contracts they will sponsor calls to) rather than by protocol-level restriction. That the protocol acknowledges the risk without a protocol-level mitigation currently deployed (`RemoveGasRewards` not yet active) is directly analogous to the Splits team's "Acknowledged" resolution of the analogous H-01 residual risk.

### Recommendation
Since this is a known, documented economic trade-off in NEAR's fee model rather than a coding defect, the practical mitigation is operational (as the docs already suggest): meta-transaction relayers and other gas-sponsoring services must restrict which contracts/receivers they will pay gas for, and/or bound total sponsored spend per signer/receiver, exactly mirroring the Splits team's stated mitigation of adding documentation/UI warnings about withdrawal-incentive configurations that permit this class of drain. Protocol-level, the existing `RemoveGasRewards` feature (already implemented, gated behind a protocol version) removes the underlying incentive; expediting its activation would close this vector entirely.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker deploys a minimal contract `Faucet` whose method does non-trivial but cheap-to-attach-gas work (maximizing `gas_burnt_for_function_call` per call), e.g. a tight compute loop bounded by `max_gas_burnt`.
2. Attacker convinces or abuses a gas-sponsoring relayer (via `DelegateAction`/meta-transaction, or a public "free calls" endpoint) into repeatedly submitting `FunctionCall` receipts to `Faucet`, paid for by the relayer's account, as shown in the reward accounting test: [8](#0-7) 
3. Each receipt credits `Faucet`'s account with `gas_burnt_for_function_call * 3/10 * gas_price`, per the runtime logic at `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:1107-1138`.
4. Repeating step 2 many times, funded entirely by the relayer's gas payments, transfers an unbounded fraction of the relayer's balance to the attacker's `Faucet` account, with no protocol-level circuit breaker.

```markdown
### Contract Reward

A rather unique property of Near Protocol is that a part of the gas fee goes to
the contract owner. This "smart contract gets paid" model is pretty much the
opposite design choice from the "smart contract pays" model that for example
[Cycles in the Internet
Computer](https://internetcomputer.org/docs/current/developer-docs/gas-cost#details-cost-of-compute-and-storage-transactions-on-the-internet-computer)
implement.

The idea is that it gives contract developers a source of income and hence an
incentive to create useful contracts that are commonly used. But there are also
downsides, such as when implementing a free meta-transaction relayer one has to
be careful not to be susceptible to faucet-draining attacks where an attacker
extracts funds from the relayer by making calls to a contract they own.
```

```rust
pub fn test_smart_contract_reward(node: impl Node) {
    let node_user = node.user();
    let root = node_user.get_state_root();
    let bob = node_user.view_account(&bob_account()).unwrap();
    let initial_amount = TESTING_INIT_BALANCE.checked_sub(TESTING_INIT_STAKE).unwrap();
    assert_eq!(bob.amount, initial_amount);
    let transaction_result = node_user
        .function_call(
            alice_account(),
            bob_account(),
            "run_test",
            vec![],
            Gas::from_teragas(100),
            Balance::ZERO,
        )
        .unwrap();
    assert_eq!(
        transaction_result.status,
        FinalExecutionStatus::SuccessValue(10i32.to_le_bytes().to_vec())
    );
    assert_eq!(transaction_result.receipts_outcome.len(), 2);
    let new_root = node_user.get_state_root();
    assert_ne!(root, new_root);

    let fee_helper = fee_helper(&node);
    let bob = node_user.view_account(&bob_account()).unwrap();
    let gas_burnt_for_function_call = transaction_result.receipts_outcome[0]
        .outcome
        .gas_burnt
        .checked_sub(fee_helper.function_call_exec_gas(b"run_test".len() as u64))
        .unwrap();
    let reward = fee_helper.gas_burnt_to_reward(gas_burnt_for_function_call);
    assert_eq!(bob.amount, initial_amount.checked_add(reward).unwrap());
```
