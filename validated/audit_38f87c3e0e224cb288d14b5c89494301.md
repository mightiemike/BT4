Confirmed: only `ExchangeTransactionContract` carries an `expected` field for slippage bound; `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` have no such protection at all.

### Title
Lack of slippage protection in ExchangeInject/ExchangeWithdraw actuators allows unexpected token loss from front-run pool ratio changes - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java)

### Summary
`ExchangeInjectActuator` (and the symmetric `ExchangeWithdrawActuator`) compute the counter-asset amount (`anotherTokenQuant`) required/returned for a bancor-style TRC10 exchange pool operation strictly from the pool's on-chain balances *at execution time*, using a ratio calculated inside `execute()`/`doValidate()`. Unlike `ExchangeTransactionActuator`, which carries an `expected` field in `ExchangeTransactionContract` and validates `anotherTokenQuant < tokenExpected` to reject unfavorable executions, `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` have no equivalent bound field. This mirrors the reported bug class: the "price" (token ratio) is recomputed at the moment of processing rather than fixed/bounded at the time the user forms their intent, with no slippage protection mechanism available to the caller.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeInjectActuator.execute` reads the live pool balances and derives the paired-token amount the sender must contribute purely from the current ratio: [1](#0-0) 

The contract itself only carries `owner_address`, `exchange_id`, `token_id`, and `quant` — no `expected`/minimum/maximum bound field exists to cap `anotherTokenQuant`: [2](#0-1) 

Compare this with `ExchangeTransactionContract`, which explicitly adds an `expected` field and is validated against the computed swap output before execution: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

`ExchangeWithdrawActuator` has the same structural gap: it recomputes `anotherTokenQuant` from live pool balances with only a "precision" sanity check (0.01% rounding tolerance), not a user-supplied slippage bound: [5](#0-4) 

Because Tron transactions have an expiration window and sit in the mempool/block-producer queue before being packed into a block, any other `ExchangeTransactionContract`, `ExchangeInjectContract`, or `ExchangeWithdrawContract` broadcast against the same `exchange_id` and confirmed first will shift `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`, changing the ratio used when the victim's inject/withdraw transaction actually executes.

### Impact Explanation
For `ExchangeInject`, a victim intending to inject `quant` of `token_id` at a ratio observed off-chain can be forced, once other trades land first, to have a materially different amount of `anotherTokenID` deducted from their account — potentially far more than they budgeted for, since `anotherTokenQuant` is computed and *deducted unconditionally* with no cap: [6](#0-5) 
This is unprivileged: any account can call `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` and any other account can front-run the pool ratio via ordinary `ExchangeTransactionContract` trades, leading to unexpected accounting outcomes for the victim (asset/balance loss beyond expectation), matching the report's "unexpected cost increases" and "accounting corruption" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exchange pool ratios change with every matched `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade against the pool, and transaction ordering within a block (and across the confirmation delay) is not controlled by the sender. Any active TRC10 bancor exchange with real trading volume will regularly shift the ratio between when a user forms an inject/withdraw transaction and when it is packed into a block, making exploitation straightforward and requiring no special privileges — only the ability to submit an ordinary `ExchangeTransactionContract` transaction beforehand.

### Recommendation
Add an optional slippage-bound field (analogous to `expected` in `ExchangeTransactionContract`) to `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract`, and validate the computed `anotherTokenQuant` against it in `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute`/`doValidate` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute`/`doValidate`, rejecting the transaction if the counter-asset amount falls outside the caller-specified bound.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes a pending `ExchangeInjectContract` from victim V for pool `exchange_id=E`, injecting `quant` of `token_id=A`.
2. Attacker submits (and gets confirmed first) a large `ExchangeTransactionContract` swap against pool `E` that shifts `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` significantly.
3. V's `ExchangeInjectContract` is then processed by `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute`, which recomputes `anotherTokenQuant` from the now-skewed balances at lines 73-80, and unconditionally debits that amount from V's account at lines 88-99.
4. V ends up contributing a different (larger or smaller) amount of the counter-asset than intended when they formed the transaction, with no ability to have specified or enforced a bound — the exact "no slippage protection on a dynamically recomputed exchange rate" pattern described in the report.
