### Title
Wallet contract pays relayer fee for emulated ERC-20/base-token transfers even when the underlying transfer action fails - (File: `runtime/near-wallet-contract/implementation/wallet-contract/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
In the eth-wallet contract, when a user submits an emulated `EOABaseTokenTransfer` or `ERC20Transfer`, a relayer fee is paid out as an independent, unconditional NEAR transfer receipt at the moment the transaction is parsed, before the "payload" action (the base-token transfer or `ft_transfer` call) is known to succeed. This is the exact same bug class as the Amp.sol report: a value-moving operation is dispatched without checking/waiting on the success of the corresponding "transferFrom"-equivalent call.

### Finding Description
`inner_rlp_execute` computes the relayer fee and creates the refund promise directly via the raw `env::promise_batch_create` / `env::promise_batch_action_transfer` host calls, independent of the `Promise` chain that carries out the actual transfer/`ft_transfer` action and its callback: [1](#0-0) 

This fee-refund receipt is scheduled as a standalone batch, not `.then()`-chained after the transfer/`ft_transfer` action, and not gated on the `PromiseResult` that is later checked in `rlp_execute_callback`: [2](#0-1) 

By contrast, every other cross-contract-dependent value movement in this contract *is* properly gated on `env::promise_result`/`PromiseResult` — e.g., the caller-deposit refund on failure at lines 296-305, and the address-registrar / NEP-141 storage-balance callbacks at lines 141-159 and 203-221. The relayer-fee transfer is the one exception: it is fired-and-forgotten before the outcome of the base-token transfer or `ft_transfer` call is known, exactly mirroring the Amp.sol pattern of moving value based on an assumed (unchecked) success of a dependent operation.

### Impact Explanation
Because the fee promise is independent of the main action's promise result, a relayer can always collect the fee taken from the wallet's own NEAR balance regardless of whether the emulated ERC-20 transfer or base-token transfer actually succeeds. For example:
- An `ERC20Transfer` whose `ft_transfer` call later fails (e.g., insufficient token balance, receiver not registered/`storage_deposit` failure, gas exhaustion) still results in the fee being paid to the relayer, because the fee-transfer receipt was already dispatched independently in `inner_rlp_execute` before the outcome of the `ft_transfer` chain is known.
- Since anyone can act as (or collude with) the relayer submitting a transaction on behalf of a wallet (the "meta-transaction sender" role), this allows unauthorized value extraction from the wallet-contract account's own $NEAR balance — the contract pays for a service it never received, and this can be repeated on every nonce cycle. This is a concrete unauthorized value movement, not merely a resource-only or no-impact defect.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any relayer submitting the RLP-encoded transaction (via `rlp_execute`, reachable by a plain function call/meta-tx as described) can trigger this: they simply need to relay a transaction whose fee is non-zero and whose ultimate ERC-20/`ft_transfer` action is destined to fail (which the relayer trivially controls or can predict, e.g. by picking a token amount exceeding balance, or racing an unregistered `receiver_id` and letting the follow-on `storage_deposit`+`ft_transfer` batch fail). No special privileges beyond being a normal caller/relayer of the wallet contract are required, making this a Medium/High likelihood, directly reachable bug class.

### Recommendation
Chain the relayer fee-refund transfer after the completion of the transfer/`ft_transfer` action (or its callback), and only dispatch it when `env::promise_result`/`PromiseResult::Successful` confirms the action succeeded — mirroring the pattern already used for `CallerDeposit` refunds in `rlp_execute_callback`. Concretely, defer creation of the fee-refund promise (currently at lib.rs:382-384) until inside `rlp_execute_callback` (or an equivalent callback) and gate it on `PromiseResult::Successful` of the transfer/`ft_transfer` receipt.

### Proof of Concept
1. A user signs an RLP Ethereum transaction representing an `ERC20Transfer` with a non-zero fee (`tx.max_fee_per_gas * tx.gas_limit` converted to yoctoNEAR), for an amount larger than the wallet's actual token balance on the target NEP-141 contract.
2. A relayer submits this transaction to `rlp_execute`. In `inner_rlp_execute`, the fee-refund promise to the relayer's account is created unconditionally at lines 381-384 before the `ERC20Transfer` promise chain (`storage_balance_of` → `nep_141_storage_balance_callback` → `ft_transfer`) is even built.
3. The `ft_transfer` call subsequently fails (insufficient token balance). `rlp_execute_callback` correctly reports `success: false` and refunds any `CallerDeposit`, but the relayer's fee transfer receipt — already independently dispatched — executes and succeeds regardless.
4. Result: the wallet's $NEAR balance is drained by the fee amount even though the requested token transfer never happened, and the relayer can repeat this on subsequent nonces.
