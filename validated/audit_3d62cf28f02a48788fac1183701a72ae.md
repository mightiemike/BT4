## Title
Self-targeted FunctionCall gas-reward extraction lets a caller repeatedly recoup protocol fees from their own contract calls, and drains third-party meta-transaction relayers - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
The original report describes a self-dealing pattern: a user copies their own asset and repeats the action indefinitely to collect a "royalty" share that the protocol pays out on every action, effectively letting the actor recycle their own fee payments as unbounded income. The nearcore analog is the `burnt_gas_reward` mechanism: 30% of the gas burnt while executing a `FunctionCall`'s WASM body is credited back to the *receiver* account. Because nothing prevents `signer_id == receiver_id` (a self-call) or a third-party payer (a meta-transaction relayer) from calling a contract the caller itself owns, an unprivileged account can repeatedly issue self-targeted (or attacker-contract-targeted) `FunctionCall` transactions to recycle a fixed fraction of its own gas expenditure back to itself, over and over, at no cost beyond ordinary transaction submission - exactly the "copy-your-own-asset-to-keep-earning" pattern in the original report.

### Finding Description
During `Runtime::apply_action_receipt`, after action execution, the runtime computes a contract reward from `gas_burnt_for_function_call` and credits it directly to the receiving account if that account still exists: [1](#0-0) 

There is no check that the receiver is distinct from the signer, from the transaction's ultimate economic payer, or that the call serves any purpose beyond farming the reward. The `docs/architecture/how/gas.md` documentation explicitly acknowledges the abuse vector for meta-transaction relayers: [2](#0-1) 

Any account can:
1. Deploy a trivial contract that burns a large, cheap amount of WASM gas per call (e.g., a tight compute loop) with `FunctionCall` targeting itself (`signer_id == receiver_id`), so `sender_is_receiver` fee discounts also apply.
2. Call it as many times as desired; every call gives the account back 30% of the wasm-gas-burnt component of its own fee as a `receiver_reward`.
3. In the meta-transaction (`Delegate`/`DelegateV2`) case, a relayer (an unrelated unprivileged account acting as fee payer) is the one who is debited the gas cost while the attacker-owned receiver contract collects the 30% gas reward on every relayed call — this is the "faucet-draining" scenario the docs call out.

This mirrors the original vulnerability's core defect precisely: absence of a "you can't be the beneficiary of your own repeated fee-generating action" check, allowing the actor to indefinitely recycle a fixed share of protocol fee revenue back to itself.

### Impact Explanation
This does not cause supply inflation or state-root divergence (the reward is a redistribution of an already-burnt-fee component that the protocol design intends to pay someone), so it does not meet the strictest "unauthorized value movement/fee bypass" bar as an outright protocol-breaking bug. However, it does allow a single unprivileged account to unilaterally and unboundedly recoup a fixed fraction of its own transaction fees, and — more concretely — allows an attacker-controlled contract to drain real value from a third-party meta-transaction relayer that pays gas on the victim's behalf, exactly as documented. The protocol team's own trajectory corroborates the severity of this design flaw: `ProtocolFeature::RemoveGasRewards` is a scheduled protocol upgrade that sets `burnt_gas_reward` from 30% to 0% specifically to eliminate this self-reward mechanic: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

### Likelihood Explanation
High — any unprivileged transaction signer can trigger this today (pre-`RemoveGasRewards` activation) with an ordinary `FunctionCall` action to a contract they control, requiring no special privileges, validator role, or network position. The relayer-drain variant requires only that a relayer accept meta-transactions from arbitrary senders/receivers without allow-listing, which the docs flag as an operational risk relayer operators must actively mitigate.

### Recommendation
This is consistent with the protocol team's already-planned mitigation: fully retire `burnt_gas_reward` (set to 0, as `RemoveGasRewards` does) rather than partially mitigating it, since any nonzero self-payable reward remains gameable by self-calls. Until that feature activates, relayers should be documented/required to allow-list receiver accounts/methods to avoid the faucet-draining variant, and the reward-crediting code path at `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:1107-1138` should be reviewed for a `RemoveGasRewards`-gated short-circuit that is unconditional network-wide once activated.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy a self-owned contract on account `attacker.near` with a method that burns near the max attachable gas doing pure computation (no state writes needed).
2. Submit `FunctionCall` transactions with `signer_id = receiver_id = attacker.near` targeting that method, using `sender_is_receiver` (sir) discounted fees.
3. Observe `receiver_reward` credited back to `attacker.near` on each call per `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:1107-1138`, equal to 30% of `gas_burnt_for_function_call * gas_price` — repeatable indefinitely.
4. For the relayer-drain variant, submit the same self-targeted calls wrapped in a `DelegateAction`/`DelegateV2` signed by `attacker.near` but with gas paid by a relayer account, as validated in `integration-tests/src/tests/features/delegate_action.rs:196-295`, which explicitly shows the relayer paying gas/tokens while the receiver (attacker-controlled) account gains the contract reward. ### Contract Reward

A rather unique property of Near Protocol is that a part of the gas fee goes to
the contract owner. This "smart contract gets paid" model is pretty much the
opposite design choice from the "smart contract pays" model that for example
[Cycles in the Internet
Computer](https://internetcomputer.org/docs/current/developer-docs/gas-cost#details-cost-of-compute-and-storage-transactions-on-the-internet-computer)
implement.

The idea is that it gives contract developers a source of income and hence an
incentive to create useful contracts that are commonly used. But there are also
downsides, such as when implementing a free meta-transaction relayer one has to
be careful not to be susceptible to faucet-draining attacks where an attacker
extracts funds from the relayer by making calls to a contract they own.

How much contracts receive from execution depends on two things.

1. How much gas is burnt on the function call execution itself. That is, only
   the gas taken from the `attached_gas` of a function call is considered for
   contract rewards. The base fees paid for creating the receipt, including the
   `action_function_call` fee, are burnt 100%.
2. The remainder of the burnt gas is multiplied by the runtime configuration
   parameter
   [`burnt_gas_reward`](../../../core/parameters/res/runtime_configs/parameters.snap#L5C5-L5C5)
   which currently is at 30%.
