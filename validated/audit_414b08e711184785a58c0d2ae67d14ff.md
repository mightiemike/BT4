### Title
DeleteAccount's `beneficiary_id` is never checked for existence, permanently burning the account's remaining balance on a typo or reused address - (File: runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs)

### Summary
`action_delete_account` pays out the deleted account's entire remaining balance to an attacker/user-controlled `beneficiary_id` with no check that the beneficiary account actually exists, mirroring the Solidity pattern of paying a fee/reward to an unvalidated address. Because the resulting refund receipt is a "free" system-predecessor receipt, if the beneficiary turns out not to exist the receipt fails and its balance is *burned* rather than returned to anyone, permanently destroying the funds instead of reverting the deletion or refunding the original owner.

### Finding Description
When a `DeleteAccount` action executes, the current balance is unconditionally scheduled to be paid to `delete_account.beneficiary_id` via a synthetic transfer receipt: [1](#0-0) 

The only validation ever performed on `beneficiary_id` is a syntactic `AccountId::validate` check on receipt creation (format only — length/charset), not an existence check on the target account: [2](#0-1) 

The account itself is deleted immediately afterward (`remove_account`), so there is no way to detect after the fact that the chosen beneficiary was wrong: [3](#0-2) 

Crucially, this balance-refund receipt is a *system-predecessor* ("free") receipt. The protocol-model spec documents the resulting behavior explicitly: such refund receipts burn zero gas, and if the receipt fails (e.g., because the named `beneficiary_id` account does not exist and is not an implicit account eligible for auto-creation), the deposit is burned into `other_burnt_amount` instead of being refunded to anyone: [4](#0-3) 

This is the direct nearcore analog of the Sherlock/Gitcoin finding: a value-transfer target address (here, `beneficiary_id`, a NEAR `AccountId`) is accepted and used for a token payout without confirming it is a valid, reachable destination, and the fallback behavior on an invalid target is to permanently destroy the funds rather than fail safely or preserve them.

### Impact Explanation
Any transaction signer can submit a `DeleteAccount` action specifying an arbitrary `beneficiary_id` string that passes format validation but does not correspond to any existing (or auto-creatable implicit) account — e.g., a typo, a not-yet-created named sub-account, or an account that gets deleted in the same or a prior chunk. When the resulting refund receipt is processed and the receiver account is absent, the entire remaining balance of the deleted account is irrecoverably burned rather than returned to the deleter or any other party. This is a genuine "permanently frozen/lost funds" outcome reachable from a single signed transaction with no elevated privileges required.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires the signer to name a syntactically valid but non-existent (and non-implicit) `AccountId` as beneficiary. This can happen accidentally (typo in a rarely-used sub-account name) or be triggered deliberately by a user/tooling bug, and there is no compile-time or verification-time existence check to prevent it — the runtime path performs no existence check anywhere prior to execution, and the deletion is irreversible once the account is removed.

### Recommendation
Before removing the account in `action_delete_account`, verify that `beneficiary_id` refers to an existing account (or is a valid implicit/deterministic account id capable of being auto-created by the resulting Transfer). If the beneficiary cannot be confirmed to exist, either reject the `DeleteAccount` action with an explicit `ActionError` (similar to existing checks like `DeleteAccountWithLargeState`) or route the balance to a treasury/burn-safe fallback rather than silently destroying it via the failed-refund-burns-deposit path documented at `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs` (referenced in `protocol-model/spec/runtime-execution.md:152`).

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker/user signs a `DeleteAccount` action on their own account `alice.near`, setting `beneficiary_id = "typo123.near"` (a syntactically valid, never-created named account, not an implicit account id).
2. `action_delete_account` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:380-386`) creates a `Receipt::new_balance_refund(beneficiary_id, account_balance)` and immediately calls `remove_account` on `alice.near` (`:387`), so the deletion is unconditional and irreversible regardless of `beneficiary_id`'s validity.
3. When the refund receipt is later processed with `beneficiary_id` as receiver, since the account does not exist and is not implicit, the transfer receipt fails; per `runtime-execution.md:151-153`, a failed refund receipt burns its deposit into `other_burnt_amount` instead of refunding anyone.
4. Result: `alice.near`'s entire remaining NEAR balance is permanently destroyed with no recipient, purely due to an unvalidated `beneficiary_id`.
