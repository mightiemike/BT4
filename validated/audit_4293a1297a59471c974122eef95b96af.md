### Title
Meta-Transaction Deposit Refund Misdirected to Sender Instead of Relayer on Inner-Receipt Failure — (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`)

---

### Summary

When a relayer submits a meta-transaction (`DelegateAction`), it pays the full deposit for all inner actions. If the inner receipt fails on the receiver's shard, the deposit refund is sent to the `predecessor_id` of the inner receipt — which is set to the **sender** (Alice), not the relayer. An unprivileged sender can deliberately craft a `DelegateAction` with a large deposit attached to an inner action that is guaranteed to fail, causing the relayer to permanently lose the deposit while the sender gains it.

---

### Finding Description

In `apply_delegate_action`, the inner receipt spawned from a `DelegateAction` is constructed with `predecessor_id = sender_id` (Alice): [1](#0-0) 

The comment at line 471–475 acknowledges this explicitly: [2](#0-1) 

When the inner receipt fails, `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` issues a deposit-refund receipt to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()`: [3](#0-2) 

`balance_refund_receiver()` returns `predecessor_id` (Alice) because no `refund_to` override is set on a `Receipt::V0`: [4](#0-3) 

The deposit-refund receipt is a free system transfer: [5](#0-4) 

The nearcore architecture documentation explicitly acknowledges the financial incentive this creates for Alice: [6](#0-5) 

There is no on-chain guard — no `max_deposit` parameter, no slippage cap, no protocol-level check — that limits how large a deposit Alice can embed in a `DelegateAction` or that redirects the refund to the relayer on failure.

---

### Impact Explanation

**Broken invariant:** The relayer who pays the deposit should receive the deposit refund if the inner action fails. This invariant is broken at the protocol level.

**Exact corrupted value:** If Alice embeds a `TransferAction { deposit: D }` (or a `FunctionCall` with `deposit: D`) in a `DelegateAction` targeting a contract she controls or knows will panic, and the relayer submits it:

- Relayer's balance decreases by `D` (paid at transaction verification time via `verify_and_charge_transaction`).
- On inner-receipt failure, `Receipt::new_balance_refund` sends `D` to Alice.
- Net: Alice gains `D` NEAR; relayer loses `D` NEAR. Gas costs are separate and also borne by the relayer.

**Scope match:** Stealing of funds from an unprivileged relayer triggered by an ordinary user-submitted `DelegateAction`. No privileged role, no network-level DoS, no external system dependency.

---

### Likelihood Explanation

- Any user who can sign a `DelegateAction` (i.e., any account with an access key) can trigger this.
- The attack requires only a single meta-transaction submission by the relayer.
- The relayer has no on-chain mechanism to cap the deposit it is willing to pay; it must rely entirely on off-chain inspection of the `DelegateAction` content.
- A malicious sender can make the inner action appear valid at inspection time (e.g., the target contract exists and the method exists) but arrange for it to fail at execution time (e.g., by deleting the contract, draining its state, or using a contract that panics conditionally).
- The `max_block_height` window gives the attacker a timing window to manipulate state between relayer inspection and on-chain execution — the direct analog of the "assets transferred after auction started" bug class. [7](#0-6) 

---

### Recommendation

1. **Protocol fix:** Change the `predecessor_id` of the inner receipt generated by `apply_delegate_action` to the relayer's account ID (the outer receipt's `signer_id`), or introduce a `refund_to` field on the inner receipt pointing to the relayer, so that deposit refunds on failure return to the party that paid them.

2. **Slippage cap (analog of `maxCash`):** Add a `max_deposit` field to `DelegateAction` / `DelegateActionV2` that the runtime enforces: if the sum of deposits in the inner actions exceeds `max_deposit`, the delegate action fails before the inner receipt is created. This gives the sender a self-imposed cap and gives relayers a verifiable on-chain commitment.

3. **Short-term relayer guidance:** Until a protocol fix is deployed, relayers must re-verify the sender's state (contract existence, balance, etc.) immediately before submission and set a very short `max_block_height` to minimize the window for state manipulation.

---

### Proof of Concept

```
1. Alice creates a contract `trap.alice.near` that always panics on any call.
2. Alice signs a DelegateAction:
     sender_id:       alice.near
     receiver_id:     trap.alice.near
     actions:         [FunctionCall { method: "drain", deposit: 100 NEAR, gas: 30 TGas }]
     max_block_height: current_height + 50
3. Alice sends the SignedDelegateAction to a relayer off-chain.
4. The relayer inspects it: trap.alice.near exists, method "drain" exists, looks valid.
5. The relayer wraps it in a transaction (relayer pays gas + 100 NEAR deposit) and submits.
6. On alice.near's shard, apply_delegate_action creates inner receipt:
     predecessor_id: alice.near   ← set by line 457 of actions.rs
     receiver_id:    trap.alice.near
     actions:        [FunctionCall { deposit: 100 NEAR }]
7. On trap.alice.near's shard, the function call panics (inner receipt fails).
8. refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits issues:
     Receipt::new_balance_refund(alice.near, 100 NEAR)   ← deposit goes to Alice
9. Result: relayer loses 100 NEAR + gas; Alice gains 100 NEAR.
```
