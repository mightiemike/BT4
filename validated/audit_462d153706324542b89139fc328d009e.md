### Title
Miner-controlled block timestamp is checked against each validator's unsynchronized local clock, causing signer split-brain on identical block proposals - (File: stackslib/src/net/api/postblock_proposal.rs)

### Summary
`NakamotoBlockHeader::timestamp` is set unilaterally by the block's miner and is documented as needing to be "at most 15 seconds into the future" for signers to accept it. The bound is enforced by comparing the miner-supplied value to `get_epoch_time_secs()` — the wall-clock of whichever node performs the `/v3/block_proposal` validation — with no network-wide clock-synchronization or median-time mechanism. Because each signer typically validates through its own co-located node, and nothing in the protocol enforces NTP-level clock agreement across the signer set, the single (minority, unprivileged) block-producing miner can pick a timestamp value near the edge of the allowed window that a subset of signers' clocks judge acceptable and another subset judge as "too far in the future," causing the identical block to be accepted by some signers and rejected by others.

### Finding Description
`NakamotoBlockHeader.timestamp` is attacker-controlled: it is set and signed by the tenure's current miner with no verifiable relationship to global time other than a self-reported value [3](#0-2)  . The only place this field's "future" bound is checked is in `NakamotoBlockProposal::validate`, which each signer's node runs independently, comparing `self.block.header.timestamp` to that node's own `get_epoch_time_secs() + 15` [4](#0-3) .

Unlike Bitcoin, where header timestamps are checked against a median of many prior peer-observed timestamps (see the analogous median-of-11 rule in `SpvClient::validate_header_work`) [5](#0-4) , Stacks Nakamoto blocks have no such network-derived clock reference: the check is purely "my local clock + 15s." Signer nodes are independently operated and their system clocks are not required to be tightly synchronized by the protocol. A miner (the sole, unprivileged actor producing the proposal for its tenure) can therefore choose `timestamp = T` such that `T` is within the 15-second window for signers whose clocks read behind, but already more than 15 seconds "in the future" for signers whose clocks read ahead (or vice versa) — turning a single deterministic input into a non-deterministic accept/reject verdict across the honest signer set.

This equality — "every honest signer validating the same block header must reach the same accept/reject verdict" — is broken solely by an adversarial choice of one field controlled by an unprivileged party (the miner), combined with pre-existing (non-adversarial) clock skew across independently operated signer nodes.

### Impact Explanation
This is a minority-triggerable validation divergence: identical block bytes are validated to different results (`Ok` vs `InvalidTimestamp` reject) purely due to which node's clock backs the check, not due to any majority coordination or Bitcoin-consensus fault. This can fragment the signer weight backing a given block right at the edge of the 70% signing threshold, causing miners' blocks to intermittently fail to gather sufficient signatures (temporary tip disagreement / stalled tenure), or, in the adversarial direction, letting a miner deliberately engineer this split to degrade liveness/censorship-resistance during their own tenure. It does not, by itself, cause a state-root mismatch or fund loss, so it falls under the High-severity "minority-triggerable validation divergence / temporary tip disagreement" category rather than Critical.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires (a) natural or induced clock skew across the independently-run signer fleet greater than fractions of a second near the 15-second boundary, and (b) the miner (or a relay observing near-boundary latency) choosing a timestamp value close to that boundary — both of which are realistic on a permissionless network of independently operated nodes without enforced NTP discipline. This does not require a majority of signers or any privileged access; any minority miner in its own tenure can attempt it.

### Recommendation
Do not rely purely on each validating node's unsynchronized local wall clock for the "future" bound. Consider deriving the future-time bound from a network-observed reference (e.g., the burnchain block's own timestamp/median or a signer-quorum-reported time) rather than `get_epoch_time_secs()` of the single validating node, and/or widen the tolerance to account for realistic clock skew, and require the check to be deterministic based on data included in / derivable from the block and its burn view rather than each node's local clock.

### Proof of Concept
1. Node/signers S1 and S2 have clocks that differ by `Δ` seconds (a realistic, non-adversarial condition for independently operated infrastructure).
2. The miner of the current tenure sets `header.timestamp = local_miner_clock + 15` (maximally aggressive but nominally-valid value from the miner's own clock).
3. S1, whose clock is behind the miner's, computes `get_epoch_time_secs() + 15 >= header.timestamp` → accepts (per `postblock_proposal.rs:657-669`).
4. S2, whose clock is ahead of the miner's by more than `Δ`, computes `get_epoch_time_secs() + 15 < header.timestamp` → rejects with `InvalidTimestamp` (same code path).
5. The same block is simultaneously `Ok` for S1 and `Err(BlockValidateRejectReason)` for S2, demonstrating the split verdict purely from the check in `stackslib/src/net/api/postblock_proposal.rs:637-669` against unsynchronized local clocks.
