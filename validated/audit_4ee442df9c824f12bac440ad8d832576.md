This confirms the analog is real and well-documented as an inherent risk in the codebase itself: `total_deposit` explicitly accounts for delegate-action inner deposits as being paid by the relayer, with the comment noting the refund goes to the sender instead [1](#0-0) . This is charged upfront to the relayer at transaction verification time via `total_cost` computed in `tx_cost`/`verify_and_charge_tx_ephemeral`, deducting `total_deposit(tx.actions)` (which recurses into delegate inner actions) from the relayer's balance [2](#0-1) . When the wrapped inner action fails on the receiver's shard, the resulting deposit-refund receipt is sent to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()`, which defaults to `predecessor_id()` (the delegate action's `sender_id`, i.e., Alice) rather than the actual payer (the relayer) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) .

### Title
Balance refunds for failed meta-transaction inner actions are misdirected to the delegate-action sender instead of the relayer who actually funded the deposit - (File: runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs, runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs, core/primitives/src/receipt.rs)

### Summary
In NEP-366 meta-transactions, a relayer pays all gas and attached-deposit costs for actions delegated by a sender. `total_deposit` for the outer transaction recursively sums the deposits of the inner `DelegateAction` actions and charges the relayer for them at `verify_and_charge_tx_ephemeral` time [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) . However, if the receipt carrying the inner actions fails on the receiver's shard, the deposit refund is routed to `predecessor_id` of that receipt — which `apply_delegate_action` sets to the delegate action's `sender_id` (the unprivileged party who never paid) — instead of back to the relayer who actually funded the deposit [4](#0-3) [3](#0-2) [5](#0-4) . This mirrors the report's core defect pattern: a refund intended for the actual fee/deposit payer is instead sent to an unrelated account by construction of the refund target logic.

### Finding Description
Any account holder can act as "Alice" (the meta-transaction sender) and sign a `SignedDelegateAction` containing an action with an attached deposit (e.g., `Transfer` or a `FunctionCall` with `deposit`) targeting a receiver contract designed or expected to reject the call (e.g., due to insufficient storage, a panic, an assertion, or any deterministic failure condition Alice can trigger or predict). A relayer, unaware of this designed failure, wraps the `SignedDelegateAction` in a transaction it signs and submits. The relayer's account balance is debited upfront for the total deposit computed by `total_deposit`, which recurses into the delegate action's inner actions specifically to account for this cost being borne by the relayer [6](#0-5) .

`apply_delegate_action` constructs the forwarded receipt with `predecessor_id: sender_id.clone()` (Alice), not the relayer [7](#0-6) . When that receipt executes on the receiver's shard and fails, `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` computes `deposit_refund = total_deposit` and issues `Receipt::new_balance_refund(receipt.balance_refund_receiver(), deposit_refund)` [5](#0-4) . Since `balance_refund_receiver()` falls back to `predecessor_id()` when no explicit `refund_to` is set [3](#0-2) , the refunded deposit lands on Alice's account, not the relayer's, even though the relayer's balance was the one debited to fund the deposit in the first place.

This is explicitly acknowledged in the project's own architecture documentation as a known financial-incentive hazard for relayers, but it remains unmitigated in the actual runtime code: "there is a financial incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached but will fail on Bob's shard" [8](#0-7) .

### Impact Explanation
This constitutes unauthorized value movement from the relayer to the meta-transaction sender: the relayer is charged the full deposit amount up front, but the refund upon deterministic failure is credited to the sender instead of returned to the relayer. A malicious sender can systematically drain relayer funds by crafting meta-transactions whose inner actions carry large deposits and are guaranteed (or highly likely) to fail on the receiver, capturing the deposit refund for themselves. Because relayers are a core, permissionless part of NEAR's meta-transaction / gas-sponsorship model (any account can act as relayer for any signed delegate action forwarded to it), this is reachable by any unprivileged transaction submitter with a delegate-action-capable relayer service, and it directly moves value away from the party who legitimately paid for it.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is significant for any relayer that accepts general-purpose (non-whitelisted) delegate actions with attached deposits, since the sender fully controls both the inner action content and, indirectly, whether it will fail (e.g., calling a contract method with conditions it can arrange to violate, or targets known to reject certain calls). The docs already flag this as a known relayer-side risk, indicating the underlying mechanism is real and exploitable without any privileged access, network position, or validator collusion — a single crafted transaction plus one relayer submission suffices.

### Recommendation
When constructing the receipt for a `DelegateAction`'s inner actions in `apply_delegate_action`, set the `refund_to` field (available on `ActionReceiptV2`/`ReceiptEnum::ActionV2`) to the relayer's account (the `signer_id`/`predecessor_id` of the outer delegate-action receipt) rather than leaving it unset so that `balance_refund_receiver()` falls back to the inner receipt's `predecessor_id` (the sender). This ensures deposit refunds for failed inner actions return to the party that actually paid for the deposit, consistent with how gas refunds already correctly go to the signer/relayer.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy or identify a receiver contract `Bob` whose target method deterministically fails for a given set of inputs (or use a plain account with `Action::Transfer` and cause the receipt to fail via, e.g., an account-does-not-exist condition, or use a `FunctionCall` to a contract method engineered to panic/assert given attacker-controlled args).
2. As `Alice` (sender), construct a `DelegateAction` with `sender_id = Alice`, `receiver_id = Bob`, and `actions` containing an action with a large attached `deposit` combined with a triggerable-failure call, sign it as `SignedDelegateAction`.
3. Forward the `SignedDelegateAction` to a `Relayer`, which wraps it in a `SignedTransaction` it signs, per the standard meta-tx flow shown in `test_meta_tx`/`check_meta_tx_execution` [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) .
4. The relayer's balance is debited for `total_deposit` (via `tx_cost`/`verify_and_charge_tx_ephemeral`) [2](#0-1) .
5. `apply_delegate_action` forwards a receipt to `Bob` with `predecessor_id = Alice` [7](#0-6) .
6. The inner action fails on Bob's shard as designed; `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` issues a balance-refund receipt to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()` = `Alice` (predecessor_id), crediting the deposit to Alice instead of the Relayer [5](#0-4) [3](#0-2) .
7. Observe: Relayer's balance decreased by the deposit amount permanently; Alice's balance increased by that same deposit amount, despite never having funded it — net value transfer from Relayer to Alice.

    // the new key to itself.
    let add_key_action = Action::AddKey(Box::new(AddKeyAction {
        public_key: new_key.clone(),
        access_key: AccessKey::full_access(),
    }));
    let candidate_nonce = env.rpc_node().get_next_nonce(&candidate);
    let delegate_action = DelegateAction {
        sender_id: candidate.clone(),
        receiver_id: candidate.clone(),
        actions: vec![NonDelegateAction::try_from(add_key_action).unwrap()],
        nonce: candidate_nonce,
        max_block_height: env.rpc_node().head().height + 100,
        public_key: candidate_signer.public_key(),
    };
    let signed_delegate_action = SignedDelegateAction::sign(&candidate_signer, delegate_action);

    // The relayer wraps and submits it, paying the gas.
    let relayer_balance_before = env.rpc_node().query_balance(&relayer);
    let meta_tx =
        env.rpc_node().tx_from_actions(&relayer, &candidate, vec![signed_delegate_action.into()]);
    env.rpc_runner().run_tx(meta_tx, Duration::seconds(5));

    // Both keys now exist on the candidate account, and its balance is unchanged:
    // the relayer, not the candidate, paid the gas.
    assert!(
        env.rpc_node().view_access_key_query(&candidate, &candidate_signer.public_key()).is_ok()

    let relayer_balance_after = env.rpc_node().query_balance(&relayer);
    assert!(
        relayer_balance_after < relayer_balance_before,
        "relayer balance should decrease (it paid the gas): before={relayer_balance_before}, after={relayer_balance_after}"
    );
