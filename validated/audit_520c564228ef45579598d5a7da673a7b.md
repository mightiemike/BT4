### Title
Meta-transaction balance refunds are misdirected to the DelegateAction sender instead of the relayer who paid for the deposit - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`)

### Summary
In NEAR's meta-transaction (`DelegateAction`) flow, the relayer prepays the attached deposit for all inner actions, but if the wrapped action fails on the receiver's shard, the deposit refund is sent to the DelegateAction's `sender_id` (the account that only signed, but did not pay) instead of back to the relayer who actually funded the deposit. This mirrors the `PrivatePool.flashLoan()` bug class: value that was actually supplied by one party (the payer/initiator) is refunded to a different, non-paying party due to the protocol pulling the "cost" from one identity while crediting/refunding a different one.

### Finding Description
When a relayer submits a `SignedTransaction` wrapping a `DelegateAction` on behalf of `Alice` targeting `Bob`, the relayer's account is charged for all gas costs and any deposits attached to the inner actions up front. `apply_delegate_action` then builds a new receipt for the inner actions with `predecessor_id: sender_id.clone()` (i.e., Alice, not the relayer) [1](#0-0) .

Per the protocol's refund rules, "deposit refunds" for a failed receipt are always sent to the `predecessor_id` of that receipt [2](#0-1) . Because the inner receipt's `predecessor_id` is Alice rather than the relayer, if the inner action (e.g., a `Transfer`) fails on Bob's shard, the deposit refund goes to Alice — even though the relayer is the one who actually paid for the deposit through the meta-transaction. This exact mismatch is explicitly acknowledged in nearcore's own architecture documentation:

> "In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then this balance is taken from the relayer. ... But what if the transaction fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is Alice and therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached but will fail on Bob's shard." [3](#0-2) 

This is the direct structural analog of the flashloan bug: the protocol takes/reserves funds from one account (the relayer, the actual payer/initiator) but returns them to a different account (Alice, the nominal "receiver"/beneficiary of the inner action) when the operation fails, rather than returning them to whoever actually supplied the funds.

### Impact Explanation
A malicious `Alice` can use a compliant relayer service (which trustlessly relays meta-transactions and prepays gas/deposits) to construct a `DelegateAction` with a large attached deposit targeting an inner action that is crafted to fail deterministically on the receiver's shard (e.g., a `FunctionCall` to a nonexistent method, or a transfer to an account that will reject it, or with insufficient gas). The relayer pays for the deposit, but the deposit refund credits Alice's account instead of the relayer's. This is unauthorized value movement from the relayer to the malicious meta-transaction sender, effectively letting Alice drain relayer-subsidized deposits at will, with no economic cost to herself beyond the (recoverable) failed action's send fees.

### Likelihood Explanation
This is trivially reachable by any account that can construct and sign a `DelegateAction` and get it relayed — it requires no special privileges beyond being a normal meta-transaction sender, and any relayer that offers to pay for attached-deposit actions on behalf of users (a common meta-tx/relayer business model) is exposed. The behavior is deterministic (not probabilistic) and repeatable.

### Recommendation
When generating the deposit-refund receipt for a failed inner action originating from a `DelegateAction`, refund the deposit to the actual payer of the deposit (the relayer/original transaction signer) rather than unconditionally to the receipt's `predecessor_id`, or explicitly document/enforce that relayers must not prepay deposits for delegated actions without independently verifying/guaranteeing execution success (e.g., via simulation) before relaying, and consider exposing a distinct "deposit payer" field on the generated receipt so refund routing can track the actual funder, analogous to how `refund_to_account_id` already lets a sender redirect gas/balance refunds [4](#0-3) .

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer `R` receives a `SignedTransaction` from `Alice` containing a `DelegateAction` targeting `Bob` with an inner `Action::Transfer` carrying a large `deposit`, plus a `FunctionCall` to a nonexistent method with minimal gas (guaranteed to fail).
2. `R` signs and submits the transaction, paying gas and having the transaction's cost (including the deposit) charged to `R`'s balance at the point the outer transaction is converted to a receipt.
3. Runtime converts the `DelegateAction` on Alice's shard via `apply_delegate_action`, creating a new receipt with `predecessor_id = Alice`, `receiver_id = Bob` [1](#0-0) .
4. Execution fails on Bob's shard (nonexistent method).
5. Runtime generates a deposit refund receipt sent to the failed receipt's `predecessor_id`, which is Alice, per `Receipt::new_balance_refund` [5](#0-4) .
6. Alice receives the deposit balance that was actually paid for by relayer `R`, confirming the misdirected refund as documented in nearcore's own architecture notes [6](#0-5) .

## Balance refunds in meta transactions

Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor
(a.k.a. sender) of the receipt. This makes sense, as we deposit the attached
balance to the receiver, who has to explicitly reattach a new balance to new
receipts they might spawn.

In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an
inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then
this balance is taken from the relayer.

The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction
and agrees to pay for it, so nothing wrong so far. But what if the transaction
fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and
therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is
something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial
incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached
but will fail on Bob's shard.
