### Title
Energy overcharge in `BatchValidateSign`/`ValidateMultiSign` precompiles: energy billed for the full signature count declared in calldata while verification work is capped and rejected at `MAX_SIZE` - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/vm/PrecompiledContracts.java`)

### Summary
The `BatchValidateSign` and `ValidateMultiSign` TVM precompiled contracts compute their energy cost from the raw calldata length — i.e., from the number of signature "slots" the caller declares in the ABI-encoded input — but the actual verification work they perform is hard-capped at `MAX_SIZE` (16 and 5 signatures, respectively). When the declared signature count exceeds the cap, the precompile performs **zero** signature recoveries and returns a failure result, yet the caller is still charged energy proportional to the inflated, uncapped count. This mirrors the reported bug class: fee is computed from a caller-supplied "requested records" quantity while the actual returned/processed work is silently capped.

### Finding Description
`BatchValidateSign.getEnergyForData` derives the signature count purely from `data.length`: [1](#0-0) 

This `cnt` is not bounded by `MAX_SIZE` — it can be arbitrarily large as long as the caller supplies enough calldata (e.g., large `energyLimit`), so `getEnergyForData` returns `cnt * ENGERYPERSIGN` for however many "signature" entries are encoded, regardless of whether they will ever be processed.

However, `doExecute` explicitly rejects any input where the actual `sigArraySize`/`addrArraySize`/`signatures.length` exceeds `MAX_SIZE = 16`, returning immediately with `DATA_FALSE` and performing no recovery/verification of any signature: [2](#0-1) 

The same pattern exists in `ValidateMultiSign`, where `getEnergyForData` computes `cnt = (data.length / WORD_SIZE - 5) / 5` uncapped, while `execute` rejects any `signatures.length > MAX_SIZE (5)` before doing any verification: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

In both precompiles, the energy-charging function (`getEnergyForData`) and the actual work function (`execute`/`doExecute`) are mismatched exactly as in the reported bug: the fee scales with the caller-declared quantity, while the real work is capped at a small constant, and any excess is billed but not delivered — indeed, in this case, exceeding the cap yields *zero* useful work rather than a partial (capped) result, making the divergence between "amount billed" and "amount of work performed" strictly worse than the referenced report's `min(20, records)` case.

### Impact Explanation
Energy in java-tron is directly convertible to TRX (burned or paid to the energy pool) once an account's frozen energy is exhausted, so this is a direct, quantifiable overcharge of caller funds for a precompile call that performs no signature verification once the declared count exceeds `MAX_SIZE`. Any unprivileged account or contract invoking `batchvalidatesign`/`validatemultisign` with an oversized (but validly ABI-encoded) signature array pays energy proportional to the declared array length while receiving a `DATA_FALSE`/all-zero result with no verification performed — the caller cannot get any value for the energy spent above the cap. This is reachable by any contract call (no privileged role required) and constitutes underpriced/overpriced public-work billing divergence consistent with the accepted impact categories (accounting/settlement divergence for resource fees).

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high: any user or contract can trigger this by calling the `batchvalidatesign(bytes32,bytes[],address[])` or `validateMultiSign(...)` precompile with a signature array larger than `MAX_SIZE` — no special permissions or preconditions are required, and the mismatch triggers deterministically whenever `cnt > MAX_SIZE`. This could be exploited by malicious contracts or dApps designed to intentionally waste callers' energy, or accidentally triggered by developers who don't realize the hard cap exists, exactly as described in the referenced report regarding hidden/silent overcharge from a caller-supplied quantity.

### Recommendation
Synchronize the energy cost with the capped amount of work actually performed:
- In `BatchValidateSign.getEnergyForData` and `ValidateMultiSign.getEnergyForData`, clamp `cnt` to `MAX_SIZE` before multiplying by `ENGERYPERSIGN`, i.e. `Math.min(cnt, MAX_SIZE) * ENGERYPERSIGN`.
- Alternatively/additionally, validate the signature-count cap earlier in `getEnergyForData` (or restructure the precompile so energy is charged only after the size check passes) so that oversized requests fail fast with proportionally minimal cost, rather than billing for the full uncapped declared count.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy/trigger a contract that calls the `batchvalidatesign(bytes32,bytes[],address[])` precompile (address `0x66`-family) with a `bytes[]` signature array containing, e.g., 100 entries (encoded as valid ABI data, with `sigArraySize`/`addrArraySize` = 100).
2. `getEnergyForData` computes `cnt = 100` from calldata length, charging `100 * 1500 = 150,000` energy to the caller's account/energy pool. [1](#0-0) 
3. `doExecute` detects `sigArraySize (100) > MAX_SIZE (16)` and immediately returns `Pair.of(true, DATA_FALSE)` without recovering/verifying a single signature. [5](#0-4) 
4. The caller is billed the full 150,000 energy for zero verification work — a total loss of the energy spent above what any capped/valid call (≤16 signatures, ≤24,000 energy) would have cost, confirming the fee/work divergence.
