### Title
Excess attached deposit is not refunded on successful registration in the wallet contract's `AddressRegistrar` — ([File: runtime/near-wallet-contract/implementation/address-registrar/src/lib.rs])

### Summary
The `AddressRegistrar::register` method, part of the NEAR wallet contract's address-registrar component, validates the attached deposit with a loose lower-bound check (`given_deposit < required_deposit`) instead of requiring an exact match. When the check passes and a new mapping is inserted (the `Entry::Vacant` branch), the entire attached deposit is retained by the contract with no refund of any amount exceeding the actual storage cost — unlike the collision branch (`Entry::Occupied`), which explicitly refunds the full attached deposit back to the caller. Any caller who overestimates the required deposit permanently loses the difference.

### Finding Description
`register` computes the storage cost required to persist a new `(address -> account_id)` entry and compares it against the attached deposit: [1](#0-0) 

The check only rejects the call when `given_deposit < required_deposit`; it never rejects, caps, or refunds the excess when `given_deposit > required_deposit`. In the success path, the deposit is simply kept in full: [2](#0-1) 

Notably, the contract is fully capable of refunding: in the collision branch it explicitly issues a transfer of the entire `given_deposit` back to the predecessor because "no storage was updated": [3](#0-2) 

This mirrors the reported Solidity pattern exactly: a payable/deposit-consuming entry point validates only a minimum-sufficiency condition (`msg.value >= cost` / `attached_deposit >= required_deposit`) rather than an exact-cost condition, and the excess is neither rejected nor refunded on the success path. Because `env::storage_byte_cost()` (backed by the protocol's `storage_amount_per_byte` parameter) is read at execution time and callers must estimate/attach a deposit ahead of execution, any caller who attaches a deposit larger than the minimum required — whether from simple overestimation, stale client-side estimates, or reuse of a deposit value from a prior call — has that entire excess silently absorbed by the contract instead of refunded.

### Impact Explanation
This is a concrete, unauthorized value movement: any unprivileged account calling `register` on the deployed `AddressRegistrar` (a component of the NEAR wallet contract, reachable via a normal `FunctionCall` transaction/RPC call) permanently loses any amount attached beyond the exact storage cost, with no path to recovery. This matches the "permanently frozen/lost funds" impact class called out in the validation criteria.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high: attaching a deposit slightly above the exact required amount is a normal and expected client behavior (to safely cover estimation error), so most callers who are not extremely precise about computing `20 + account_id.len()` bytes times the exact current `storage_amount_per_byte` will overpay and lose the difference on every successful registration.

### Recommendation
In the success (`Entry::Vacant`) branch of `register`, refund any amount of `given_deposit` in excess of `required_deposit` back to the predecessor, mirroring the refund logic already present in the `Entry::Occupied` branch, or alternatively require `given_deposit == required_deposit` and reject/refund otherwise.

### Proof of Concept
1. A caller invokes `register(account_id)` on the deployed `AddressRegistrar` contract, attaching a deposit larger than `20 + account_id.len()` bytes × current `storage_amount_per_byte` (e.g., attaching `2x` the minimum required amount to be safe against estimation error).
2. The address for `account_id` is not yet registered, so `self.addresses.entry(address)` returns `Entry::Vacant`.
3. The check `given_deposit < required_deposit` at [4](#0-3)  passes (deposit is sufficient), and the entry is inserted at [5](#0-4) .
4. No refund transfer is issued for the excess deposit, unlike the `Entry::Occupied` branch which does issue a full refund transfer at [6](#0-5) .
5. The caller's excess deposit remains permanently locked in the contract's balance, unrecoverable by the caller.
