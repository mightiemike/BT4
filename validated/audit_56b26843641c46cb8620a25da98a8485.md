### Title
Meta-transaction (Delegate Action) deposit refunds are misdirected to the delegating sender instead of the relayer who paid them - (File: runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs)

### Summary
In NEP-366 meta transactions, the relayer pays for all attached deposits of the inner actions it delegates on behalf of a sender ("Alice"). When `apply_delegate_action` builds the inner receipt, it sets `predecessor_id` to the delegate action's `sender_id` (Alice), not the relayer, even though the relayer supplied the deposit balance. If the inner action receipt subsequently fails on the receiver's shard, the runtime's deposit-refund logic sends the entire refunded deposit to the receipt's `predecessor_id` (Alice) rather than to the relayer who actually paid it. This lets a malicious or self-serving sender siphon relayer-funded deposits by crafting delegate actions that predictably fail on the receiver shard.

### Finding Description
`apply_delegate_action` constructs the inner `ActionReceipt` with `predecessor_id: sender_id.clone()` — the sender_id from the `DelegateAction`, i.e., Alice — while the deposit for the inner actions was prepaid entirely by the relayer at transaction-conversion time on the relayer's shard. [1](#0-0) 

`total_deposit`, used to compute how much balance was attached (and thus how much to refund on failure), explicitly documents this mismatch: "Relayer pays the deposit but if actions fail, the deposit is refunded to Sender of DelegateAction." [2](#0-1) 

When the receipt execution fails, `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` refunds the full deposit to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()`, which defaults to `predecessor_id()` (Alice) unless a V2 receipt's `refund_to` override is explicitly set (which delegate actions do not set). [5](#0-4) [3](#0-2) 

This is explicitly acknowledged in the protocol documentation: "The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction... But what if the transaction fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is Alice and therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached but will fail on Bob's shard." [4](#0-3) 

This is structurally identical to the DLoop bug class: leftover/refunded value that rightfully belongs to the party who supplied it (the relayer, analogous to the depositing user Alice in the report) is instead credited to a different party (the delegating sender, analogous to the vault) purely because of how the internal accounting attributes ownership (predecessor_id vs. actual payer).

### Impact Explanation
Any account acting as sender in a meta-transaction can construct a `DelegateAction`/`DelegateV2` whose inner actions carry a large attached deposit and are engineered to fail on the receiver's shard (e.g., targeting a non-existent method, an account/receiver mismatch, or any other guaranteed-fail condition on the destination shard, which the sender fully controls when crafting the delegate action). The relayer, who prepaid the deposit gas/balance cost in good faith, never gets it back; instead the full deposit balance flows to the sender's own account via the deposit-refund receipt. This is a direct, unauthorized transfer of value from the relayer to the sender with no compensating mechanism, constituting real fund loss for any permissionless/public relayer service.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires only a single signed meta-transaction crafted by an ordinary account acting as sender, exploiting a relayer service (which by design has no direct control over which inner actions/deposits it is asked to relay beyond off-chain trust assumptions). The failure condition on the receiver's shard is fully attacker-controlled (e.g., non-existent method name, insufficient gas for the callee, or a receiver contract that panics), making exploitation straightforward and repeatable against any relayer that doesn't perfectly simulate/verify execution outcomes ahead of time across shards.

### Recommendation
Track the actual balance payer separately from `predecessor_id` for delegate-action-spawned receipts (e.g., always populate `refund_to` on the `ActionReceiptV2`/`DelegateV2` path with the relayer's account id), so that `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` sends deposit refunds back to whoever actually funded the deposit rather than defaulting to the delegate action's `sender_id`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer R receives a `SignedDelegateAction` from sender A specifying `receiver_id = B` and an inner `FunctionCall` action with a large `deposit` and a `method_name` that does not exist on B (or any other condition guaranteed to fail once gas/exec begins on B's shard).
2. R wraps and submits this as the outer transaction; R's account pays for `SEND`/`EXEC` gas and the deposit is charged as part of R's transaction cost per `calculate_tx_cost`.
3. `apply_delegate_action` runs on A's shard, unwraps the inner actions, and creates the inner `ActionReceipt` with `predecessor_id = A` (sender_id), then forwards it to B's shard. [1](#0-0) 
4. On B's shard, the `FunctionCall` action fails (method not found). `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` computes `deposit_refund = total_deposit` and emits `Receipt::new_balance_refund(receipt.balance_refund_receiver(), deposit_refund)`, where `balance_refund_receiver()` resolves to `predecessor_id()` = A. [5](#0-4) [3](#0-2) 
5. A's account balance increases by the full deposit amount that R paid for; R's balance is permanently reduced by that deposit with no refund path, confirmed by the documented relayer risk description. [6](#0-5)

## Balance refunds in meta transactions

Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor
(a.k.a. sender) of the receipt. This makes sense, as we deposit the attached
balance to the receiver, who has to explicitly reattach a new balance to new
receipts they might spawn.

In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an
inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then
this balance is taken from the relayer.

The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction
and agrees to pay for it, so nothing wrong so far. But what if the transaction
fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and
therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is
something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial
incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached
but will fail on Bob's shard.
