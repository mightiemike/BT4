### Title
Front-running / unpredictable-timing risk via unbounded, immediately-effective `UpdateSettingContract` and `UpdateEnergyLimitContract` — ([File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java])

### Summary
Any smart-contract deployer (an ordinary, unprivileged account — not a witness/committee/admin role) can change `consume_user_resource_percent` and `origin_energy_limit` for their deployed contract at any time, with the change taking effect on the very next block/transaction with no delay, time lock, or two-step commit. This lets a contract owner front-run pending calls to their own contract to alter the energy-cost split imposed on unsuspecting callers, matching the "unpredictable behavior due to front-running/bad timing" bug class from the report (which flagged immediate-effect parameter changes in `GlobalConfig`/`PerpetualGovernance`/`AMMGovernance`).

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` directly overwrites the deployed contract's `consumeUserResourcePercent` in the `ContractStore` as soon as the transaction is processed: [1](#0-0) 

Validation only bounds the value to `[0,100]` and checks that the caller is the contract's deployer — there is no rate limit, cooldown, or advance-notice mechanism: [2](#0-1) 

Similarly, `UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.execute()` immediately overwrites `originEnergyLimit`, again gated only by ownership and a `>0` bound, with no delay: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Both values are consumed live by the TVM energy-splitting logic in `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio`, which computes how much energy the caller vs. the contract creator must pay based on the *current* `consumeUserResourcePercent` and `originEnergyLimit` at execution time: [5](#0-4) 

Because these settings can be changed in one block and take effect for transactions confirmed in the very same or next block, a contract deployer observing pending calls in the mempool can front-run them: e.g., raise `consumeUserResourcePercent` toward 100 or lower `originEnergyLimit`/`consumeUserResourcePercent` split just ahead of a victim's `TriggerSmartContract` call, shifting more of the energy cost onto the caller than the caller assumed when computing `feeLimit`, causing unexpected `OUT_OF_ENERGY` failures or higher-than-expected fee consumption. `UpdateSettingContractActuator`/`UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator` are reachable by any account via a normal broadcast transaction — no special role or committee vote is required, unlike system-parameter changes that go through `ProposalService`.

### Impact Explanation
A malicious (or simply inattentive) contract owner can time these updates to extract more energy fees from unsuspecting users of their own contract, or cause user transactions to unexpectedly fail (revert due to insufficient caller-side energy), all without any warning to the caller. Because the caller's `feeLimit` is computed off-chain based on the contract's state before their transaction is broadcast, and the owner can alter `consumeUserResourcePercent`/`originEnergyLimit` atomically in the same or an intervening block, users have no on-chain guarantee that their assumed energy split still holds when their transaction actually executes. This is a real accounting/DoS-style impact on unprivileged callers interacting with third-party contracts, not merely a self-inflicted issue for the contract owner.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any contract deployer can trigger this at will with a standard, cheap broadcast transaction (`UpdateSettingContract`/`UpdateEnergyLimitContract`), and mempool visibility of pending `TriggerSmartContract` calls to their contract makes front-running straightforward on a public chain. No special privileges, keys, or committee votes are needed — only ordinary control over a deployed contract, which any user can obtain by deploying a contract themselves.

### Recommendation
Introduce a mandatory delay (e.g., N blocks) between when `UpdateSettingContract`/`UpdateEnergyLimitContract` is broadcast and when it takes effect, or require a two-phase "announce then commit" pattern similar to what's recommended for `GlobalConfig`/`PerpetualGovernance` parameter changes, so that in-flight caller transactions are evaluated against the energy-split parameters that existed when they were signed/broadcast, not parameters that can change arbitrarily and atomically just before execution.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deployer creates a contract `C` with `consumeUserResourcePercent = 20` (caller pays 20% of energy) and reasonable `originEnergyLimit`.
2. Victim inspects `C`'s settings, computes `feeLimit`/expects to pay ~20% energy share, and broadcasts a `TriggerSmartContract` call to `C`.
3. Before the victim's transaction is confirmed, the deployer broadcasts `UpdateSettingContract` setting `consumeUserResourcePercent = 100` (via `wallet/updatesetting`, backed by `UpdateSettingContractActuator`), which is applied immediately with no delay.
4. When the victim's transaction executes, `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` now computes the caller's required energy using the new 100% split, causing the victim to pay far more energy than expected or run out of energy and fail, despite having sized `feeLimit` for the old 20% split.
