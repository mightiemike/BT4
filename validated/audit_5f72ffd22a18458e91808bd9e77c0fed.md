### Title
Pausing Interest Accrual Does Not Freeze the Accrual Clock, Causing a Retroactive Interest Jump on Unpause - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar])

### Summary
When the `accrue` pause flag is active, vault `accrue()` returns the stale `index`/`lindex` without updating `last-update`. Because `last-update` is only advanced inside the non‑paused branch (and only when the index actually changed), the clock used to compute elapsed accrual time is never reset for the duration of the pause. When accrual is later resumed, `next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` compute `time-delta` as `stacks-block-time - last-update`, which now spans the *entire* paused interval plus any time since. The vault instantly applies a full-window interest jump to whichever principal/shares exist at that moment, rather than the pause genuinely freezing interest accrual as intended.

### Finding Description
`accrue()` has two branches keyed off `pause-states.accrue`: [1](#0-0) 

In the paused branch, the function returns the currently stored `index`/`lindex` unchanged and, critically, never calls `(var-set last-update stacks-block-time)`. That update only happens in the non-paused branch, and only when `idx`/`next` (or `lidx`/`nliq`) actually differ: [2](#0-1) 

`next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` always compute `time-delta` as `(- stacks-block-time (var-get last-update))` when not paused. Since `last-update` was frozen at its pre-pause value for the whole paused interval, the very first `accrue()` call after unpausing computes a `time-delta` that includes the entire paused duration as if interest had continued accruing in real time — even though the explicit purpose of the `accrue` pause flag is to suspend interest calculation.

Meanwhile, `deposit`, `redeem`, and `system-borrow` gate on independent pause flags (`deposit`, `redeem`, `borrow`), not on `accrue`: [3](#0-2) 

So while accrual is paused, users can still freely deposit/redeem/borrow at the frozen index. Anyone can move principal and shares in and out of the vault during the paused window with zero accrual, and the moment the pause is lifted, the entire deferred interest window (not zero, as intended by the pause, but a lump sum for elapsed real time) lands on whatever balances exist at that instant — this mirrors the report's root cause where a value that should reflect a special, frozen state (`slope=0`, constant bias for infinite locks) is instead recomputed from a generic time-delta formula that ignores the frozen state and produces the wrong result.

### Impact Explanation
Because `last-update` is only advanced on change and never reset/excluded for the paused window ("a clock advanced only on change"), the interest that should never have accrued during the pause is instead dumped in full onto the pool's current supply/debt at the unpause instant. A depositor who enters right before the pause is lifted (at zero cost, since deposits are unaffected by the `accrue` flag) captures a pro-rata share of this retroactive interest jump the instant `lindex` increases — interest they did not economically earn — diluting/redirecting yield away from suppliers who were actually exposed for that time and are unaware their claim on that yield has been redistributed. This is theft of unclaimed yield (High impact) realized purely through the accrual/pause interaction, without needing any counterparty interference — the mechanism operates on the vault's own state, not by one user acting against another.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account holding a Zest DAO-controlled pause permission (this is an operational lever, not a compromise) that toggles `accrue` off and later back on triggers this. No collusion or governance compromise is required for the exploitation step itself: once accrual is paused for any period, any external actor can watch for the unpause event/transaction and front-run it with a deposit, then immediately benefit from the resulting `lindex` jump. The precondition (an `accrue` pause being used at all) is an expected operational feature (e.g., during oracle incidents), making this a realistic, not purely theoretical, scenario.

### Recommendation
When toggling `pause-states.accrue` (or on every `accrue()` call while paused), update `last-update` to `stacks-block-time` so that the paused interval is excluded from future `time-delta` calculations, i.e., the clock should be "paused" together with the accrual logic, not just the index update. Equivalently, persist the pause start/end timestamps and subtract the paused duration from the elapsed time used in `next-index`/`next-liquidity-index`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Vault has active suppliers with total assets accruing interest normally; `last-update = T0`.
2. Governance sets `pause-states.accrue = true` at `T1` (e.g., for an oracle/market incident). `last-update` remains `T0` because no state change happens in the paused branch: [4](#0-3) 
3. During the pause window (`T1` → `T2`), an attacker calls `deposit()`, which is gated only by the `deposit` flag, not `accrue`, so it succeeds at the frozen `index`/`lindex`: [5](#0-4) 
4. Governance unpauses accrual at `T2` (sets `pause-states.accrue = false`).
5. The next call to `accrue()` (triggered by any deposit/redeem/borrow/repay) takes the non-paused branch; `next-liquidity-index` computes `time-delta = T2 - T0` (the full paused span plus any pre-pause elapsed time), applying the entire deferred interest to `lindex` in one step: [6](#0-5) 
6. The attacker, holding shares deposited at `T1.5` with zero real exposure to interest generation, immediately redeems and receives a pro-rata cut of the full `T0`→`T2` interest jump, at the expense of suppliers who were actually invested through that period.

(define-private (next-index)
  (let ((states (var-get pause-states))
        (idx (var-get index)))
    (if (get accrue states)
        idx
        (let (
            (rate (interest-rate))
            (time-delta (- stacks-block-time (var-get last-update)))
            (multiplier (if (is-eq time-delta u0)
                          INDEX-PRECISION
                          (calc-multiplier-delta rate time-delta true))))
          (calc-index-next idx multiplier)))))

(define-public (accrue)
  (let ((states (var-get pause-states))
        (idx (var-get index))
        (lidx (var-get lindex)))
      (if (get accrue states)
          ;; PAUSED: Pass-through without reverting
          (ok { index: idx, lindex: lidx })
          ;; NOT PAUSED: Normal accrual logic
          (let ((next (next-index))
                (nliq (next-liquidity-index))
                (scaled-principal (var-get principal-scaled))
                (old-debt (mul-div-down scaled-principal idx INDEX-PRECISION))
                (new-debt (mul-div-down scaled-principal next INDEX-PRECISION))
                (debt-delta (if (> new-debt old-debt) (- new-debt old-debt) u0))
                (reserve-inc (mul-div-down debt-delta (var-get fee-reserve) BPS))
                (treasury-lp (if (> reserve-inc u0) (mul-div-down reserve-inc (total-supply) (- (total-assets-preview) reserve-inc)) u0)))
            (if (not (is-eq idx next))
                (var-set index next)
                false)
            (if (not (is-eq lidx nliq))
                (var-set lindex nliq)
                false)
            (if (> treasury-lp u0)
                (try! (ft-mint? zft treasury-lp .dao-treasury))
                false)
            (if (or (not (is-eq idx next)) (not (is-eq lidx nliq)))
                (var-set last-update stacks-block-time)
                false)
            (ok { index: next, lindex: nliq })))))
