This confirms the analog clearly: `redeem` explicitly guards against a zero-value division result with `ERR-OUTPUT-ZERO` [1](#0-0) , but `deposit` has no equivalent guard on `inkind` (the minted-shares amount produced by `mul-div-down`) [2](#0-1) . This is the same "division rounding can make output effectively zero" bug class as the external report, just on the deposit side. This pattern is identical across all six vault contracts (STX, sBTC, USDC, USDH, stSTX, stSTXbtc).

### Title
Deposit share calculation lacks a zero-output guard, allowing division rounding to strand a depositor's funds with zero shares minted - (File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar and equivalent v0-vault-*.clar files)

### Summary
The `deposit` function in each Zest vault contract computes the number of shares to mint via `convert-to-shares-preview`, which performs `mul-div-down amount ts ta` [3](#0-2) . Because Clarity integer division truncates toward zero, a small `amount` relative to the vault's asset-to-supply ratio can compute `inkind` (shares to mint) as `u0`. Unlike `redeem`, which explicitly reverts on a zero result via `(asserts! (> inkind u0) ERR-OUTPUT-ZERO)` [4](#0-3) , `deposit` has no such check [5](#0-4) .

### Finding Description
1. `deposit` calls `convert-to-shares-preview` to derive `inkind`, the number of `zft` shares to mint for the caller's `amount` of underlying [6](#0-5) .
2. The only checks performed are: not paused, initialized, not in flashloan, `amount > 0`, `inkind >= min-out` (slippage), and supply cap [5](#0-4) . There is no `(asserts! (> inkind u0) ...)`.
3. If the caller passes the default/no-slippage-protection value `min-out = u0` (which passes trivially since `u0 >= u0`), and `inkind` rounds down to `u0` because `amount * ts / ta < 1` (i.e., the deposit amount is small relative to the vault's assets-per-share ratio), the transaction proceeds.
4. `receive-underlying` pulls the real underlying tokens from the depositor into the vault, `ft-mint?` mints `u0` shares (a no-op, not a revert) to `recipient`, and `assets` is incremented by the full `amount` [7](#0-6) .
5. Net effect: the depositor's real underlying tokens permanently enter the vault's `assets` balance, but the depositor receives no `zft` shares to redeem them back. This is a single-transaction, guard-mutation-ordering flaw identical in mechanism to the referenced Buyout `fractionPrice` rounding-to-zero issue: the "price" (here, shares-per-unit-of-assets) rounds to zero, the depositor pays full value, and gets zero of the compensating asset, while the value silently accrues to all other existing shareholders (their shares are now backed by more assets with no corresponding dilution).

### Impact Explanation
This falls under permanent freezing/theft of the depositor's own funds: the deposited underlying is absorbed into vault `assets` with zero shares minted in return, so the depositor has no claim (via `redeem`) to recover it. The value is not destroyed but is redistributed to existing shareholders, matching the "difference goes to those who cash out" dynamic described in the external report. This is exploitable identically across `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, and `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`, since they share the same `convert-to-shares-preview`/`deposit` structure [8](#0-7) .

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood scales with how much the vault's share price (`ta/ts`) has appreciated from 1:1 (via accrued interest/treasury-LP minting) and how small a deposit is relative to that ratio; as `total-assets-preview()` grows relative to `total-supply-preview()`, the minimum non-zero deposit amount increases, widening the window where a small/dust deposit or a deposit sized just under the rounding threshold mints zero shares. Any integrator, bot, or user who calls `deposit` with `min-out = u0` (the trivial/no-protection value) is exposed without any explicit malicious action needed from a third party.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit zero-output guard to `deposit`, mirroring the existing `redeem` protection:
```clarity
(asserts! (> inkind u0) ERR-OUTPUT-ZERO)
```
placed alongside the other `asserts!` checks in `deposit`, in all affected vault contracts (`v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`).

### Proof of Concept
1. Let vault have `total-supply-preview() = ts` and `total-assets-preview() = ta` such that `ta > ts` (share price > 1, e.g. after interest accrual/treasury-LP minting increased `assets` without minting proportional shares for the depositor's benefit).
2. A depositor calls `deposit(amount, u0, recipient)` where `amount` is small enough that `mul-div-down(amount, ts, ta)` truncates to `u0` (e.g., `amount < ta/ts`).
3. `inkind` is computed as `u0`; `(>= inkind min-out)` passes since `min-out = u0`; no other check rejects `inkind = u0`.
4. `receive-underlying` transfers `amount` of real underlying tokens from the depositor into the vault; `ft-mint? zft u0 recipient` mints zero shares; `assets` is increased by `amount`.
5. The depositor now holds `zft` balance unchanged (no new shares) despite having transferred `amount` of underlying into the vault, and cannot redeem it back — the deposited value has been silently transferred to existing shareholders via the improved `assets`-to-`total-supply` ratio.
