### Title
Contract owner can instantly change `consumeUserResourcePercent` with no timelock or bounds validation, causing unpredictable/front-runnable energy-fee splitting for unprivileged callers - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java)

### Summary
`UpdateSettingContractActuator` allows the deployer/owner of a smart contract to change `consume_user_resource_percent` — the ratio that determines how much of a call's energy cost is paid by the contract owner versus the calling user — and this change takes effect immediately in the very next block, with no timelock, no minimum-change validation, and no notice to callers who have already built and signed transactions expecting the current ratio.

### Finding Description
The actuator directly overwrites the contract's `consumeUserResourcePercent` field as soon as the transaction executes, with the only checks being that the caller is the contract's owner and that the percent is within `[0,100]`: [1](#0-0) 

There is no delay, no old-value/new-value delta bound, and no requirement that the change only apply to future transactions signed after the update — it takes effect for the very next transaction sent to the contract, exactly matching the report's core issue that "an administrator... can update the [dependent] contract without warning" and "updating... takes effect immediately."

This ratio directly drives how energy limits and bills are apportioned between the contract owner (`origin`) and the caller in `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio`/`getTotalEnergyLimitWithFloatRatio`: [2](#0-1) 

and in the final billing step in `TransactionTrace.pay()`, where `percent` (derived from `getConsumeUserResourcePercent`) determines how much of the actual energy bill is charged to the caller vs. the contract owner: [3](#0-2) 

Because a caller has no way to know at the time they sign and broadcast a `TriggerSmartContract` transaction whether the owner will change the percent before it is packed into a block, an owner can:
- Front-run a specific pending caller transaction by setting `consumeUserResourcePercent` to 100 right before it lands, forcing the caller to pay the full energy cost instead of the expected split (or the reverse, to shift the cost onto themselves temporarily to lure users, then flip back).
- Cause previously-fee-estimated transactions to unexpectedly run out of energy (fail) because the caller's actual required energy limit (`callerEnergyLimit`) is a function of the live percent, which can change between fee estimation/signing and execution.

This is a direct, unprivileged-user-facing analog of the reported bug class: a single privileged party changes a parameter governing interaction economics with no timelock/notice, and unprivileged third-party callers bear the unpredictable consequences.

### Impact Explanation
This affects the accounting/settlement of energy fees between callers and contract owners — an unprivileged caller's transaction can unexpectedly fail (running out of energy) or the caller can be charged energy they did not anticipate, due to a same-block/adjacent-block change they had no visibility into and no recourse for. This maps to the "settlement/accounting" and "invalid-state/divergence" impact categories from concrete, reachable production code (not a mock), reachable by any smart contract owner against any user interacting with their contract.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high: any deployed contract's owner is entitled to call `UpdateSettingContract` at will (subject only to signing a normal transaction), the change is applied with zero delay, and users interacting with popular contracts (e.g., DeFi/dApp contracts) routinely trust the currently observed `consume_user_resource_percent` when estimating `fee_limit`. No special privileges beyond contract ownership are required, and the actuator provides no safeguards against abrupt or malicious changes.

### Recommendation
- Introduce a mandatory delay (e.g., apply the new `consumeUserResourcePercent` only starting at a future block/maintenance cycle) similar to the two-phase commit pattern used for committee proposals in `ProposalService`/`ProposalUtil`, so pending user transactions are not retroactively affected.
- Emit an event/log for the pending change and enforce a minimum notice period before it takes effect.
- Consider bounding the magnitude of a single change (e.g., percent can only shift by a limited amount per time window) to reduce the blast radius of a malicious or accidental adjustment.

### Proof of Concept
1. Contract owner deploys a contract with `consumeUserResourcePercent = 10` (caller pays 10% of energy).
2. A user estimates `feeLimit` and signs `TriggerSmartContract` assuming the 10% split, expecting `callerEnergyLimit` to be small.
3. Before the user's transaction is confirmed, the owner submits `UpdateSettingContract` setting `consumeUserResourcePercent = 100` — validated and applied instantly per `UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute` (no timelock).
4. When the user's transaction executes, `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` computes `callerEnergyLimit` using the new 100% split, and `TransactionTrace.pay()` charges the caller the full energy bill instead of the expected 10%, causing unexpected cost or an out-of-energy revert that the user could not have foreseen at signing time.
