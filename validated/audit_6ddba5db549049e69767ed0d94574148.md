### Title
Balance refunds in meta-transactions (`DelegateAction`) are paid to the inner sender instead of the relayer who funded the deposit - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`, `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
When a relayer submits a meta-transaction (`DelegateAction`/NEP-366) on behalf of a sender (e.g. Alice), the relayer's transaction pays for all attached deposits and gas of the inner actions. If the inner receipt fails on the receiver's shard, the deposit refund is routed to the `predecessor_id` of that failed receipt, which is set to the inner `sender_id` (Alice) — not the relayer who actually funded the deposit.

### Finding Description
`apply_delegate_action` builds the forwarded action receipt with `predecessor_id: sender_id.clone()` (the `DelegateAction`'s declared sender) while the relayer's transaction is the one that purchased and prepaid all gas/deposit cost for these actions [1](#0-0) . When that inner action receipt later fails execution (e.g., unmet balance/storage requirement on the receiver, a rejecting contract, or any other failure condition), `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` computes `deposit_refund` and issues it via `Receipt::new_balance_refund(receipt.balance_refund_receiver(), deposit_refund)` [2](#0-1) . `balance_refund_receiver()` defaults to `predecessor_id()` unless a `refund_to` override is set on the receipt [3](#0-2) , and the delegate-forwarded receipt does not set `refund_to`, so the refund goes to the inner sender (Alice), not the relayer.

This is explicitly acknowledged in the code comment: "Note, Relayer prepaid all fees and all things required by actions: attached deposits and attached gas. If something goes wrong, deposit is refunded to the predecessor, this is sender_id/Sender in DelegateAction. ... Therefore Relayer should verify DelegateAction before submitting it because it spends the attached deposit" [4](#0-3) , and in protocol documentation: "The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction and agrees to pay for it... But what if the transaction fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. ... there is a financial incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached but will fail on Bob's shard" [5](#0-4) .

### Impact Explanation
Structurally identical to the reported bug class: funds are deducted from the actual payer (relayer) but refunded to a third party (the delegate sender) on failure, not back to the payer. Since Alice fully controls whether/how the inner receipt fails (e.g., by manipulating a fungible-token balance right before execution, or crafting actions that will predictably fail on the receiver shard after the relayer has already paid the deposit), an unprivileged transaction/meta-transaction sender can cause direct, unauthorized value transfer away from the relayer to herself, with no recourse for the relayer within the protocol.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires only a single crafted `SignedDelegateAction` submitted through a normal relayer flow — no privileged access, no validator collusion, and no network-layer assumptions. It is directly reachable by any account acting as an unprivileged meta-transaction sender, and is deterministic and repeatable by design (the sender can trivially construct actions that fail on the receiver shard, e.g., a transfer exceeding her own token balance at execution time).

### Recommendation
Preserve the relayer's identity through the forwarded delegate receipt, e.g. by carrying the relayer's account through an explicit `refund_to` on the forwarded action receipt (as already supported by `ActionReceiptV2::refund_to` / `promise_set_refund_to` mechanics used for other custom-refund scenarios) so that deposit refunds from failed inner actions of a `DelegateAction` return to the relayer rather than to the delegate's declared sender, or require relayers to explicitly opt into bearing this risk with a protocol-level cap/insurance mechanism.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer receives a `SignedDelegateAction` from Alice specifying a `Transfer` (or `FunctionCall` with attached deposit) to Bob's contract, with the relayer as the outer transaction signer/payer.
2. Relayer submits the wrapping transaction; gas and deposit are deducted from the relayer's balance at transaction-to-receipt conversion.
3. The delegate action is unpacked on Alice's shard via `apply_delegate_action`, producing a new action receipt with `predecessor_id = Alice`, `receiver_id = Bob`, carrying the relayer-funded deposit [6](#0-5) .
4. Alice ensures the inner action fails on Bob's shard (e.g., insufficient storage stake on Bob, or a contract that always rejects the deposit for certain conditions she controls).
5. `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` on Bob's shard issues `Receipt::new_balance_refund(receipt.balance_refund_receiver(), deposit_refund)`, and since `predecessor_id == Alice`, the deposit refund receipt targets Alice, crediting her balance with funds that the relayer paid [2](#0-1) .
6. Net effect: relayer's balance is permanently reduced by the deposit amount; Alice's balance increases by the same amount, without the relayer's consent or any corrective receipt back to the relayer.

    pub fn refund_to(&self) -> &Option<AccountId> {
        match self.receipt() {
            ReceiptEnum::Action(_)
            | ReceiptEnum::Data(_)
            | ReceiptEnum::PromiseYield(_)
            | ReceiptEnum::PromiseResume(_)
            | ReceiptEnum::GlobalContractDistribution(_) => &None,
            ReceiptEnum::ActionV2(action_receipt_v2)
            | ReceiptEnum::PromiseYieldV2(action_receipt_v2) => &action_receipt_v2.refund_to,
        }
    }

    pub fn balance_refund_receiver(&self) -> &AccountId {
        self.refund_to().as_ref().unwrap_or_else(|| self.predecessor_id())
    }
