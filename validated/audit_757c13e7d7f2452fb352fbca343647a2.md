This confirms the mechanism. `debt-preview` uses `next-index`, which computes `time-delta = stacks-block-time - last-update` at [1](#0-0) , and `accrue` persists `last-update` **only when `idx` or `lidx` actually changed** at [2](#0-1) . Because `calc-multiplier-delta` always rounds **up** for the borrow index at [3](#0-2) , `idx` advances by at least 1 unit on almost every call with `rate>0` and `time-delta>0`, so `last-update` gets reset almost every call — this is the "clock advanced only on change" pattern, but here the guard is nearly always satisfied, defeating its own purpose of accumulating a meaningful window.

The protocol fee (`reserve-inc`/`treasury-lp`) is computed **per-call**, not cumulatively, from `debt-delta = new-debt - old-debt` via `mul-div-down` at [4](#0-3) . For any realistic `scaled-principal` (e.g., an amount on the order of `1e6`–`1e9` in underlying-decimal terms), the single-block idx increment (~1 out of `INDEX-PRECISION` = `1e12`) produces a `debt-delta` far below `INDEX-PRECISION`, causing `mul-div-down` to floor `debt-delta` — and hence `reserve-inc` — to `0`. The borrower's compounding is preserved because it's carried forward in the persisted `idx`, but the DAO treasury's minted share (`ft-mint? zft treasury-lp .dao-treasury`) at [5](#0-4)  is silently skipped (no revert) every time this happens.

Because `accrue` is a permissionless `define-public` function at [6](#0-5) , an attacker (or even just organic frequent callers, e.g. every deposit/borrow/repay/redeem transaction of other users, since they all call `accrue`) can repeatedly invoke it in short succession, permanently resetting the reserve-fee window to sub-threshold sizes and causing the protocol's reserve-factor cut of interest to be lost to rounding, forever, instead of being deferred and eventually collected. This mirrors the Sherlock H-6 root cause: a delta computed against a moving "before" checkpoint that resets on every call, rounding to zero when the call cadence is fast relative to the amount accrued. This same pattern is duplicated identically across `v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`, and `v0-vault-sbtc.clar` (and their `local-testing` counterparts), since they all share the identical `accrue` structure.

### Title
Permanent loss of DAO treasury reserve fees via repeated `accrue()` calls rounding `debt-delta`/`reserve-inc` to zero - (File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar and sibling vault contracts)

### Summary
`accrue()` is a permissionless public function in every Zest vault contract that computes the protocol's reserve-factor fee (`reserve-inc`, minted as vault shares to `dao-treasury`) from the *per-call* delta between the previous and next borrow index. `last-update` — the checkpoint used to size the accrual window — is advanced on every call in which the index changes at all, which happens on virtually every call because `calc-multiplier-delta` rounds up. This lets anyone repeatedly call `accrue()` (directly, or simply by transacting frequently against the vault) so that each call's accrual window is too small for `debt-delta` to clear the rounding threshold, causing `reserve-inc`/`treasury-lp` to floor to zero every time, permanently losing the DAO's interest-fee revenue that would otherwise have accrued over a longer, uninterrupted window.

### Finding Description
In `accrue()` at [6](#0-5) :
1. `next-index` computes `time-delta = stacks-block-time - last-update` and applies a round-up multiplier: [3](#0-2)  and [1](#0-0) . Because of the round-up, `next` differs from `idx` by at least 1 (in `INDEX-PRECISION` = `1e12` units) whenever `rate > 0` and `time-delta > 0`.
2. `debt-delta = new-debt - old-debt` is computed by re-deriving `old-debt`/`new-debt` from `scaled-principal` and `idx`/`next` via `mul-div-down` (floor division), then `reserve-inc = mul-div-down(debt-delta, fee-reserve, BPS)` at [4](#0-3) . For a small enough `next - idx` (i.e., a small enough elapsed window), `debt-delta` floors to `0`, and thus `reserve-inc` floors to `0`, so no shares are minted to `dao-treasury`: [5](#0-4) .
3. Critically, `last-update` is set to `stacks-block-time` whenever `idx` **or** `lidx` changed — not conditioned on whether `reserve-inc` was nonzero: [2](#0-1) . Since `idx` changes on almost every call (round-up), `last-update` resets almost every call, so the accrual window used for the *next* fee computation never gets a chance to grow large enough to produce a nonzero `reserve-inc`, unlike the borrower's own debt tracking, which is preserved correctly because it's carried in the persisted `idx` state rather than recomputed as a fresh delta each time.
4. Anyone can call `accrue()` directly (it is `define-public` with no access control), or simply transact against the vault frequently (deposit/borrow/repay/redeem all call `accrue()` internally), to keep re-triggering this sub-threshold window.

### Impact Explanation
This causes a permanent, un-recoverable loss of the DAO treasury's reserve-factor fee revenue on interest accrued by the vault — the protocol's share of yield is silently discarded via floor-rounding rather than being deferred to a later, larger window. This is the same root-cause pattern validated as High severity in the referenced Sherlock report (griefing via high-frequency small-delta claims causing rounding-to-zero loss of yield), and it maps to the in-scope impact class of permanent loss/freezing of unclaimed protocol yield.

### Likelihood Explanation
`accrue()` requires no special permission and no capital — any address can call it, and it is also invoked as a side effect of ordinary vault operations (`deposit`, `system-borrow`, `system-repay`, `redeem`) performed by unrelated users in quick succession, so the condition can be triggered unintentionally as well as maliciously. The precision math (`INDEX-PRECISION` = `1e12`, `scaled-principal` on the order of the underlying asset's raw units) means that, for typical vault sizes and normal-length blocks, the round-up increment to `idx` is tiny relative to `scaled-principal`, making `debt-delta` (and therefore `reserve-inc`) round to zero under sub-threshold time windows — i.e., whenever calls happen more frequently than the time needed for interest to accrue at least one `INDEX-PRECISION`-scaled unit of debt for the given `fee-reserve`/`BPS` ratio.

### Recommendation
Track reserve-fee accrual cumulatively (e.g., accumulate `debt-delta` in a persistent variable across calls and only reset/mint once it exceeds a meaningful threshold), or decouple `last-update` advancement for the fee computation from `last-update` advancement for the index itself, so that a call which produces `reserve-inc == 0` does not reset the accrual window used to compute the next `reserve-inc`. Alternatively, use round-up (not round-down) for `reserve-inc`/`debt-delta` computation so it never silently discards small positive amounts, at the cost of over-accruing by at most 1 unit per call.

### Proof of Concept
1. A vault (e.g. `v0-vault-usdc.clar`) has an outstanding `scaled-principal` on the order of `1e8`–`1e9` (e.g. a position of a few hundred to a few thousand USDC-equivalent, well below `INDEX-PRECISION = 1e12`), and `fee-reserve` set to a typical value (e.g. 1000 bps = 10%).
2. Attacker (or unrelated frequent users) calls `accrue()` (or any of `deposit`/`system-borrow`/`system-repay`/`redeem`, which call `accrue()` internally) on every block/transaction.
3. Each call: `next-index` computes a `time-delta` since the *last* `accrue` call (which was just reset by the previous call), producing a `next` index that is `idx + 1` (in `1e12` units) at most; `debt-delta = mul-div-down(scaled-principal, next-idx, INDEX-PRECISION)` floors to `0` because `scaled-principal * 1 < INDEX-PRECISION` in relative terms; thus `reserve-inc = 0` and no `ft-mint?` to `.dao-treasury` occurs, per [7](#0-6) .
4. Because `idx` did change (however minutely, due to round-up), `last-update` is reset to `stacks-block-time` at [2](#0-1) , so the next call starts its window from zero again.
5. Repeating this every block indefinitely means the treasury's reserve-factor cut of interest is rounded to zero forever, even though the borrower's debt (tracked via persisted `idx`) continues to compound correctly and the vault genuinely earns interest over time — the DAO permanently forfeits its fee revenue on that interest.
