The reported bug is specific to a Uniswap V4-style AMM contract (`OracleUniGeoDistribution`) that converts a WAD-scaled oracle price into a `sqrtPriceX96`/tick under the flawed assumption that both paired tokens (bond and stablecoin) share the same decimals. This mechanism (tick/sqrt-price math for two paired currencies) does not exist anywhere in the Zest v2 Clarity codebase, which is a lending protocol without any AMM or tick-based pricing.

Within Zest v2, decimals are handled explicitly and per-asset rather than assumed equal between two currencies: every asset carries its own `decimals` field in its registry entry, and USD value conversions always normalize using that specific asset's own decimals via `normalize`/`mul-div-down` with `(pow u10 decimals)`, e.g. in `get-asset-value`, `find-and-resolve-asset-value`, `process-debt-asset`, and `process-collateral-asset` [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) . Additionally, all oracle prices (Pyth and DIA) are explicitly normalized to a fixed 8-decimal precision before being combined with any asset-specific `decimals`, via `normalize-pyth` and the documented "DIA prices are assumed to be pre-normalized to 8 decimals" convention [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) .

Since there is no shared-decimals assumption between two differently-decimaled tokens anywhere in this codebase's price/value math, and no tick/sqrt-price computation exists at all, the reported bug class does not map onto any reachable code path here. It also does not fit any of the permitted single-transaction/single-block analog mechanisms (stale cache, clock-advance-on-change, pause pass-through, health-check-then-mutate, fold-absorbs-failure, mutation-before-guard, or multi-step entry stranding value).
