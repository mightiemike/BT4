### Title
Meta-Transaction Balance Refunds Are Sent to the DelegateAction Sender Instead of the Paying Relayer, Allowing Value Extraction - ([File: runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs])

### Summary
When a relayer submits a `DelegateAction` (NEP-366 meta transaction) on behalf of a sender ("Alice"), the relayer's account pays for any token balance attached to the inner actions. However, the receipt spawned for those inner actions is created with `predecessor_id` set to the delegate-action sender (Alice), not the relayer. Because deposit refunds are always routed to `predecessor_id`, if the inner action fails on the destination shard, the attached deposit is refunded to Alice — the party who never paid it — instead of the relayer who actually funded it.

This is also explicitly documented as a known trade-off in the codebase's own architecture docs: [3](#0-2) 

The comment inside `apply_delegate_action` itself acknowledges the mismatch: [4](#0-3) 

This mirrors the reported Cooler bug class: a party who deposits tokens on behalf of a transaction they did not benefit from (the relayer, analogous to the loan requester) has those tokens routed by the protocol to a different, unprivileged party (Alice, analogous to the `owner()`), purely because the protocol's "predecessor" bookkeeping identifies the wrong account as the depositor's origin.

### Impact Explanation
A malicious or app-specific sender (Alice) can craft a `SignedDelegateAction` whose inner action(s) attach a balance and are engineered to deterministically fail on the destination shard (e.g., targeting a nonexistent method, a receiver that will reject the call, or a receiver a hair's-breadth away from a storage-staking/gas failure). The relayer's shard debits the relayer for the attached deposit and gas when converting the transaction to a receipt; when the inner action fails on the destination shard, the deposit refund is sent to Alice, not the relayer. This is unauthorized value movement from the relayer to the sender, entirely triggerable by a single submitted meta-transaction with no relayer or validator misbehavior involved.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires no privileged access — any account can act as "Alice," sign a `DelegateAction` with an attached deposit on an inner action, and induce failure at the receiver shard. The only external dependency is convincing a relayer to relay the transaction, which is exactly the trust model meta-transactions are built around; the docs themselves state relayers "must be aware" of this risk, confirming it is a real, reachable, and previously known but still-unmitigated code path.

### Recommendation
Track the true funder of attached deposits separately from `predecessor_id` when constructing the receipt in `apply_delegate_action`, or set `refund_to` explicitly to the relayer (the transaction's original signer, `action_receipt.signer_id()`) for the forwarded receipt so `Receipt::new_balance_refund`/the deposit-refund logic sends failed-deposit refunds back to whoever actually paid for them, consistent with how `refund_to` is already used elsewhere (e.g., `promise_set_refund_to`) to redirect refunds to the true depositor.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer relays a `SignedDelegateAction` from Alice targeting contract `Bob` with an inner `FunctionCall`/`Transfer` action carrying a large attached deposit and a method name Alice knows will not exist on `Bob` (or gas insufficient to complete execution).
2. On the relayer's shard, `verify_and_charge_tx_ephemeral` debits the relayer's account for gas + the attached deposit as part of `total_cost` for the outer transaction.
3. `apply_delegate_action` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:499-513`) creates the forwarded receipt with `predecessor_id: sender_id` (Alice), and forwards it to Bob's shard.
4. Execution fails on Bob's shard (unknown method / insufficient gas); `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` generates a `Receipt::new_balance_refund(predecessor_id, deposit)` targeting Alice.
5. Alice's account balance increases by the deposit amount that was actually debited from the relayer's account, completing the unauthorized transfer.

```markdown
## Balance refunds in meta transactions

Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor
(a.k.a. sender) of the receipt. This makes sense, as we deposit the attached
balance to the receiver, who has to explicitly reattach a new balance to new
receipts they might spawn.

In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an
inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then
this balance is taken from the relayer.

The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction
and agrees to pay for it, so nothing wrong so far. But what if the transaction
fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and
therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is
something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial
incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached
but will fail on Bob's shard.
```
