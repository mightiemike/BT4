### Title
Missing Slippage Protection in `ExchangeInjectActuator` Enables Sandwich Attack on TRON's Built-in Bancor-style Exchange - ([File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java])

### Summary
The `ExchangeInjectContract` used to add liquidity to TRON's native Bancor-style `Exchange`/`ExchangeV2` pools has no counterpart-token minimum-amount protection, mirroring the `BondingCurvePool.graduateToken` liquidity-addition flaw in the external report. The paired token amount is computed on-chain from the *current* pool ratio at execution time, with no user-supplied bound, so an unprivileged attacker can sandwich the injection with `ExchangeTransactionContract` trades to force the injector to supply an unfavorable ratio and extract value.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeInjectContract` only carries `owner_address`, `exchange_id`, `token_id`, and `quant` — there is no `expected`/minimum field for the paired token, unlike `ExchangeTransactionContract`, which does carry an `expected` slippage-protection field: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

In `ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate`/`execute`, the counterpart amount `anotherTokenQuant` is derived purely from the pool's live `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` ratio at the time the transaction is processed, with no minimum threshold check against what the caller actually intended to accept: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

This is the same root cause as the reported issue: a liquidity-providing operation that trusts the pool's current price/ratio without an enforced minimum, making it exploitable by front-running the ratio just before the injection lands.

### Impact Explanation
Any unprivileged account can submit `ExchangeTransactionContract` trades (validated/executed by `ExchangeTransactionActuator`, using the Bancor-formula `ExchangeProcessor.exchange`) to shift `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` immediately before a pending `ExchangeInjectContract` lands, then reverse the trade immediately after: [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Because the injector (the exchange creator — itself a permissionless role obtainable by any account via `ExchangeCreateContract`) has no way to bound the counterpart amount, the sandwiching attacker can force the injection to occur at a skewed ratio and capture the difference when reversing the price back, directly extracting value from the liquidity provider. This is a concrete accounting/value-extraction impact on live on-chain token balances, not theoretical.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation only requires: (1) observing a pending `ExchangeInjectContract` in the mempool/next block, and (2) submitting ordinary, permissionless `ExchangeTransactionContract` trades before and after it — both are standard, unprivileged operations already exposed on the network. No special access or trusted role is required by the attacker, making this readily exploitable whenever a creator injects meaningful liquidity into an active pool.

### Recommendation
Add a caller-supplied minimum (or maximum) bound for `anotherTokenQuant` to `ExchangeInjectContract`, analogous to the `expected` field already present in `ExchangeTransactionContract`, and enforce it in `ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate`/`execute` so the transaction reverts if the computed counterpart amount falls outside the caller's accepted range.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker monitors the mempool and observes a creator's pending `ExchangeInjectContract` for exchange `X` injecting `tokenQuant` of `firstToken`.
2. Attacker front-runs with a large `ExchangeTransactionContract` sell of `firstToken` into the pool, shifting `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` per `ExchangeCapsule.transaction`/`ExchangeProcessor.exchange`.
3. The creator's `ExchangeInjectContract` executes next, with `anotherTokenQuant` computed from the now-skewed ratio in `ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate` (lines 215-231), forcing the creator to contribute more `secondToken` than intended, with no minimum check to reject this.
4. Attacker back-runs with a reversing `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade, restoring the ratio and pocketing the excess `secondToken` value extracted from the creator's injection.
