### Title
Interest accrual pause pass-through freezes `last-update` while deposits/borrows continue, letting the retroactive "catch-up" interest be diverted to a user who times their entry - ([File: local-testing/contracts/vault/vault-stx.clar])

### Summary
Every vault's `accrue` function checks `pause-states` and, when the `accrue` flag is paused, returns the *current* (stale) `index`/`lindex` without recomputing interest and without touching `last-update` — a "pause that passes through instead of reverting" rather than blocking the call. [1](#0-0)  Meanwhile `deposit`, `redeem`, and `system-borrow` still call `(try! (accrue))` and proceed with token transfers, share minting/burning and debt bookkeeping using that stale index instead of being blocked. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description
`last-update` — the clock that anchors the next `next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` interest computation — is only advanced when the index actually changes:

```
(if (or (not (is-eq idx next)) (not (is-eq lidx nliq)))
    (var-set last-update stacks-block-time)
    false)
``` [4](#0-3) 

While `pause-states.accrue` is `true`, the whole `if` branch computing `next`/`nliq`/`reserve-inc`/`treasury-lp` is skipped entirely and `accrue` just returns the old `{index, lindex}` — the clock is never advanced during this period:

```
(if (get accrue states)
    ;; PAUSED: Pass-through without reverting
    (ok { index: idx, lindex: lidx })
    ;; NOT PAUSED: Normal accrual logic
    ...)
``` [1](#0-0) 

However, this pause flag does **not** gate `deposit`, `redeem`, or `system-borrow`, which each call `accrue` for bookkeeping and then continue unconditionally with `ft-mint?`/`ft-burn?`/`send-underlying`/`var-set principal-scaled` using the frozen `index`. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Because `last-update` is frozen for the entire duration of the accrual pause (which is controlled by the DAO via `set-pause-states`, and can span an arbitrary number of blocks), the moment accrual is unpaused, the next `accrue()` call computes `next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` using the full elapsed time since the *original* `last-update`, i.e. the whole paused interval's interest is applied atomically in a single step. Any depositor/redeemer whose transaction lands in this catch-up window (or who enters right before the unpause, holding shares through it) receives the benefit of the jump on their current share balance, while borrowers who borrowed and fully repaid during the pause paid none of that interval's interest, even though the vault kept processing their borrow/repay transactions as if nothing were frozen. This is the "clock advanced only on change" analog combined with a "pause that passes through instead of reverting" — the pause silently stops the clock instead of blocking the operations that depend on it, letting value shift to whoever's shares/debt are outstanding at the unpause boundary rather than being fairly distributed across the pause period. This pattern is duplicated identically across every vault (`vault-stx`, `vault-sbtc`, `vault-ststx`, `vault-usdc`, `vault-usdh`, `vault-ststxbtc`) and their mainnet `v0-*` counterparts. [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation
Depositors/lenders present in the vault throughout the pause do not have the frozen interval's interest reflected in `total-assets-preview`/`index` at the moment they might redeem inside the pause, while a party who deposits right at the boundary and holds through the catch-up captures a pro-rata share of interest that should have accrued to earlier depositors — a redistribution of unclaimed yield away from the users who actually supplied liquidity during the frozen period. This lands on temporary/permanent freezing or theft of unclaimed yield for depositors, since the interest owed for the paused interval is not attributed based on who held shares during that interval, but based on who holds shares at the instant of the retroactive index jump.

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering this requires the DAO to pause and later unpause vault accrual (`set-pause-states`), which is a normal operational lever exposed in the codebase, and does not require any privileged-key compromise beyond calls the DAO already makes intentionally for maintenance. The `deposit`/`redeem`/`system-borrow` paths remaining open during a paused-accrual window is a straightforward, repeatable sequence of ordinary user calls plus one DAO-issued pause/unpause pair, so once such a pause window occurs, any market participant can act on it.

### Recommendation
Either (a) block `deposit`, `redeem`, `system-borrow`, and `system-repay` while `pause-states.accrue` is true (fail closed instead of pass-through), or (b) advance `last-update` to `stacks-block-time` even on the paused branch so that no retroactive interest is ever silently accumulated and then dumped in one atomic jump when accrual resumes.

### Proof of Concept
1. DAO calls `set-pause-states` on `vault-stx` (or any vault) setting `accrue: true`. [8](#0-7) 
2. During the pause, `last-update` stays frozen at its pre-pause value because the `accrue` branch that updates it is skipped. [9](#0-8) 
3. While paused, users continue to call `deposit`/`redeem`/`system-borrow`, all of which call `accrue` (getting the stale index) and then proceed to mint/burn shares and move funds normally — the pause does not block them. [5](#0-4) 
4. A user deposits a large amount immediately before the DAO unpauses accrual, obtaining shares priced at the stale (pre-catch-up) index/ratio.
5. DAO calls `set-pause-states` again with `accrue: false`. The next `accrue()` call (triggered by any subsequent deposit/redeem/borrow/repay) computes `next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` using the entire elapsed time since the original `last-update`, applying the whole pause window's interest at once. [10](#0-9) 
6. The large depositor from step 4 now holds shares that appreciate by the full retroactive interest jump, even though their capital was not at risk/earning during the paused interval, diluting the yield legitimately owed to depositors who held shares throughout the pause.

Note: I was unable to independently confirm the exact `next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` formulas (their line ranges were not retrieved before the tool budget ran out), so the precise magnitude of the retroactive jump and whether any additional internal safeguard exists there is unverified; this should be checked directly in the vault contracts (`next-index`, `next-liquidity-index`, `total-assets-preview` definitions) before finalizing severity.

(define-public (accrue)
  (let ((states (var-get pause-states))
        (idx (var-get index))
        (lidx (var-get lindex)))
      (if (get accrue states)
          ;; PAUSED: Pass-through without reverting
          (ok { index: idx, lindex: lidx })
          ;; NOT PAUSED: Normal accrual logic
          (let ((next (next-index))
                (nliq (next-liquidity-index))
                (scaled-principal (var-get principal-scaled))
                (old-debt (mul-div-down scaled-principal idx INDEX-PRECISION))
                (new-debt (mul-div-down scaled-principal next INDEX-PRECISION))
                (debt-delta (if (> new-debt old-debt) (- new-debt old-debt) u0))
                (reserve-inc (mul-div-down debt-delta (var-get fee-reserve) BPS))
                (treasury-lp (if (> reserve-inc u0) (mul-div-down reserve-inc (total-supply) (- (total-assets-preview) reserve-inc)) u0)))
            (if (not (is-eq idx next))
                (var-set index next)
                false)
            (if (not (is-eq lidx nliq))
                (var-set lindex nliq)
                false)

                false)
