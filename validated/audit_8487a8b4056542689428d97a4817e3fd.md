### Title
Metering credit conversion for gas spend rounds down to zero, allowing free capability execution and fund loss to node operators - ([File: core/services/workflows/metering/balance_store.go])

### Summary
The Chainlink workflow (CRE) metering subsystem converts a capability's reported resource usage (e.g., gas spent) into "universal credits" that are deducted from a workflow owner's balance. This conversion divides the reported amount by a conversion rate and rounds the result to a fixed number of decimal places [1](#0-0) . Just as in the Surge Protocol bug — where `_totalDebt * _borrowRate * _timeDelta / (365 days * 1e18)` could truncate to `0` for small enough inputs, causing lenders to receive no interest — the metering conversion `amount.Div(rate).Round(defaultDecimalPrecision)` can truncate to `0.0000000000` when the gas amount is small relative to the conversion rate, causing zero credits to be deducted for real, non-zero resource consumption [2](#0-1) .

### Finding Description
`convertToBalance` in `balanceStore` handles gas-type resource dimensions specially: it computes `amount.Div(rate).Round(defaultDecimalPrecision)`, where `defaultDecimalPrecision` is fixed at 10 [3](#0-2) . If the gas amount reported by a node for a given capability call is small relative to the "gas per credit" conversion rate configured by the billing service, the quotient can be smaller than `1e-10` and rounds to exactly zero.

This converted value flows through `Report.Settle`, which is the unprivileged-user-reachable path invoked at the end of every capability call in a workflow execution: for each unit of spend reported by DON nodes, it calls `r.balance.ConvertToBalance(unit, value)` to compute `bal`, adds it to `spentCredits`, and then refunds the difference between the amount earmarked earlier (via `Deduct`) and the actual `spentCredits` back to the user's balance: `r.balance.Add(step.Deduction.Sub(spentCredits))` [4](#0-3) . If `bal` (and thus `spentCredits`) is zero due to rounding, the entire earmarked deduction is refunded, meaning the workflow owner is charged nothing for that capability invocation, even though real gas was consumed on-chain by the DON and paid for by the network/node operators.

Any workflow owner (an unprivileged user relative to the node/billing trust boundary) can trigger this by invoking capabilities whose gas cost, given the current `GasTokensPerCredit` conversion rate, produces a converted balance below the rounding threshold. Because a rate card and gas-per-credit values are billing-service-controlled and can vary, and gas costs for individual capability calls (e.g., very lightweight write operations, or when gas prices are low) can be small, this is a realistic condition rather than a purely theoretical edge case.

### Impact Explanation
Repeated invocation of capability calls that fall under the rounding threshold allows a workflow owner to consume real network/gas resources funded by the Chainlink DON while being charged zero credits per call. This is a direct analog to the reported bug class: a division/rounding defect causes a fee/interest computation to yield 0, and the party who should be paying pays nothing while the counterparty (lenders in the original bug; node operators/billing service here) bears the cost. At scale (many repeated low-gas-cost capability calls), this results in ongoing fund loss for the entity funding the gas/compute (node operators or the billing service), which constitutes a fund-loss/misreporting outcome in the metering and billing trust boundary.

### Likelihood Explanation
The likelihood depends on the specific `GasTokensPerCredit` rate configured by the billing service relative to typical per-call gas amounts. Because rates are dynamic (fetched via `GetWorkflowExecutionRates`) and can be set to large "gas per credit" values (e.g., for cheap/low-value gas tokens) while a single capability call might consume comparatively small gas, the rounding-to-zero condition is plausible without requiring any privileged access — a normal workflow owner triggers it simply through normal usage patterns of low-cost capability calls. The existing test suite exercises the gas-shift and rounding logic (e.g., `Test_Report_FormatReport`), but does not appear to explicitly guard against the amount-to-balance conversion truncating to zero credits [5](#0-4) .

### Recommendation
- Do not allow a non-zero resource spend to convert to exactly zero credits: if `amount` is non-zero but the converted balance rounds to zero, either round up to the smallest representable credit unit or track spend in higher-precision units before rounding for display/logging only.
- Avoid rounding to a fixed low-precision decimal (`defaultDecimalPrecision = 10`) before deduction; perform balance arithmetic at full precision and only round when formatting final output (`FormatReport` / `StringFixed`), analogous to the audit's recommendation to keep additional decimals internally and convert only at the boundary.
- Add an invariant check in `Report.Settle` that flags (or fails closed / switches to metering mode) when reported non-zero resource spend converts to zero credits, similar to how the code already switches to metering mode on other conversion failures [6](#0-5) .

### Proof of Concept
1. Billing service configures `GasTokensPerCredit` such that 1 credit = a large number of gas-token units (e.g., `"10000000000"` wei per credit as seen in test fixtures) [7](#0-6) .
2. A workflow owner triggers a capability whose actual gas spend, after the `Shift(18)` fixed-point conversion applied to gas spend types [8](#0-7) , results in an `amount` small enough that `amount.Div(rate)` is less than `1e-10`.
3. `convertToBalance` rounds this to `0` [2](#0-1) .
4. `Report.Settle` computes `bal = 0`, so `spentCredits` for that step remains `0`, and the full earmarked `step.Deduction` is refunded to the user's balance [9](#0-8) .
5. Repeating this call many times lets the workflow owner consume real gas paid for by the network while their credit balance never decreases for that spend type.

Note: I could not directly confirm the exact numeric thresholds/rate ranges used in production billing configuration (this is controlled by an external billing service, not fully visible in this repo), so the exact frequency/scale at which this rounding-to-zero condition triggers in live deployments is uncertain and would need runtime configuration data to confirm precisely.
