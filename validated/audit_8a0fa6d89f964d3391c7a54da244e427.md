Confirmed: `try_into_near_action` for `Action::AddKey` and `Action::DeleteKey` (`types.rs:262-296`) completely discards the `additional_value` parameter — it is only added into the outgoing `deposit` for `FunctionCall`/`Transfer` (`types.rs:243-261`). Meanwhile `CallerDeposit::new` (`types.rs:180-191`) records the external caller's `attached_deposit` for refund, but `rlp_execute_callback` (`lib.rs:296-316`) only issues that refund on `PromiseResult::Failed`; on `PromiseResult::Successful` the deposit is silently kept with no transfer to anyone. This mirrors the Tokemak bug class: an external caller's `msg.value`/attached deposit is accepted by a router-like contract (the Wallet Contract), the value is not forwarded into the actual action performed (`AddKey`/`DeleteKey` ignore it entirely, same as WETH not reaching the vault), and it is not refunded on success — money becomes permanently stuck in the target eth-implicit account's balance, unrecoverable by the relayer/caller who sent it, and with no protocol-level sweep. This is a genuine "permanently frozen funds" outcome reachable purely by any external relayer/caller submitting an `rlp_execute` call with attached deposit for an `AddKey`/`DeleteKey`-encoded EVM tx.

### Title
Attached NEAR deposit is silently stranded when an external caller's `rlp_execute` call encodes an `AddKey`/`DeleteKey` action - (File: `runtime/near-wallet-contract/implementation/wallet-contract/src/types.rs`)

### Summary
`WalletContract::rlp_execute` is `#[payable]` and accepts an attached NEAR deposit from any caller (relayer or third party) alongside an RLP-encoded Ethereum transaction that is decoded into a NEAR action. When the decoded action is `AddKey` or `DeleteKey`, the attached deposit ("additional value") is dropped on the floor instead of being forwarded to the target account or refunded to the caller on success, permanently trapping the funds in the eth-implicit account.

### Finding Description
`inner_rlp_execute` (`lib.rs:330-473`) builds a `CallerDeposit` for any external (non-self) caller from the attached deposit: [1](#0-0) 

The decoded action's Near-native "additional value" (attached deposit, scaled from the ETH tx's `value` field) is passed into `Action::try_into_near_action`: [2](#0-1) 

Inside `try_into_near_action`, `additional_value` is only folded into the outgoing NEAR action's `deposit` for the `FunctionCall` and `Transfer` variants. For `AddKey` and `DeleteKey` it is completely ignored — no deposit field exists on these NEAR actions to carry it: [3](#0-2) 

Meanwhile `CallerDeposit` (holding the *same* attached deposit) is only refunded when the resulting cross-contract promise fails, in `rlp_execute_callback`: [4](#0-3) 

`AddKeyAction`/`DeleteKeyAction`/`add_access_key_allowance_with_nonce`/`delete_key` promises in `action_to_promise` (`lib.rs:475-501`) carry no deposit at all, and these succeed trivially (they don't consume or reject the attached deposit) — so `PromiseResult::Successful` is the outcome, and the caller's deposit is neither transferred anywhere nor refunded. The yoctoNEAR simply becomes part of the eth-implicit account's own balance with no attribution back to the sender and no mechanism to claim/refund it. This is structurally the same defect described in the report: a value-accepting entrypoint (`rlp_execute`, `#[payable]`) that conditionally fails to route or refund the attached value depending on which downstream action is executed, in contrast to `LMPVaultRouterBase._processEthIn` stranding WETH in the router when `weth.deposit()` and `pullToken()` are both exercised.

### Impact Explanation
Any external relayer or third-party caller who attaches NEAR tokens to an `rlp_execute` call whose decoded Ethereum-encoded action turns out to be `AddKey` or `DeleteKey` will have those tokens permanently absorbed into the target eth-implicit account's balance with no accounting, no event, and no refund path. Since the wallet contract exposes no generic withdraw/sweep, the funds are not "stealable" by a third party in the exact way WETH was in the Sherlock report, but they are unrecoverable by the original sender — a straightforward instance of permanently frozen/misdirected funds, one of the explicitly accepted impact categories (unauthorized value movement / permanently frozen funds). Because `AddKey`/`DeleteKey` calls are routine (adding a relayer key, revoking a key), and any external caller (not just the account owner) can attach a deposit to `rlp_execute`, the loss is directly triggerable by a single transaction from an unprivileged signer/relayer.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-High. Triggering this requires only: (1) an external account (relayer or arbitrary caller) calling `rlp_execute` with a non-zero attached deposit, and (2) the RLP-encoded Ethereum transaction decoding to an `AddKey` or `DeleteKey` NEAR action (the standard flow for relayer onboarding, see `test_wallet_contract_interaction` in `integration-tests/src/tests/features/wallet_contract.rs:280-304`, which adds a relayer key via `rlp_execute`). A relayer that mistakenly (or a hostile relayer intentionally sending a small deposit through a user's signed `AddKey`/`DeleteKey` transaction) attaches any yoctoNEAR will lose it irrecoverably; nothing in validation rejects a non-zero `attached_deposit` for these action kinds.

### Recommendation
Either (a) reject `rlp_execute` calls with non-zero attached deposit when the parsed action is `AddKey`/`DeleteKey` (fail fast with a clear `UserError`, consistent with how `ExcessYoctoNear` is already checked for `FunctionCall`/`Transfer` in `internal.rs:250,264`), or (b) always refund the `CallerDeposit` back to the external caller regardless of promise success when the underlying action does not consume the deposit — i.e., make refund logic depend on whether the action type actually forwarded `additional_value`, not solely on `PromiseResult::Failed`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy the Wallet Contract (`near-wallet-contract`) for an ETH-implicit account, as in `integration-tests/src/tests/features/wallet_contract.rs:227-346`.
2. Have an external account (e.g. `relayer`) call `rlp_execute(target, tx_bytes_b64)` with `deposit = N` yoctoNEAR attached, where `tx_bytes_b64` encodes an `AddKey` (selector `ADD_KEY_SELECTOR`, `types.rs:28`) action for the ETH-implicit account, and a nonzero ETH `value` field (which becomes `additional_value` in `internal.rs:161-164`).
3. Observe: the transaction succeeds (`rlp_execute_callback` returns `PromiseResult::Successful`, `lib.rs:313-315`), the access key is added, and the `N` yoctoNEAR attached deposit is retained by the wallet contract's own account balance — no refund is issued to `relayer`, and no receiver ever gets credited with `N`.
4. Assert `relayer`'s balance decreased by `N` (plus gas) while no other party's balance increased by `N`; the amount is unrecoverable through any exposed method of the contract.
