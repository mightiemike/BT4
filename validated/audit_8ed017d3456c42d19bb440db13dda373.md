### Title
Missing slippage protection in `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java, actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java)

### Summary
TRON's TRC10 bancor-style `Exchange` pools expose three user-facing operations analogous to an ERC-4626 vault: `ExchangeInjectContract` (add liquidity, like `deposit`/`mint`), `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (remove liquidity, like `redeem`), and `ExchangeTransactionContract` (swap). Only the swap operation carries a caller-supplied minimum-output (`expected`) field that is enforced during validation. The inject and withdraw operations compute a proportional counter-token amount purely from the live pool ratio at execution time, with no field for the caller to bound the acceptable output, exposing users to the exact "missing slippage protection" bug class described in the report.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeTransactionContract` includes an `expected` field [1](#0-0) , and `ExchangeTransactionActuator.doValidate()` computes the resulting `anotherTokenQuant` and reverts the transaction with `"token required must greater than expected"` if it falls short [2](#0-1) .

In contrast, `ExchangeInjectContract` only carries `owner_address`, `exchange_id`, `token_id`, and `quant` — no minimum/maximum bound field [3](#0-2) . `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()` computes `anotherTokenQuant` strictly from the current pool ratio (`secondTokenBalance * tokenQuant / firstTokenBalance` or the symmetric case) and unconditionally deducts that amount from the caller's balance/asset, with no caller-supplied ceiling to protect against an unfavorable ratio at execution time [4](#0-3) .

Likewise, `ExchangeWithdrawContract` only carries `owner_address`, `exchange_id`, `token_id`, and `quant` — again no floor/expected field [5](#0-4) . `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` computes `anotherTokenQuant` from the live ratio and credits whatever that yields, with no caller-supplied minimum to guard against receiving less than expected [6](#0-5) .

Because the pool ratio (`firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`) can move between the time a user signs/broadcasts an inject or withdraw transaction and the time it is included in a block — due to other `ExchangeInjectContract`, `ExchangeWithdrawContract`, or `ExchangeTransactionContract` operations against the same `exchange_id` being ordered first (including by a block-producing witness/SR) — the actual counter-token amount charged (inject) or credited (withdraw) can deviate significantly from what the user expected when they signed, with no on-chain mechanism to abort the transaction if the deviation is unacceptable.

### Impact Explanation
Impact is Medium: any account injecting liquidity into or withdrawing liquidity from a TRC10 `Exchange` pool can be forced to pay more of the counter-token than intended (inject) or receive less of the counter-token than intended (withdraw) if the pool ratio shifts due to intervening transactions, with no way to cap the loss. This mirrors the reported vault bug class of unprotected `deposit`/`mint`/`redeem` operations causing unexpected value loss.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: it requires another transaction affecting the same `exchange_id` (inject, withdraw, or transaction) to be ordered between the victim's transaction being signed and included, which is plausible in busy pools or via intentional front-running/sandwiching by an SR or other network participant, since `ExchangeCreateContract` is permissionless and any account can create pools or trade against them.

### Recommendation
Add an `expected`-style bound to `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (e.g., a maximum-another-token-quant for inject, a minimum-another-token-quant for withdraw), analogous to the `expected` field already present in `ExchangeTransactionContract`, and enforce it in `ExchangeInjectActuator`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator` validation the same way `ExchangeTransactionActuator.doValidate()` enforces `tokenExpected` [2](#0-1) .

### Proof of Concept
1. Pool P has `firstTokenBalance` = 100_000_000, `secondTokenBalance` = 200_000_000.
2. Alice broadcasts `ExchangeInjectContract` injecting 1_000_000 of the first token, expecting to also pay ~2_000_000 of the second token based on the ratio at broadcast time, per the computation in `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()` [7](#0-6) .
3. Before Alice's transaction is packed into a block, another account (or the block-producing witness itself) submits an `ExchangeTransactionContract`/`ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` against the same pool that shifts the ratio unfavorably.
4. Alice's `ExchangeInjectContract` still executes (no bound field exists to check against), deducting a `anotherTokenQuant` far larger than she anticipated, with the only protections being unrelated overflow/precision checks in `doValidate()` [8](#0-7) .
5. The same scenario applies symmetrically to `ExchangeWithdrawActuator`, where Alice as the pool creator receives less `anotherTokenQuant` than expected on withdrawal [9](#0-8) .
