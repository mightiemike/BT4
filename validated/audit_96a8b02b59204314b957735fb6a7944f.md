## Analog Found

### Title
Meta-transaction sender can redirect a relayer-funded deposit to themselves via the balance-refund-to-predecessor mechanism - ([File: runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs])

### Summary
NEAR's meta-transaction (NEP-366, `DelegateAction`) flow lets an unprivileged "Sender" get an unprivileged "Relayer" to prepay an attached token deposit for an inner action. If that inner action fails on the final receiver's shard, the protocol's refund logic sends the deposit back to the **predecessor of the failing receipt** — which is the delegate-action Sender, not the Relayer who actually paid for it. This is functionally identical to the Sablier bug class: an unprivileged, trusted-by-convention role ("Sender"/stream owner in Sablier, meta-tx "Sender"/delegate signer here) can reclaim value that another unprivileged party ("depositor" in Sablier, "Relayer" here) contributed, by triggering a failure/refund path, resulting in the depositor being defrauded of value they funded on behalf of a third party.

### Finding Description
In `apply_delegate_action` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:453-535`), the Relayer's transaction pays for all attached deposits and gas of the inner actions, but the code comment explicitly documents the trust assumption being exploited: [1](#0-0) 

The refund routing is implemented generically for all receipts in `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` (`runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:1284-1419`): on failure, `deposit_refund = total_deposit`, and the refund receipt is generated via `Receipt::new_balance_refund(receipt.balance_refund_receiver(), deposit_refund)`: [2](#0-1) 

`Receipt::new_balance_refund` constructs a system-predecessor transfer receipt back to whichever account is returned by `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()`: [3](#0-2) 

The protocol's own architecture documentation confirms the receiver of this refund is the **predecessor of the receipt** (i.e., the delegate-action Sender/"Alice"), not the Relayer who funded the deposit: [4](#0-3) 

Because the inner-action receipt executing on the final receiver's shard has `predecessor_id = sender_id` (set when `apply_delegate_action` builds the forwarded receipt, `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:500-513`), any deposit attached to that inner action that fails to execute is refunded to the Sender — the party who signed the `DelegateAction` but never paid anything — rather than to the Relayer who actually transferred the NEAR to cover it.

### Impact Explanation
A malicious Sender can construct a `DelegateAction` whose inner action carries a large attached deposit and is engineered to predictably fail on the receiver's shard (e.g., targeting a non-existent method, an account/receiver state that will reject the call, or any other deterministic failure condition the Sender can arrange). The Relayer, believing it is prepaying a deposit destined for the receiver (e.g., a token transfer to a third party or a deposit for a contract call), instead has that value redirected to the Sender when the action fails — the Sender "refunds" and pockets a deposit it never paid, at the Relayer's direct expense. This is a concrete unauthorized value movement from one unprivileged party (Relayer, "depositor") to another (Sender, "recipient of the refund") that neither runtime consensus nor validators prevent — it is a protocol-level design choice.

### Likelihood Explanation
This is trivially reachable by any two unprivileged parties who can construct and submit ordinary transactions: a Sender who signs a `SignedDelegateAction` and a Relayer who wraps it into a transaction and submits it, exactly as intended by NEP-366's normal usage flow. No validator collusion, special permissions, or network-level attack is required — only the willingness of a Relayer to service a meta-transaction from an untrusted Sender, which is the exact operating model relayers are expected to support (general-purpose or third-party relayers per the docs).

### Recommendation
This is currently an explicitly acknowledged design/trust limitation in NEAR's own documentation (`docs/architecture/how/meta-tx.md`), analogous to Sablier's official response that "depositors are not expected to fund arbitrary streams" and stream/refund trust is assumed. Consistent with that precedent, no change is strictly required if relayers are expected to bear this risk, but if this is to be hardened, consider: (1) allowing the Relayer to specify itself as the balance-refund receiver for deposits it prepays in delegated actions (mirroring how gas refunds already go to the Relayer/signer, not the Sender), or (2) failing/rejecting delegate actions whose inner-action failure conditions are deterministically known at validation time so a Relayer can simulate and reject risky meta-transactions before submission. Relayer implementations must otherwise treat this as an inherent trust boundary and pre-simulate/pre-vet `DelegateAction`s before funding their deposits, exactly as the existing documentation already warns.

### Proof of Concept
1. Sender ("Alice") signs a `SignedDelegateAction` whose single inner action is, e.g., a `FunctionCall` to a receiver contract/method that Alice knows will fail deterministically (wrong args, insufficient state, non-existent method, etc.), with a large `deposit` attached.
2. Relayer, trusting Alice's request, wraps the `SignedDelegateAction` in its own transaction and submits it, paying for gas and the attached deposit (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:453-535` forwards the receipt with `predecessor_id = sender_id`).
3. The inner-action receipt executes on the final receiver's shard and fails as engineered.
4. `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` computes `deposit_refund = total_deposit` and issues `Receipt::new_balance_refund(receipt.balance_refund_receiver(), deposit_refund)` (`runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:1303`, `:1402-1407`), which per the documented behavior sends the deposit to the receipt's predecessor — Alice — not to the Relayer.
5. Alice receives the full deposit that the Relayer paid for, without having contributed any of her own funds; the Relayer suffers the full loss.

Note: I was unable to fully inspect the exact body of `Receipt::balance_refund_receiver()` (only its call sites and the corroborating architecture doc were available via search); if further confirmation of the precise field returned (predecessor vs. some other id) is needed, a full read of that function in `core/primitives/src/receipt.rs` would be advisable, but the documented protocol behavior and the code comment in `actions.rs` both independently confirm the refund-to-Sender routing described above.

```markdown
## Balance refunds in meta transactions

Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor
(a.k.a. sender) of the receipt. This makes sense, as we deposit the attached
balance to the receiver, who has to explicitly reattach a new balance to new
receipts they might spawn.

In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an
inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then
this balance is taken from the relayer.

The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction
and agrees to pay for it, so nothing wrong so far. But what if the transaction
fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and
therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is
something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial
incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached
but will fail on Bob's shard.
```
