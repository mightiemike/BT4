### Title
Frontrunning of `rlp_execute` fee refund lets anyone hijack a relayer's reward and grief the honest relayer's gas - (File: `runtime/near-wallet-contract/implementation/wallet-contract/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
The NEAR Wallet Contract's `rlp_execute` entry point accepts a base64-encoded, user-signed Ethereum-style transaction (`tx_bytes_b64`) from *any* caller and, for emulated base-token/ERC-20 transfers with a non-zero `fee`, pays that fee to `env::predecessor_account_id()` — i.e., whoever happens to submit the call. [1](#0-0)  Because `tx_bytes_b64` is fully self-contained signed data (the same bytes any relayer would submit), any account can copy a pending relayer's `rlp_execute` transaction from the mempool and resubmit it first, claiming the `fee` for themselves while the original relayer's identical resubmission fails because the wallet's nonce has already advanced.

### Finding Description
`rlp_execute` is a public, unauthenticated method — no access-control check ties the caller to a specific relayer identity: [2](#0-1) 

Inside `inner_rlp_execute`, once the RLP transaction is parsed, the contract explicitly pays the transaction's `fee` field to `context.predecessor_account_id` — the immediate caller of `rlp_execute`, not necessarily the intended/original relayer: [1](#0-0) 

The comment even acknowledges the intended design ("This allows any relayer to safely serve base token transfers from any wallet") but does not consider mempool frontrunning: the `tx_bytes_b64` payload is the entire authorization — it is signed by the wallet owner and contains no binding to a specific relayer's account. Any account observing a pending `rlp_execute(target, tx_bytes_b64)` call in the transaction pool can extract the same `tx_bytes_b64` and call `rlp_execute` themselves with a higher priority/gas price, becoming the `predecessor_account_id` who receives the `fee` refund.

The wallet's internal nonce is incremented as soon as the transaction is successfully parsed (before the refund promise executes), specifically for `EOABaseTokenTransfer` (without address_check) and `ERC20Transfer`: [3](#0-2)  and nonce mismatches are classified as `RelayerError::InvalidNonce`, which bans/revokes the calling relayer's access key when the caller is a bound relayer key (`env::signer_account_id() == current_account_id`): [4](#0-3)  and the error/ban semantics: [5](#0-4) 

Consequently, once a frontrunner successfully replays the transaction first: (1) the frontrunner receives the fee refund intended for the honest relayer, and (2) the honest relayer's own resubmission of the same `tx_bytes_b64` will be rejected with `InvalidNonce` — burning their gas and, if they use a dedicated relayer access key on the wallet, causing that key to be revoked (`ban_relayer`), a further loss to the honest party.

### Impact Explanation
This is a concrete, unauthorized value-movement bug reachable by any unprivileged account (no special permission needed to call `rlp_execute`): a third party can systematically steal the `fee` compensation meant for the legitimate relayer that serves a given eth-implicit wallet, and cause the honest relayer to waste gas and potentially lose their delegated access key. This matches the required "concrete unauthorized value movement" bar, and is directly analogous to the reported Futureswap `maybePaySender`/mempool-frontrunning bug class: a reward tied to whoever submits a valid signed payload, with no restriction that only an authorized submitter (approved relayer/oracle) can claim it.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high in economic terms whenever `fee > cost of frontrunning gas`, which is the entire incentive design of this fee field (it exists specifically to compensate arbitrary relayers for the gas of relaying). Any mempool-observing bot can watch for `rlp_execute` calls with a non-zero fee and resubmit them with higher priority. No special network position, validator role, or trust relationship is required — only observing the pending transaction (which is a normal, permitted capability of any transaction submitter/RPC user), making this straightforward to execute in practice.

### Recommendation
Add a binding between the `fee` payout and an authorized submitter: e.g., require the RLP-signed payload to explicitly name the intended relayer account (analogous to `msg.sender` binding) and pay the fee only to that named account, or require that `rlp_execute` be called via the wallet's own dedicated relayer access key (so `env::signer_account_id() == current_account_id`) rather than paying whichever arbitrary `predecessor_account_id` happens to call it. Alternatively, have the fee payment amount decrease/burn instead of being paid to an arbitrary predecessor unless the predecessor is a pre-approved relayer for that wallet.

### Proof of Concept
1. Wallet owner Alice signs an Ethereum-style ERC20/base-token transfer transaction with a non-zero relayer `fee` and hands it (`tx_bytes_b64`) to Relayer R off-chain.
2. R broadcasts `rlp_execute(target, tx_bytes_b64)` as a NEAR transaction; it enters the mempool.
3. Attacker M observes the pending transaction, extracts the identical `target`/`tx_bytes_b64` arguments, and submits their own `rlp_execute(target, tx_bytes_b64)` call with higher gas price/priority.
4. M's call lands first: `inner_rlp_execute` parses the transaction successfully, increments the wallet's nonce, and creates a refund promise to `context.predecessor_account_id` = M, per [6](#0-5) . M receives Alice's intended relayer fee.
5. R's original transaction now executes against the already-incremented nonce, is parsed as `RelayerError::InvalidNonce`, fails, and (if R used a bound relayer access key) that key is revoked via the `ban_relayer` path shown in [4](#0-3) . R has wasted gas and lost the fee they were entitled to.
