### Title
Wallet Contract pays relayer fee refund unconditionally, before the requested action's success is verified - (File: `runtime/near-wallet-contract/implementation/wallet-contract/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
The NEAR Wallet Contract's `inner_rlp_execute` function sends the relayer's fee refund as a separate, independent promise batch **before** the actual user-requested action (base-token transfer or NEP-141/ERC-20 `ft_transfer` emulation) is even scheduled, and without any dependency on that action's outcome. This is the structural analog of the reported "unsafe ERC20 operation" pattern — value is moved out of an account without checking that the paired operation actually succeeded.

### Finding Description
In `inner_rlp_execute`, once parsing of the Ethereum-style transaction succeeds, the contract immediately creates a *standalone* transfer promise to refund the relayer's fee: [1](#0-0) 

This `refund_promise` is created via `env::promise_batch_create` / `env::promise_batch_action_transfer` and is **not chained (`.then`)** to the promise that performs the user's requested action. The actual action (e.g. the `ft_transfer` emulation or base-token transfer) is constructed afterwards in a separate `match` block: [2](#0-1) 

Because the two promises are independent, the fee-refund transfer to the relayer executes and finalizes regardless of whether the follow-up action (the `ft_transfer` call to the token contract, or the storage-deposit + transfer sequence in `nep_141_storage_balance_callback`) succeeds or fails. The failure/success accounting for the main action is only handled later in `rlp_execute_callback`, which is unrelated to the already-dispatched fee promise: [3](#0-2) 

This is directly analogous to the reported class of bug: performing a token/asset transfer without first confirming/gating it on the success of the associated operation it is supposed to be compensating for — the "unsafe" pattern is not verifying the outcome of a paired transfer before releasing funds.

### Impact Explanation
A relayer that calls `rlp_execute` on behalf of a wallet account is the `predecessor_account_id`/RPC caller and receives the `fee` (an amount computed from the user-signed Ethereum transaction's `max_fee_per_gas * gas_limit`) unconditionally. A relayer can submit a transaction whose associated `ft_transfer`/base-token action is designed or known to fail (e.g., attaching insufficient gas for the second leg, targeting a token contract that will reject the transfer, or any other condition that causes `PromiseResult::Failed` in `rlp_execute_callback`) while still collecting the fee, since the fee-refund promise already completed independently. This results in unauthorized value movement out of the wallet owner's account to the relayer without the corresponding service (a successful requested action) being rendered.

### Likelihood Explanation
The path is reachable by any relayer (an unprivileged transaction submitter/RPC caller) with knowledge of a valid signed Ethereum-style transaction from the wallet owner — no special privilege beyond being the transaction sender is required. Because the wallet contract itself does not gate the fee-refund promise on the outcome of the action promise, a relayer fully controls whether the paired action succeeds or fails while the fee still transfers, making exploitation straightforward and low-cost for a relayer wallet operator.

### Recommendation
Chain the fee-refund transfer to the outcome of the requested action instead of dispatching it independently — e.g., only create/execute the refund transfer inside `rlp_execute_callback` conditioned on `PromiseResult::Successful`, or make the refund promise a `.then()` continuation of the main action promise so it is only paid out when the underlying action succeeds.

### Proof of Concept
1. Wallet owner signs an Ethereum-style transaction (`rlp_execute` payload) specifying a nonzero fee and a base-token or ERC-20 (`ft_transfer`) emulated transfer.
2. A relayer submits this transaction via `rlp_execute`, but crafts/allows the follow-up action to fail (e.g., insufficient gas budget for the NEP-141 call chain, or the token/target rejects the transfer).
3. `inner_rlp_execute` creates the fee-refund promise (lines 382-384) unconditionally and separately from the action promise.
4. The fee-refund promise completes successfully and pays the relayer, while the action promise later resolves to `PromiseResult::Failed` in `rlp_execute_callback` (lines 296-312), reporting `success: false` to the caller — yet the fee has already left the wallet's balance.
