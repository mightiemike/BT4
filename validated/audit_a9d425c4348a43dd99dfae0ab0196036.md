### Title
Missing slippage/minimum-output protection in `ExchangeInjectActuator`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator` enables sandwich attacks on TRC10 bonding-curve exchange pools - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java`, `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java`)

### Summary
The built-in TRC10 AMM-style exchange (`ExchangeCreateContract`/`ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract`/`ExchangeTransactionContract`) uses a constant-ratio bonding-curve pool (`firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`) analogous to the `BondingCurvePool` reported in the external report. While `ExchangeTransactionContract` correctly implements slippage protection via its `expected` field (checked at [1](#0-0) ), the `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` messages carry no equivalent minimum/maximum bound field at all [2](#0-1) . This is the direct protocol-level analog of the reported "Lack of Slippage Protection During Graduation" bug class: a liquidity operation that derives its counter-token amount from a mutable pool ratio with no caller-enforced bound.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()` computes `anotherTokenQuant` strictly from the exchange's live `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` ratio at execution time: [3](#0-2) 

`ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` does the same for withdrawals: [4](#0-3) 

Neither `doValidate()` method accepts or enforces any caller-supplied minimum-received / maximum-paid bound; the withdraw actuator only re-derives the "expected" quantity from the *current* pool state and checks it is "precise enough" relative to that same (already-manipulated) state — it does not protect against the state having been manipulated moments earlier [5](#0-4) . By contrast, `ExchangeTransactionActuator` (the "swap" operation) explicitly validates `anotherTokenQuant < tokenExpected` and rejects the transaction if the actually-computed rate is worse than what the caller specified [1](#0-0) . This shows the protocol designers were aware of the sandwich/slippage risk for one operation type but omitted the equivalent protection for inject/withdraw.

Any unprivileged account can broadcast an `ExchangeTransactionContract` immediately before another account's `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` (front-run), sharply skew the pool ratio, let the inject/withdraw execute against the skewed ratio, then immediately reverse the trade with a second `ExchangeTransactionContract` (back-run) to restore the ratio and lock in profit extracted from the victim's inject/withdraw — a classic sandwich, since transaction ordering within a block is controlled by whichever super representative packs the block (or by fee/priority races), not by the victim.

### Impact Explanation
A successful sandwich against `ExchangeInjectContract` causes the injecting account to deposit both tokens at an unfavorable, attacker-skewed ratio, resulting in a direct value transfer from the victim to the attacker (asset/accounting corruption for the victim's account balances) — this is a concrete on-chain unauthorized value extraction, not merely theoretical, since `ExchangeInjectActuator`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator` are reachable by any broadcast transaction naming an existing `exchange_id`. Withdraw sandwiching similarly lets an attacker manipulate the ratio so the creator's withdrawal receives less of the counter-token than the pool's "fair" ratio implied moments before.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Exploitation requires: (1) an existing TRC10 exchange pool, (2) the ability to have a transaction ordered immediately before/after the victim's inject/withdraw transaction within the same or adjacent blocks — achievable by any actor monitoring the mempool/broadcast transactions and racing transaction submission, similar to standard MEV/front-running on other chains. No privileged role, leaked key, or protocol-level access is required — only ordinary `ExchangeTransactionContract`, `ExchangeInjectContract`, and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` broadcasts.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit slippage-bound field (e.g., `expected_another_token_quant_min` for inject, `expected_another_token_quant_min`/`max` for withdraw) to `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract`, and enforce it in `ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate()`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate()` analogous to the existing `tokenExpected` check in `ExchangeTransactionActuator` [1](#0-0) , rejecting the transaction if the computed counter-token amount is worse than the caller's specified bound.

### Proof of Concept
1. Account A creates or holds a TRC10 exchange pool with balances `firstTokenBalance = X`, `secondTokenBalance = Y`.
2. Attacker broadcasts `ExchangeTransactionContract` trading a large amount of `firstToken` for `secondToken` (or vice versa), skewing the ratio to `X' , Y'` (protected on this leg by their own `expected` field, so it is guaranteed profitable/safe for the attacker).
3. Account A's pre-broadcast `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` (with no `expected` bound) executes against `X'/Y'`, computing `anotherTokenQuant` via [3](#0-2)  at the skewed ratio, causing A to deposit/withdraw at an unfavorable rate.
4. Attacker broadcasts a second `ExchangeTransactionContract` reversing step 2's trade, restoring the ratio close to `X, Y` and capturing the difference extracted from A's inject/withdraw as profit.
