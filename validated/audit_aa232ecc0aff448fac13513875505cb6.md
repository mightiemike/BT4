The reported bug class—small deposits clogging a sequentially-processed queue behind a fixed-size batch-release cursor—does not have a matching analog that crosses an in-scope binding in this repository:

- Citrea's `Bridge.sol` enforces a single fixed `depositAmount` for every deposit and withdrawal , so there is no "small deposit" that can be crafted cheaper than a legitimate one to inflate a queue — unlike the `CampTimelockEscrowNativeOFT.sol` report where variable-size deposits with no minimum could be spammed.
- `deposit()` and `withdraw()`/`batchWithdraw()` do not use a cursor-based `batchRelease()` pattern with a shared `holdPeriod`/`unlockTimestamp` that must be walked in order; each `deposit` is validated independently via Bitcoin inclusion/signature checks and applied immediately , and `withdrawalUTXOs` is only an append-only record consumed off-chain by Clementine operators, not iterated sequentially on-chain .
- The one FIFO-like structure that resembles the report's queue is the sequencer's `DepositDataMempool`, which uses `fetch_deposits(limit_per_block)` to take entries in order . Even if spammed with many cheap deposit-call payloads, this only affects sequencer-side batching behavior/gas — it requires no custody, proof, determinism, or DA-integrity boundary crossing, and any impact is confined to a role (sequencer) explicitly excluded by the rules.

Since no reachable path here breaks a stated binding (cBTC-vs-Bitcoin-deposit equality, proof journal correctness, DA blob completeness, or authorized commitment/method-id upgrade), and the closest analog only touches out-of-scope denial-of-service/rate-limiting concerns tied to a privileged sequencer role, there is no valid finding to report.
