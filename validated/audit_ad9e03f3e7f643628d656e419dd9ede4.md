### Title
Relayer Deposit Stolen via Deliberately Failing DelegateAction Inner Receipt — (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`)

### Summary

In the meta-transaction (`DelegateAction`) execution path, the relayer pays all deposits for inner actions, but when the inner receipt fails on the receiver's shard, the deposit refund is sent to the **delegate sender** (Alice), not the relayer who actually paid. An unprivileged user can deliberately craft a `DelegateAction` whose inner function call will fail, causing the relayer to permanently lose the attached deposit to Alice.

### Finding Description

When `apply_delegate_action` processes a `DelegateAction`, it constructs the inner receipt with `predecessor_id` set to `sender_id` (Alice, the delegate signer): [1](#0-0) 

The code comment immediately below this construction explicitly acknowledges the broken invariant: [2](#0-1) 

When the inner receipt fails on the receiver's shard, `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` computes the deposit refund and sends it to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()`: [3](#0-2) 

`balance_refund_receiver()` returns `predecessor_id()` for `Receipt::V0` (since `refund_to` is `None` for `ReceiptEnum::Action`): [4](#0-3) 

The inner receipt's `predecessor_id` is Alice (`sender_id`), not the relayer. So the deposit refund goes to Alice, not the relayer who funded it.

The architecture documentation explicitly acknowledges this as a financial attack vector: [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation

The broken invariant is: **the party who pays a deposit should receive the refund if the action fails**. In a meta-transaction, the relayer pays the deposit (deducted from the relayer's account at transaction processing time), but the inner receipt's `predecessor_id` is Alice. When the inner receipt fails, the protocol's deposit-refund rule ("refund goes to `predecessor_id`") sends the funds to Alice instead of the relayer.

Concrete corrupted value: the relayer's account balance is reduced by `deposit` (paid at transaction time), and Alice's account balance is increased by `deposit` (received as refund), with no legitimate transfer between them.

### Likelihood Explanation

Any user who can interact with a relayer service can exploit this. The attacker:
1. Signs a `DelegateAction` targeting a non-existent method on a contract, or a method that will panic, with a large `deposit` attached.
2. Submits it to a relayer.
3. The relayer, after checking the `DelegateAction` signature and nonce validity (which pass), submits the outer transaction and pays the deposit.
4. The inner receipt fails on the receiver's shard.
5. The deposit refund is sent to Alice.

The relayer loses gas (refunded to the relayer as signer) but loses the deposit permanently to Alice. The attack is repeatable and requires no special privileges — only the ability to sign a `DelegateAction` and submit it to a relayer.

### Recommendation

The inner receipt generated in `apply_delegate_action` should use `ReceiptEnum::ActionV2` (which supports `refund_to`) and set `refund_to: Some(action_receipt.signer_id().clone())` (the relayer) so that deposit refunds on failure are returned to the relayer who paid, not to the delegate sender. [6](#0-5) 

Alternatively, the protocol could enforce that any `DelegateAction` with a non-zero deposit in its inner actions must use `ReceiptEnum::ActionV2` with `refund_to` pointing to the relayer.

### Proof of Concept

1. Alice creates a `DelegateAction` with `receiver_id = "some_contract"`, inner action = `FunctionCall { method_name: "nonexistent", deposit: 10_NEAR, gas: 300Tgas }`, valid nonce and signature.
2. Alice submits this to a relayer. The relayer verifies the signature and nonce (both valid), wraps it in a transaction, and submits it — paying 10 NEAR deposit.
3. On Alice's shard, `apply_delegate_action` succeeds (nonce valid, key exists), creating an inner receipt with `predecessor_id = Alice`, `signer_id = relayer`.
4. On `some_contract`'s shard, the function call fails (`MethodNotFound`).
5. `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` fires: `deposit_refund = 10 NEAR`, sent to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()` = `receipt.predecessor_id()` = Alice.
6. Gas refund goes to `action_receipt.signer_id()` = relayer (correct).
7. Net result: relayer loses 10 NEAR deposit + gas costs; Alice gains 10 NEAR.

The attack cost to Alice is zero (she pays no gas, has no NEAR balance requirement). The relayer loses the full deposit amount.
