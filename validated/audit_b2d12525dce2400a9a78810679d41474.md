This confirms the key mechanism: comments in `check_transfer_to_nonexisting_account` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:829-849`) explicitly note that "Refunds don't automatically create accounts, because refunds are free and we don't want some type of abuse" and "Account deletion with beneficiary creates a refund, so it'll not create a new account." This means a `DeleteAccountAction::beneficiary_id` that does not already exist as an account (and is not eligible for implicit-account auto-creation, since the refund receipt is exempt from that path) will cause the resulting `Receipt::new_balance_refund` to fail with `AccountDoesNotExist`.

### Title
Permanent burning of a deleted account's entire balance when `beneficiary_id` does not exist or has been deleted before refund execution - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`)

### Summary
`DeleteAccountAction` lets an account holder specify an arbitrary `beneficiary_id` to receive their full remaining balance. The runtime never validates that `beneficiary_id` exists before generating the payout, and the payout is sent as a system-refund receipt, which by design is never itself refunded on failure — it is simply burnt.

### Finding Description
`action_delete_account` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:299-375`) takes `delete_account.beneficiary_id` and unconditionally creates `Receipt::new_balance_refund(&delete_account.beneficiary_id, account_balance)` (line 354) for the account's full balance, with no check that `beneficiary_id` exists. This refund receipt is generated with `predecessor_id: "system"` (`core/primitives/src/receipt.rs:496-509`), and per the documented refund semantics (`docs/RuntimeSpec/Refunds.md:10-12`): "Refund receipts are identified by having `predecessor_id == "system"`… If the execution of a refund fails, the refund amount is burnt." [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

When this refund receipt is executed against `beneficiary_id`, if the account does not exist, the `Transfer` action goes through `check_transfer_to_nonexisting_account` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:829-849`). Critically, the comment explicitly states that refund receipts are *excluded* from the implicit-account-creation exception ("Refunds don't automatically create accounts... Account deletion with beneficiary creates a refund, so it'll not create a new account."), meaning even a syntactically implicit-eligible `beneficiary_id` will not have an account auto-created for it via this path if the action isn't otherwise eligible; a normal named non-existent account certainly fails. The action then returns `AccountDoesNotExist`, causing the refund receipt to fail. Because it is a system-predecessor (refund) receipt, `apply_action_receipt` explicitly does **not** attempt a second-level refund — it instead burns the deposit into `other_burnt_amount` (`runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:926-933`). [4](#0-3) 

This is the direct structural analog of the reported issue: a party entitled to receive value (the club owner / here, the account's own chosen beneficiary) cannot receive it because a strict precondition on the recipient (existing contract w/ `receive()`, vs. here an already-existing NEAR account) is not met, and — unlike ordinary transaction-level transfers where failed sends are refunded back to the sender (see `test_refund_on_send_money_to_non_existent_account`, `integration-tests/src/tests/standard_cases/mod.rs:774-812`, which shows normal ActionErrors on failed transfers roll back and refund the signer) — a failed *refund* has no fallback and the tokens are irrecoverably destroyed. [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
Any account can be permanently and irrecoverably deleted along with its entire NEAR balance if:
- The user makes a typo in `beneficiary_id`, or
- The intended `beneficiary_id` account is deleted (by itself, by an attacker, or via natural churn) between the time the `DeleteAccount` transaction is signed/broadcast and the time it executes (a race condition reachable by any third party who can observe the pending transaction and race a deletion of the target beneficiary account before it lands),

then the account's `amount` — potentially all its funds — is unconditionally burnt rather than returned to the deleting party, matching "permanently frozen/burnt funds" from unauthorized value loss due to a strict, unchecked recipient precondition.

### Likelihood Explanation
- This requires a single, ordinary `DeleteAccount` action, reachable by any account holder with a full-access key — well within the "unprivileged transaction signer" threat model.
- The mistake vector (typo'd/deleted beneficiary) is a realistic and common user/tooling error, and the race-condition vector (front-running deletion of the beneficiary account before the victim's `DeleteAccount` receipt executes) is reachable by any unprivileged actor who can observe pending transactions and submit a competing transaction.
- No special validator, network, or operator privilege is needed.

### Recommendation
Before generating the balance-refund receipt in `action_delete_account`, verify that `beneficiary_id` corresponds to an existing account (or is otherwise guaranteed to be creatable, e.g. explicitly allow implicit-account creation for delete-account refunds rather than excluding it), and reject the `DeleteAccount` action (returning an error, leaving the account and its balance untouched) if the beneficiary cannot receive funds. Alternatively, route delete-account refund receipts through the ordinary (non-system) refund path so that a failed payout to a non-existent beneficiary is returned to the deleting account rather than burnt.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice creates account `alice.near` with balance `B`.
2. Alice signs `DeleteAccount { beneficiary_id: "bob.near" }` where `bob.near` does not exist (e.g., mistyped, or Bob deletes his own account moments earlier/concurrently).
3. `action_delete_account` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:349-355`) removes `alice.near` and enqueues `Receipt::new_balance_refund("bob.near", B)`.
4. This refund receipt executes with `predecessor_id = "system"`; `bob.near` doesn't exist, `check_transfer_to_nonexisting_account` rejects implicit creation for this refund path, and the action fails with `AccountDoesNotExist`.
5. Because `receipt.predecessor_id().is_system()` is true, `apply_action_receipt` does not create a further refund and instead adds the entire `B` to `stats.balance.other_burnt_amount` (`runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:926-933`) — the funds are gone permanently, with no account (Alice's nor Bob's) ever crediting them.
