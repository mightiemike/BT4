Confirmed: `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` protobuf messages have no `expected`/minimum-output field, unlike `ExchangeTransactionContract` which does carry an `expected` field for exactly this purpose [1](#0-0) . This is enough to substantiate the analog.

### Title
Sandwich attack on `ExchangeInjectActuator`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator` due to missing minimum-output check - ([File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java], [File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java])

### Summary
TRON's TRC10 exchange (constant-product-like market) supports `ExchangeInjectContract` (add liquidity) and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (remove liquidity). Unlike `ExchangeTransactionContract`, which carries an `expected` field used as a `minAmountOut` guard, neither `ExchangeInjectContract` nor `ExchangeWithdrawContract` allows the caller to specify a minimum acceptable amount of the counter-token to be paid/received. This mirrors exactly the `PositionManager.deposit()`/`withdraw()` bug class from the report: the counter-token amount is computed purely from the live pool ratio at execution time, with no caller-supplied bound, making the outcome vulnerable to price manipulation ("sandwiching") between transaction broadcast and block inclusion.

### Finding Description
- `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()`/`doValidate()` computes `anotherTokenQuant` from the current `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` ratio at execution time and simply requires `anotherTokenQuant > 0`, with no user-specified floor: [2](#0-1) 
- The protobuf contract for injection carries only `exchange_id`, `token_id`, and `quant` — no expected/min-amount field: [3](#0-2) 
- `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()`/`doValidate()` similarly computes `anotherTokenQuant` (the amount of the paired token returned to the withdrawer) purely from the live pool ratio, again with no caller-supplied minimum: [4](#0-3) 
- Its protobuf contract is likewise missing any expected/min-amount field: [5](#0-4) 
- By contrast, `ExchangeTransactionActuator` (a swap) explicitly takes an `expected` field and enforces `anotherTokenQuant < tokenExpected` as a revert condition, proving TRON's own developers recognized and mitigated this exact bug class for swaps but not for inject/withdraw: [6](#0-5) 

Because any account can broadcast an `ExchangeTransactionContract` against the same exchange pool (subject only to owning tokenID/TRX balance, not creator-restricted), an attacker can front-run a pending `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` transaction with a large swap that skews `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`, causing the victim's inject/withdraw to execute at a manipulated ratio, then back-run with a reverse swap to restore the pool and capture the difference — a classic sandwich attack. Note that inject/withdraw themselves are restricted to the exchange's creator address, so the victim of the sandwich is the pool creator performing liquidity operations, not an arbitrary user.

### Impact Explanation
A successful sandwich forces the exchange creator's inject/withdraw to execute at an attacker-manipulated ratio, causing the creator to receive fewer counter-tokens on withdrawal (asset loss) or to inject at an unfavorable ratio (effectively donating value to the attacker's subsequent swap). This is an accounting/asset-value corruption via the RPC-exposed `wallet/exchangewithdraw` and `wallet/exchangeinject` endpoints combined with `wallet/exchangetransaction`, impacting real on-chain balances.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is medium: it requires the attacker to observe a pending inject/withdraw transaction (via mempool/broadcast monitoring) and race it with swap transactions in the same or an adjacent block — feasible on TRON given its ~3-second block time and public transaction broadcast, but requiring active monitoring and timing, and only impacting exchange creators performing liquidity operations, not arbitrary users.

### Recommendation
Add a `minAmountOut`/`expected` style field to `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (mirroring the existing `expected` field in `ExchangeTransactionContract`), and enforce it in `ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate()` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate()` by reverting when the computed `anotherTokenQuant` falls outside the caller's acceptable bound.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes a pending `ExchangeWithdrawContract` from the exchange creator withdrawing `tokenQuant` of `firstTokenID`, expecting `anotherTokenQuant` of `secondTokenID` computed from the current ratio in `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` at [7](#0-6) .
2. Attacker broadcasts an `ExchangeTransactionContract` swap that shifts `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` before the withdraw is included, changing the ratio unfavorably for the withdrawer.
3. The withdraw executes at the skewed ratio, yielding less `secondTokenID` than expected, with no on-chain check to reject this since `ExchangeWithdrawContract` has no `expected`/minimum field [5](#0-4) .
4. Attacker broadcasts a reverse swap to restore the pool ratio and realize profit from the price impact captured during steps 2–3.
