## Finding: Front-Running of PoisonMicroblock Reward Reports [1](#0-0) 

### Title
Front-running of the PoisonMicroblock reporter reward via public mempool evidence extraction - (File: stackslib/src/chainstate/stacks/db/transactions.rs)

### Summary
The `handle_poison_microblock` routine records whichever transaction's `tx-sender` submits a given microblock-fork proof first (or with the lowest `sequence`) as the "reporter" entitled to the poison-microblock commission. Because the fork evidence (`mblock_header_1`, `mblock_header_2`) is public as soon as the original reporter's transaction hits the mempool, any other party — most powerfully the block-producing miner itself — can copy that evidence into their own signed `PoisonMicroblock` transaction and have it included in the block before or in place of the original, stealing the reward. This mirrors the reported `claimMessage` front-running bug: the payout is bound to `tx-sender`/`msg.sender` of whoever gets included first, not to whoever actually discovered/owns the underlying proof.

### Finding Description
`handle_poison_microblock` derives the `reporter_principal` strictly from the transaction's `sender_principal`, and persists it via `insert_microblock_poison` keyed only by `mblock_pubk_height` and `sequence`: [2](#0-1) 

The tie-break logic only checks whether the newly submitted `sequence` is strictly lower than any existing report — there is nothing that ties the reward to the account that first became aware of, or produced, the fork evidence. The two microblock headers are self-contained, verifiable proof (`check_microblock_header_signer` just confirms both headers share a signer) and are not sender-specific — anyone holding both headers can wrap them in their own signed transaction.

This recorded reporter is later looked up when maturing rewards for the punished tenure: [3](#0-2) 

and is substituted for the miner as the coinbase-commission recipient: [4](#0-3) 

Because the `PoisonMicroblock` transaction must sit in the mempool (or be visible to the block-assembling miner) before it is confirmed, a miner assembling the block — or anyone who can get a transaction included ahead of another in the same block — can:
1. Observe the original reporter's pending `PoisonMicroblock` transaction and extract `mblock_header_1`/`mblock_header_2`.
2. Construct their own `PoisonMicroblock` transaction with the same headers, signed with their own key.
3. Include their own transaction in the block instead of (or before) the original, so `insert_microblock_poison` records the front-runner's principal as reporter.
4. Collect `poison_microblock_commission(coinbase_reward)` at maturity instead of the true discoverer.

This breaks the equality "the party that reported/discovered the fork receives the reward" — the actual on-chain equality enforced is only "the party whose transaction landed first (by sequence tie-break) at a given microblock-key height," which a single block producer unilaterally controls.

### Impact Explanation
This is a reward mis-payment bounded to the poison-microblock commission fraction of a single coinbase (`POISON_MICROBLOCK_COMMISSION_FRACTION` of `coinbase_reward` — see `poison_microblock_commission`), not a chain split or invalid-block issue: all nodes agree on who is recorded as reporter once the transaction is confirmed, so there's no consensus divergence, only economic loss to the legitimate reporter and disincentive to report poison microblocks (analogous to the Postman losing incentive in the source report). This aligns with the "High - poison or reward mis-payment bounded to fees" impact tier.

### Likelihood Explanation
Minority-triggerable: a single block-producing miner (no majority or Sybil needed) can extract the public evidence from the mempool and reorder/substitute their own transaction ahead of the original reporter's. This requires no special privilege beyond normal block-assembly authority that any miner already has.

### Recommendation
Bind the poison-microblock reward to proof of original disclosure rather than raw transaction inclusion order — e.g., require a commit-reveal scheme (hash-commit the evidence first, then reveal), or have the protocol accept the earliest-broadcast/mempool-timestamped proof rather than trusting `tx-sender` of whichever transaction is mined. Alternatively, since evidence is self-verifying, consider making the commission unclaimable by extraction alone (e.g., requiring a signature over the reporter's principal from the microblock signer, which a copier cannot produce).

### Proof of Concept
1. Party A learns of a forked microblock stream and broadcasts a `PoisonMicroblock` transaction (`mblock_header_1`, `mblock_header_2`) signed by A, targeting sequence `S`.
2. Miner M observes A's pending transaction in the mempool, copies the two headers, and creates transaction T' with the same headers but signed by M, at the same sequence `S`.
3. M assembles the block including T' either instead of A's tx or before it. Since `insert_microblock_poison` (stackslib/src/chainstate/stacks/db/transactions.rs:828-832) is only guarded by "no lower-sequence report exists yet," T' is accepted and M is recorded as reporter.
4. At maturity, `calculate_miner_reward` (stackslib/src/chainstate/stacks/db/accounts.rs:872-895) pays the poison-microblock commission to M instead of A.
