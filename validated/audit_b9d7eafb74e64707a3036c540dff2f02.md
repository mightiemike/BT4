### Title
Unauthenticated `RescueFundsOnSourceChain` event trust lets any caller attach an unvalidated, attacker-supplied gas-fee outbound to another user's stuck UniversalTx - (File: x/uexecutor/keeper/create_outbound.go)

### Summary
The CryptoPunk bug is a two-step flow where step 1 (an "approve"-style call, `offerPunkForSaleToAddress`) is performed by the legitimate owner, but step 2 (the value-transferring call, `supplyPunk`) can be triggered by *anyone*, who also controls where the resulting value lands, because the code never checks that the step‑2 caller is the step‑1 actor. `AttachRescueOutboundFromReceipt` in `x/uexecutor/keeper/create_outbound.go` reproduces the same authorization gap in the rescue-funds path: it trusts an EVM log emitted by the `UniversalGatewayPC` contract and reconstructs a pending, TSS-signable outbound purely from attacker-suppliable event fields, without checking that the transaction that produced the log was submitted by the owner of the referenced `UniversalTx`.

### Finding Description
`AttachRescueOutboundFromReceipt` is invoked from `ExecutePayload` (`x/uexecutor/keeper/msg_execute_payload.go:119`), which is reachable by any unprivileged Cosmos account submitting a gasless `MsgExecutePayload` (`app/txpolicy/gasless.go:19`) whose derived EVM sender calls a contract that emits `RescueFundsOnSourceChain`. [1](#0-0) 

The event is decoded and the code:
1. Uses the event's attacker-supplied `universalTxId` (`x/uexecutor/types/gateway_pc_event_decode.go:132-136`) to look up **any** existing `UniversalTx`, with no check that the caller is that UTX's `InboundTx.Sender` or `RevertInstructions.FundRecipient`. [2](#0-1) 
2. Verifies only that the *referenced* UTX is in a "stuck" state (CEA deposit `FAILED`, or non-CEA `INBOUND_REVERT` reached `REVERTED`) — a state condition, not a caller-identity condition. [3](#0-2) 
3. Copies `GasFee`, `GasPrice`, and `GasLimit` **directly from the attacker-controlled event data** into the new `RESCUE_FUNDS` outbound, with no validation, bound-check, or canonical lookup. [4](#0-3) 

This is inconsistent with the normal `INBOUND_REVERT` path, `buildRevertOutbound`, which deliberately fetches gas fields from a canonical, non-attacker-controlled source (`UniversalCore.getOutboundTxGasAndFees`) rather than trusting caller-supplied values: [5](#0-4) 

Because the rescue outbound's final recipient is still pinned to `originalUtx.InboundTx.Sender` / `RevertInstructions.FundRecipient` (not the attacker), an attacker cannot redirect the principal to themselves. However, the attacker fully controls the `GasFee`/`GasPrice`/`GasLimit` fields attached to someone else's outbound, and can trigger this outbound creation for a UTX they do not own, at a time of their choosing, once the target UTX satisfies the "stuck" precondition.

### Impact Explanation
This corrupts gas-fee accounting on a canonical `UniversalTx`/`OutboundTx` record belonging to a victim who never authorized this specific rescue attempt or its gas parameters. Depending on how the gas-fee fields are consumed downstream (paid to whichever relayer/TSS-broadcaster executes the withdrawal on the source chain — a path I was not able to fully trace within the tool-call budget, specifically the exact consumption logic in `universalClient/chains/evm/tx_builder.go`), an attacker-inflated `GasFee` could materially reduce the amount the legitimate recipient ultimately receives, since these values are attached verbatim to a `PENDING` outbound that Universal Validators/TSS will sign and broadcast without re-deriving or bounding them. At minimum this is an unauthorized, attacker-triggered mutation of another user's canonical UniversalTx/outbound gas-fee state — in scope under "corruption of ... gas fee accounting, ... revert destination ... or canonical UniversalTx state."

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. The precondition (a UTX in `FAILED`/`REVERTED` stuck state) is a real, reachable state in production for any inbound whose PRC20 deposit fails or whose TSS revert cannot land, and `MsgExecutePayload` is gasless and callable by any account with a deployed or auto-deployable UEA. The remaining uncertainty is whether the on-chain `UniversalGatewayPC` "rescue" entry point (not present in this repository) itself restricts who may emit `RescueFundsOnSourceChain` for a given `universalTxId` — if that Solidity contract enforces caller == original depositor, this Go-side gap is not independently exploitable. I could not verify that contract's access control within this scan.

### Recommendation
- In `AttachRescueOutboundFromReceipt`, verify that the transaction sender (derived EVM address from `MsgExecutePayload.Signer`, or the `event.Sender` topic) matches `originalUtx.InboundTx.Sender` (or an explicitly authorized rescuer) before attaching a rescue outbound.
- Do not trust `event.GasFee`/`GasPrice`/`GasLimit` verbatim; recompute or bound-check them against a canonical source the same way `buildRevertOutbound` does via `GetGasFeeInfoForRevertOutbound`.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual (Solidity-side rescue entry point not present in this repo, so this cannot be fully demonstrated purely from `push-chain-node`):
1. Identify any `UniversalTx` (`utxId`) on Push Chain that is stuck: a CEA inbound whose first `PcTx` is `FAILED`, or a non-CEA inbound whose `INBOUND_REVERT` outbound reached `REVERTED` — this data is public via UTX queries.
2. From an attacker-controlled UEA (no relation to the stuck UTX's owner), submit a gasless `MsgExecutePayload` whose payload calls the `UniversalGatewayPC` rescue function, supplying the victim's `utxId` together with an inflated `gasFee`/`gasPrice`/`gasLimit`.
3. The resulting `RescueFundsOnSourceChain` log is picked up by `AttachRescueOutboundFromReceipt`, which passes the eligibility checks (state-based only) and appends a `RESCUE_FUNDS` `OutboundTx` to the victim's UTX with the attacker-chosen gas fields, without ever checking that the attacker is the UTX's original sender. [6](#0-5)
