### No Vulnerability found for this question.

Explanation of the review (for transparency, not part of the strict scan format):

I looked for the specific mechanisms called out as valid analogs — cached values not invalidated on source change, a health check followed by a mutating call, folds absorbing failure, and pauses that pass through instead of reverting — across `mainnet/contracts/**`.

- The closest structural match is the vault `accrue` pattern (e.g. `system-borrow`, `redeem`, `deposit` in `v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-usdc.clar`, etc.), where a paused accrual state returns the old index/lindex instead of reverting: [1](#0-0) . This is an intentional pause mechanism uniformly applied to all callers in the same block/pause window, not an asymmetric bug that lets one party extract value at another's expense — it falls under the "deliberate safety design decisions" exclusion.
- `liquidate-multi` uses `map` over positions and documents that failed sub-liquidations don't revert the batch: [2](#0-1) . This is explicitly designed to prevent front-running of bad-debt socialization, not an unguarded fold that strands funds — the docstring and design intent make this deliberate rather than accidental.
- Health-check-then-mutate sequences (`collateral-remove`, `borrow`, `liquidate` in `v0-4-market.clar`) all occur within a single atomic Clarity transaction: the health check, cache priming (`accrue-user-debts`/`accrue-and-cache`), and the state-mutating `contract-call?` to `v0-market-vault` happen in one `let`/`try!` chain with no intervening external call that could move the checked value before the mutation lands [3](#0-2) . Clarity's atomicity means a failure anywhere in the chain reverts all prior state changes in the same transaction, unlike the Mantle bug where `relayMessage` failure leaves ETH/MNT sitting in a contract across separate future transactions.
- No analog of a cross-transaction "funds stranded pending retry, exploitable by any third party" pattern (the actual root cause of the Mantle bug) exists in Zest's `market.clar` / vault contracts — there is no retryable message queue or messenger-style contract holding third-party-claimable balances.

Given the strict validation requirements (reachable path, concrete bound-value/invalidation-event/later-use triple, and exclusion of deliberate design choices), none of the candidate matches clear the bar for a valid analog under the stated rules.
