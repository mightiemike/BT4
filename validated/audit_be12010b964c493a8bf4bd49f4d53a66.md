### Title
Frozen-then-catch-up interest index in vault `accrue()` lets an attacker sandwich a paused-accrual resume to capture disproportionate unclaimed yield - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-sbtc.clar`)

### Summary
Each vault (`v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`) tracks a borrow index and liquidity index that determine the ztoken share price. The `last-update` clock used to compute elapsed time for interest is only advanced when the index actually changes, and while the vault's `accrue` pause flag is set, `accrue()` deliberately passes through without updating the index or the clock at all.

### Finding Description
`accrue()` short-circuits when paused: [1](#0-0) 

When not paused, `next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` compute a multiplier from `interest-rate() × (stacks-block-time - last-update)`: [2](#0-1) 

Critically, `last-update` is only mutated when the resulting index actually differs from the stored one: [3](#0-2) 

While the `accrue` pause flag is set, the vault returns the cached `index`/`lindex` unchanged and `last-update` is never advanced, even though `deposit`/`redeem` still call `accrue()` and continue to operate (subject only to their own independent pause flags): [4](#0-3) 

The share price (`convert-to-shares-preview`/`convert-to-assets-preview`) is a function of `total-assets`, which is derived from `total-debt` = `principal-scaled × index`. Freezing `index` freezes the share price. When the DAO later clears the `accrue` pause flag, the very next `accrue()` call computes `time-delta = stacks-block-time - last-update`, where `last-update` still holds the timestamp from *before* the pause began. The interest multiplier is then applied using the *current* interest rate over that entire elapsed span in one shot, producing a single large index jump (and a correspondingly large one-shot `treasury-lp` mint) instead of the interest having accrued continuously.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who deposits into the vault immediately before the pause is lifted acquires ztoken shares at the stale, frozen (low) price. The very next `accrue()` call - triggered by the attacker's own subsequent `redeem`, or anyone else's `deposit`/`redeem`/`borrow` - applies the entire frozen period's worth of interest in one step, instantly raising the share price for every shareholder, including the attacker's freshly minted shares. The attacker can then immediately redeem, capturing a share of interest that should have accrued to positions held throughout the frozen window, effectively stealing unclaimed yield from long-term suppliers. This falls under the High impact category: theft of unclaimed yield.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires the DAO's `accrue` pause flag (a normal, non-compromised operational lever, not a DAO exploit) to be toggled off after being on for some time - a plausible operational event (e.g., emergency pause/unpause during oracle issues). Any actor watching for the unpause transaction can front-run it with a deposit and follow it with a redeem, entirely within a small number of blocks, mirroring the exact deposit-before/withdraw-after timing described in the source report.

### Recommendation
When resuming from a paused `accrue` state, reset `last-update` to `stacks-block-time` without applying any interest multiplier for the frozen duration (treat paused time as excluded from accrual), rather than allowing the elapsed pause duration to be folded into the next live interest calculation at the then-current rate.

### Proof of Concept
1. DAO sets the vault's `accrue` pause flag to `true`; time passes (`last-update` stays fixed since `accrue()` returns cached values without mutation, per [5](#0-4) ).
2. Attacker observes the DAO transaction that flips `accrue` back to unpaused (or submits immediately after it lands) and calls `deposit()`, minting shares at the stale, low price (share price still reflects the pre-pause index).
3. The same or a following transaction calls `accrue()` (via any vault interaction); `next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` compute a large one-shot jump using `interest-rate() × (stacks-block-time - last-update)` spanning the whole frozen period, per [6](#0-5) .
4. Attacker immediately calls `redeem()`, cashing out shares at the newly inflated price, capturing a disproportionate slice of the "caught-up" interest relative to actual holding time, at the expense of suppliers who held shares throughout the frozen period.
