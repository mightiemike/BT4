### Title
Self-liquidation lets a borrower extract their own liquidation bonus and shift the shortfall onto the lending pool - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
The `liquidate()` entry point in `v0-4-market.clar` never checks that the caller (`liquidator`/`contract-caller`) differs from the `borrower`, and the collateral receiver defaults to the liquidator itself. This mirrors the reported "copy your own portfolio" pattern: a single actor plays both counter-parties of a fee/bonus-generating flow, capturing a bonus that is meant to reward an independent third party, and repeatable at will (self-liquidation instead of self-copy-royalty-farming).

### Finding Description
`liquidate()` computes health from a snapshot (`current-ltv`, `total-collateral-usd`, `total-debt-usd`) and, once `current-ltv >= ltv-liq-partial`, lets `contract-caller` repay `debt-to-repay` and seize `coll-final = debt-repay-usd * (1 + liq-penalty)` worth of collateral [1](#0-0) . The only authorization checks are that `contract-caller == tx-sender` and that liquidation is not paused; nothing asserts `borrower != liquidator` [2](#0-1) . The collateral receiver defaults to the liquidator when unspecified [3](#0-2) .

Because the liquidation-penalty bonus (`liq-penalty-min`/`liq-penalty-max`, scaled by `calc-liquidation-params`) exists to reward an independent liquidator for taking on the risk of clearing an unhealthy position, when `borrower == liquidator` the "reward" is instead extracted by the distressed position itself: the actor pays only `debt-to-repay` (at par) but removes `coll-final` (par + penalty) worth of collateral from their own obligation into their own wallet. Any resulting shortfall that cannot be covered is pushed into the bad-debt socialization path (`bad-debt-socialized` / `other-debt-repayable` computed later in the same function) [4](#0-3) , which spreads the loss across all depositors of the debt vault rather than the position owner absorbing it.

### Impact Explanation
This lets a borrower who intentionally lets (or manufactures) their own position cross `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL`/`LTV-LIQ-FULL` repeatedly self-liquidate, extracting the liquidation bonus that should compensate a genuine third-party liquidator, while any residual bad debt is socialized onto the shared vault (`vault-socialize-debt`) — i.e., paid for by all lenders/depositors. This is a temporary/permanent freezing (and effective theft) of pooled yield: value that should flow either back to a legitimate liquidator or remain in the position is instead siphoned to the self-dealing borrower at the depositors' expense, analogous to the original report where royalty fees meant for a distinct portfolio creator were captured by the copier acting as both parties.

### Likelihood Explanation
No special privileges are required — any account can borrow up to the liquidation threshold and call `liquidate()` on itself, repeating the action across multiple positions/wallets. The only friction is oracle/price movement and the same-block borrow guard (`ERR-LIQUIDATION-BORROW-SAME-BLOCK`), which prevents same-block flash-loan-style self-liquidation but does not prevent deliberate self-liquidation once a position naturally or intentionally crosses the partial-liquidation LTV in a later block.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit check in `liquidate()` (and `liquidate-redeem`/`liquidate-multi`) that `contract-caller` (or `liquidator`) is not equal to `borrower`, or otherwise disallow routing the seized collateral back to the borrower's own principal, similar to how the linked C4 report recommended blocking self-referential royalty capture.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker deposits collateral and borrows debt up to just under `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL`.
2. Attacker lets/forces the position LTV to cross `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL` (e.g., via price movement or additional borrow in a later block, respecting the `ERR-LIQUIDATION-BORROW-SAME-BLOCK` guard).
3. Attacker calls `liquidate(borrower=self, collateral-ft, debt-ft, debt-amount, min-collateral-expected, collateral-receiver=none, price-feeds)` from the same principal.
4. `health-check` passes because `current-ltv >= ltv-liq-partial` [5](#0-4) .
5. `debt-to-repay` is paid by the attacker to close part of their own debt; `coll-final` (debt value plus `liq-penalty` bonus) is transferred to `actual-receiver`, which defaults to the attacker (liquidator) [3](#0-2) .
6. Attacker has extracted collateral value in excess of the debt they repaid; if repeated to the point the position's remaining collateral cannot cover its remaining debt, the shortfall is written down through the bad-debt socialization branch, spreading the loss to other depositors of that vault [4](#0-3) .

Note: I was unable to fully re-verify the exact behavior of the `socialize-debt-asset` fold and its failure-absorption semantics in this final pass (tool access ended before the follow-up read completed); the bad-debt-socialization mechanics cited above are based on the earlier successful reads of `v0-4-market.clar:1381-1530` and `:1561-1620`, and a full audit engagement should re-confirm the exact accounting path from `liquidate()` through `vault-socialize-debt` before treating this as conclusively exploitable end-to-end.
