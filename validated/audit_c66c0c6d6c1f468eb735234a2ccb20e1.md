### Title
Meta-transaction (`Delegate`/`DelegateV2`) deposit refunds are misdirected to the `DelegateAction` sender instead of the relayer who actually paid the deposit - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`)

### Summary
For meta transactions (NEP-366), the relayer pays all gas and attached-deposit costs for the inner actions, but the protocol's refund routing is keyed on `predecessor_id`, which for the synthetic receipt generated from a `DelegateAction` is set to the `sender_id` (the meta-tx signer), not the relayer who funded it. If the inner action fails on the receiver's shard, the deposit refund is paid to the sender, not to the relayer who actually spent the NEAR. This lets an unprivileged transaction signer (the meta-tx "sender") extract value from the relayer by crafting a delegate action with a large deposit attached to an action that is guaranteed to fail.

### Finding Description
`apply_delegate_action` builds a new synthetic receipt whose `predecessor_id` is `sender_id` — i.e. the account that signed the `DelegateAction` — while the deposit and gas for this receipt were charged against the relayer's balance when the outer transaction was converted: [1](#0-0) 

`total_deposit` explicitly documents that the relayer pays the deposit but the refund on failure goes to the DelegateAction's `sender_id`: [2](#0-1) 

Deposit refunds in the runtime are always routed to the receipt's `predecessor_id` because "only the predecessor can attach deposits" — an assumption that is broken by meta transactions, where the economic payer (relayer) and the receipt's `predecessor_id` (sender) diverge: [3](#0-2) 

The nearcore documentation for meta transactions independently confirms this exact mechanism and its consequence — the refund goes to the sender ("Alice"), not the relayer, on a failed inner action, creating a "financial incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached but will fail": [4](#0-3) 

This is structurally the same class of bug as the referenced Cooler finding: an account that legitimately deposited/paid for a request (the relayer, analogous to the Cooler borrower) cannot recover its own funds through the normal failure path; instead, a different, unprivileged party (the meta-tx sender, analogous to Cooler's "owner-only" gate) ends up controlling the refund purely due to an access-control/attribution mismatch (`predecessor_id` used for refund routing vs. actual depositor).

### Impact Explanation
Any account can act as a "sender" of a `SignedDelegateAction` towards any relayer that is willing to relay meta-transactions. By constructing a delegate action whose inner action carries a large attached deposit but is engineered to fail (e.g., wrong method name, insufficient gas, a receiver contract that reverts, or a receiver that doesn't exist), the sender causes the relayer to pay the deposit up front while the automatic on-chain deposit refund flows back to the sender's account rather than the relayer's. This is a concrete unauthorized transfer of value from the relayer to the sender, purely via a single submitted transaction (the outer transaction wrapping the `SignedDelegateAction`), with no cooperation or error required from the relayer beyond agreeing to relay the meta-transaction.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires only: (1) a relayer that accepts and forwards `SignedDelegateAction`s from third parties (a normal, expected operating mode for NEP-366 relayers), and (2) a malicious sender crafting an inner action that is guaranteed to fail after the deposit has already been charged to the relayer. No special privileges, validator collusion, or network-level attack is needed — only a signed transaction/meta-transaction from an ordinary account. The nearcore docs already flag this as a known risk that relayers must defend against operationally (e.g., by not trusting unknown senders or by simulating actions first), which corroborates that the underlying refund-routing behavior is real and exploitable, not merely theoretical.

### Recommendation
Route deposit refunds for receipts spawned from a `DelegateAction` to the relayer (the transaction signer that actually attached/paid the deposit) rather than to the DelegateAction's `sender_id`, e.g. by carrying an explicit `refund_to` on the synthetic receipt built in `apply_delegate_action` (the codebase already has a `refund_to` mechanism, see `ActionReceiptV2::refund_to`) so refunds are attributed to the true economic depositor instead of `predecessor_id`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer `R` agrees to relay meta-transactions for third parties.
2. Malicious account `S` creates a `DelegateAction` with `sender_id = S`, `receiver_id = victim_contract`, and an inner `FunctionCall` action with a large `deposit` and a non-existent `method_name` (guaranteed to fail with `MethodNotFound`).
3. `S` signs the `DelegateAction` and hands the `SignedDelegateAction` to `R`, who wraps it in a transaction and submits it, paying gas and the attached deposit out of `R`'s account balance, per `total_deposit`'s documented relayer-pays semantics (`runtime/runtime/src/config.rs:557-574`).
4. `apply_delegate_action` spawns the inner receipt with `predecessor_id = S` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:456-469`).
5. The inner `FunctionCall` fails on `victim_contract`'s shard; `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` creates a deposit-refund receipt addressed to the receipt's `predecessor_id`, i.e. `S`, per the documented refund rule (`docs/RuntimeSpec/Refunds.md:15-19`).
6. `S` receives the deposit back in full; `R`'s balance is permanently reduced by the deposit amount it fronted, with no compensating refund — matching the behavior nearcore's own architecture docs describe (`docs/architecture/how/meta-tx.md:225-242`).

```rust
pub fn total_deposit(actions: &[Action]) -> Result<Balance, IntegerOverflowError> {
    let mut total_balance = Balance::ZERO;
    for action in actions {
        let action_balance;
        if let Some(delegate_action) = delegate_inner_action(action) {
            // Note, here Relayer pays the deposit but if actions fail, the deposit is

            let actions = delegate_action.get_actions();
            action_balance = total_deposit(&actions)?;

            action_balance = action.get_deposit_balance();
```

```markdown
## Balance refunds in meta transactions

Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor
(a.k.a. sender) of the receipt. This makes sense, as we deposit the attached
balance to the receiver, who has to explicitly reattach a new balance to new
receipts they might spawn.

In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an
inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then
this balance is taken from the relayer.

The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction
and agrees to pay for it, so nothing wrong so far. But what if the transaction
fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and
therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is
something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial
incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached
but will fail on Bob's shard.
```
