### Title
`action_delete_account` unconditionally rejects deletion when gas-key balances exceed the burn cap, temporarily freezing the beneficiary payout - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`)

### Summary
This is a bug-class analog of the Size `withdraw()` finding: a validation guard designed for one purpose (limiting the amount of value that can be silently burned) is applied uniformly to an action whose success the user has an unconditional right to, causing legitimate value to become temporarily inaccessible even though there is no fund loss.

### Finding Description
`action_delete_account` computes the sum of all of an account's gas-key balances and rejects the `DeleteAccount` action outright if that sum exceeds `GasKeyInfo::MAX_BALANCE_TO_BURN` (1 NEAR), before any other effect of the action is applied: [1](#0-0) 
Only after this check passes does the function push the beneficiary refund receipt for the account's regular `amount()` and actually remove the account: [2](#0-1) 

The `GasKeyBalanceTooHigh` check exists to bound how much value can be silently burned during account deletion (gas-key balances are burned, not refunded, on deletion) — this is documented as an intentional invariant: [3](#0-2) . The equivalent single-key check lives in `delete_gas_key`: [4](#0-3) 

The problem is analogous to the Size `withdraw()` bug: the check is tied to the *burn* side-effect of deletion, but it is evaluated *before* the unrelated, unconditional payout of the account's regular NEAR balance (`amount()`) to the beneficiary. A user whose account happens to hold gas keys with a combined balance above 1 NEAR (e.g. because they funded a gas key generously via `TransferToGasKey`, `access_keys.rs:257`) cannot delete their account and reclaim their possibly much larger regular balance in a single action, even though:
1. Deleting the account and burning >1 NEAR of gas-key balance is the only thing being blocked — the regular balance transfer to the beneficiary is unrelated to this cap.
2. The user's regular `amount()` may be arbitrarily larger and is completely uninvolved in the check that's failing.

### Impact Explanation
This is not a fund-loss bug: the user can always reduce gas-key balances below the cap first with `WithdrawFromGasKey` (`access_keys.rs:290`) and then retry `DeleteAccount`, exactly the "still has ways to access funds" mitigation the Size judge accepted for a Medium rating rather than High. However, until the user performs that extra step, a straightforward `DeleteAccount` transaction that should succeed and pay out the account's balance to the beneficiary is rejected with `ActionErrorKind::GasKeyBalanceTooHigh`, freezing the payout for that receipt/transaction. This matches the Size report's core impact: legitimate withdrawal blocked by a validation condition that doesn't logically belong to the value being withdrawn.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable directly and deterministically by any unprivileged account owner: fund a gas key above 1 NEAR via a normal `TransferToGasKey` action, then submit a `DeleteAccount` action naming any beneficiary. No validator or attacker cooperation needed; the condition is entirely self-inflicted account state, comparable to how, in the Size case, ordinary price movement (not an attacker) triggers the false-positive CR check.

### Recommendation
Decouple the two independent effects of `DeleteAccount`: perform the beneficiary payout for `account.amount()` unconditionally, and only apply the `GasKeyBalanceTooHigh` gate to the burn-eligible gas-key balance. Alternatively, when the gas-key sum exceeds the cap, fail only the portion tied to gas-key cleanup (e.g., require prior gas-key withdrawal) while still allowing the underlying value transfer to proceed, or clearly document/surface (via a distinct error) that this is a self-service precondition rather than a protocol-level asset freeze, and ensure wallets/UX handle it by first draining gas keys.

### Proof of Concept
1. Create account `alice.near` with `amount() = 100 NEAR` and a gas key added via `AddKey` (`access_keys.rs:194`).
2. Fund the gas key with `TransferToGasKey { deposit: 2 NEAR }` (`access_keys.rs:257`), bringing `GasKeyInfo.balance` to 2 NEAR (> `MAX_BALANCE_TO_BURN` = 1 NEAR).
3. Submit `DeleteAccount { beneficiary_id: bob.near }` from `alice.near`.
4. `action_delete_account` computes `gas_key_balance_to_burn = 2 NEAR`, which exceeds `GasKeyInfo::MAX_BALANCE_TO_BURN`, and returns `ActionErrorKind::GasKeyBalanceTooHigh` [1](#0-0) . The transaction fails and `alice.near`'s 100 NEAR regular balance is not paid out to `bob.near`, even though that balance has nothing to do with the gas-key burn cap.
5. Only after `alice.near` first submits `WithdrawFromGasKey` to bring the gas-key balance at/below 1 NEAR can the `DeleteAccount` succeed and release the 100 NEAR to the beneficiary — confirming the temporary, self-resolvable freeze analogous to the Size `M-10` finding. This behavior is exercised (for the error path) by `test_delete_account_gas_key_balance_too_high`, referenced in the docs at [3](#0-2) , though I could not locate/verify that specific test's exact assertions with the tools available in this session — a Devin session could pull up `runtime/runtime/src/tests/apply.rs` or `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs` test modules to confirm the exact behavior and boundary conditions.
