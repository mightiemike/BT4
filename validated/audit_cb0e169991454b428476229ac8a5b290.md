### Title
Gas refund surplus leakage in cross-shard meta-transactions - ([File: runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs])

### Summary
A vulnerability exists in the interaction between meta-transactions (NEP-366) and the `AccountCostIncrease` gas pricing model (PV 85). When a meta-transaction is executed across shards, gas is purchased by the relayer at the source shard's gas price, but unspent gas and price surplus refunds are issued to the `signer_id` of the receipt. In a meta-transaction, the `signer_id` is the user (the delegate sender), while the tokens were originally provided by the relayer. This results in a "conversion rate" style exploit where a user can gain tokens from a relayer's gas purchase if the gas price drops or if they intentionally fail the inner actions.

### Finding Description
In NEAR, gas is purchased upfront in NEAR tokens at a `receipt_gas_price` which is `max(current_gas_price, min_gas_purchase_price)` [1](#0-0) . For meta-transactions, the relayer pays this cost at the source shard [2](#0-1) .

The vulnerability stems from how `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` identifies the refund recipient. The function issues a gas refund receipt (`Receipt::new_gas_refund`) which targets the `signer_id` of the original receipt [3](#0-2) . In a meta-transaction, the `signer_id` is the user who signed the `DelegateAction`, not the relayer who paid for the transaction [4](#0-3) .

Under the `AccountCostIncrease` feature (PV 85), if the gas price at the time of execution is lower than the purchase price, a `price_surplus` is calculated and refunded [5](#0-4) . Because this refund is bundled with the gas refund, it is sent to the user (`signer_id`) instead of the relayer. An attacker can sign a meta-transaction with a large amount of attached gas (up to 300 Tgas) and a `max_block_height` far in the future. If the gas price decreases between the time the relayer submits the transaction and when it executes, the user "gains" the price difference in tokens at the relayer's expense.

### Impact Explanation
This is a native analog to the "value vs shares" bug: tokens (value) are converted to gas (shares) at one rate (source shard price) and converted back at a potentially different rate or to a different entity.
- **Loss of funds**: Relayers lose tokens that should have been refunded to them.
- **Balance manipulation**: Users can extract tokens from relayers by exploiting gas price volatility or by simply attaching maximum gas and ensuring the inner action fails (refunding the relayer's tokens to the user's account).
- **Incentive misalignment**: Relayers cannot safely provide services without trusting users not to front-run gas price drops or intentionally fail actions to steal the gas deposit.

### Likelihood Explanation
The likelihood is high because the behavior is baked into the protocol's refund logic for meta-transactions. While relayers are advised to be "aware" of this [6](#0-5) , there is no protocol-level guard to prevent a user from receiving the `price_surplus` or `unused_gas_balance_refund` which was paid for by the relayer's account balance.

### Recommendation
The protocol should distinguish between the "payer" (relayer) and the "authorizer" (signer) in meta-transactions. Refunds for gas (which are priced based on the purchase price) should be routed back to the account that actually debited the tokens at the transaction conversion step (the relayer), while only deposit refunds (for tokens attached via `Transfer` actions inside the meta-tx) should follow the predecessor path.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer (Account R) submits a transaction containing a `DelegateAction` signed by User (Account U).
2. The gas price at Shard 0 (Relayer's shard) is 200,000,000 yN. Relayer pays for 300 Tgas.
3. The receipt is forwarded to Shard 1 (User's shard).
4. By the time the receipt executes, the network gas price has dropped to 100,000,000 yN.
5. `Runtime::apply` on Shard 1 calculates a `price_surplus` of `(200m - 100m) * 300 Tgas`.
6. `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` generates a refund receipt where `receiver_id` is Account U (the `signer_id`) [7](#0-6) .
7. User U receives the tokens, effectively stealing the price difference from Relayer R. [8](#0-7)
