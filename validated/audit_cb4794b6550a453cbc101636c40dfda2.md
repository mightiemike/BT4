This is a confirmed, well-documented value-loss path for meta-transaction relayers, structurally analogous to the Cooler bug: an unprivileged party (the relayer) attaches value/pays the cost of a transaction whose nominal "owner" (the `sender_id`/signer of the inner `DelegateAction`) is a different account, and on failure the value is refunded to that other account rather than to the party who actually funded it.

### Title
Meta-transaction (`DelegateAction`) balance refunds route to the delegate's `sender_id`, not the relayer who funded the deposit, letting a malicious sender steal the relayer's attached deposit on a crafted failure - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`)

### Summary
When a relayer submits a meta-transaction (`DelegateAction`/`DelegateV2`), the runtime spawns a new receipt whose `predecessor_id` is set to the delegate's `sender_id` (the account the inner actions act on behalf of), while all gas and attached deposits for the inner actions are paid for out of the relayer's balance. If the inner receipt fails on the receiver's shard, the standard deposit-refund path sends the refund to `balance_refund_receiver()`, which defaults to `predecessor_id` (i.e., the `sender_id`), not to the relayer who actually paid the deposit.

### Finding Description
`apply_delegate_action` explicitly builds the forwarded receipt with `predecessor_id: sender_id.clone()` while noting in its own comment that "Relayer prepaid all fees and all things required by actions: attached deposits and attached gas... If something goes wrong, deposit is refunded to the predecessor, this is sender_id/Sender in DelegateAction" [1](#0-0) .

The generic refund logic used everywhere in the runtime computes the deposit-refund receiver as `refund_to().unwrap_or(predecessor_id())` [2](#0-1) , and deposit refunds on receipt failure are sent to that predecessor by design ("Deposit refunds ... sent as a refund to a `predecessor_id` (because only the predecessor can attach deposits)") [3](#0-2) .

The project's own architecture docs confirm this is a real, acknowledged financial-incentive problem for relayers: "Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor... if the transaction fails execution on Bob's shard... the predecessor is Alice and therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached but will fail on Bob's shard." [4](#0-3) 

This is structurally identical to the Cooler bug pattern: the party who deposits/pays value (relayer ≈ "User B" who deposits collateral) is not the party who controls the transaction's nominal identity/refund routing (`sender_id` ≈ "User A", the Cooler owner), and the protocol routes the returned value to the wrong party by design, letting the "owner" (here, the malicious delegate sender) capture value that the depositing party (relayer) paid for.

### Impact Explanation
A malicious `sender_id` can construct/solicit a `DelegateAction` whose inner action(s) carry a large attached deposit and a `receiver_id`/method chosen so the inner action will fail deterministically on the receiver's shard (e.g., a nonexistent method, insufficient storage stake on the receiver, or any other guaranteed `ActionError`). If a relayer (who has no reason to fully simulate cross-shard execution before relaying, or who is economically incentivized to relay third-party meta-transactions, e.g. a public relayer service) submits it, the relayer's balance funds the attached deposit, but the resulting deposit-refund receipt is paid to the malicious `sender_id`, not back to the relayer. This is a concrete unauthorized transfer of value from the relayer to an attacker-controlled account, directly reachable by any unprivileged account able to submit a `DelegateAction`/meta-transaction through an untrusted or semi-trusted relayer.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate-to-high in any deployment where relayers accept `DelegateAction`s from third parties without simulating/validating the entire chain of actions before signing/broadcasting (a common relayer pattern for gasless UX, e.g., public relayer services, wallets, or NEP-366-based sponsorship). The attack requires no special privilege — only the ability to author a `DelegateAction` with `sender_id` set to the attacker's own account and to get any relayer to countersign/forward it, which is the entire point of the meta-transaction feature. The project's own documentation flags this exact incentive, indicating the runtime behavior is intentional/known but still represents unauthorized value movement against a relayer who is not sufficiently defensive.

### Recommendation
- Route deposit refunds for meta-transaction (`DelegateAction`) receipts to the relayer (the transaction's `signer_id`/original fee payer) by default, or explicitly require relayers to set `refund_to` via `promise_set_refund_to` on any forwarded receipt that carries an attached deposit, and reject/flag any inbound `DelegateAction` where refund routing is ambiguous.
- At minimum, strengthen documentation/tooling (e.g., relayer SDKs) to force explicit acknowledgement or on-chain enforcement of a maximum deposit/refund-destination policy so relayers cannot be tricked into forwarding large-deposit `DelegateAction`s that are engineered to fail and redirect funds to the `sender_id`.
- Consider protocol-level changes making `refund_to` default to `signer_id` for receipts spawned from `Delegate`/`DelegateV2` actions, since the relayer (signer) — not the delegate `sender_id` (predecessor) — is the actual economic depositor in this one case.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker controls account `sender_id = mallory.near` (no special privileges required).
2. Attacker crafts a `DelegateAction { sender_id: mallory.near, receiver_id: victim_contract.near, actions: [FunctionCall { method_name: "definitely_does_not_exist", deposit: 1000 NEAR, gas: ... }], ... }` and signs it as required by NEP-366.
3. A relayer (public/gasless-relayer service) receives this signed `DelegateAction`, wraps it in a transaction it signs and pays for, and submits it, per `apply_delegate_action` — the relayer's balance funds the 1000 NEAR deposit and all fees [1](#0-0) .
4. The forwarded receipt executes on `victim_contract.near`'s shard with `predecessor_id = mallory.near` (not the relayer) and fails because the method does not exist.
5. `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits`/deposit-refund generation sends the entire 1000 NEAR deposit refund to `balance_refund_receiver()`, i.e., `predecessor_id = mallory.near` [2](#0-1) .
6. Mallory receives back the 1000 NEAR that the relayer paid for; the relayer has permanently lost that value, with no recourse from the protocol itself. This exact scenario and its financial-incentive risk to relayers is described in-repo [4](#0-3) .

```rust
        match self.receipt() {
            ReceiptEnum::Action(_)
            | ReceiptEnum::Data(_)
            | ReceiptEnum::PromiseYield(_)
            | ReceiptEnum::PromiseResume(_)
            | ReceiptEnum::GlobalContractDistribution(_) => &None,
            ReceiptEnum::ActionV2(action_receipt_v2)
            | ReceiptEnum::PromiseYieldV2(action_receipt_v2) => &action_receipt_v2.refund_to,

    }

        self.refund_to().as_ref().unwrap_or_else(|| self.predecessor_id())
```

```markdown
## Balance refunds in meta transactions

Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor
(a.k.a. sender) of the receipt. This makes sense, as we deposit the attached
balance to the receiver, who has to explicitly reattach a new balance to new
receipts they might spawn.

In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an
inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then
this balance is taken from the relayer.

The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction
and agrees to pay for it, so nothing wrong so far. But what if the transaction
fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and
therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is
something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial
incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached
but will fail on Bob's shard.
```
