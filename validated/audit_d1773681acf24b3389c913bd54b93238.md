## Analysis

The Solidity bug class is: a **fixed, hard-coded gas stipend** attached to an external call is insufficient for the receiver's actual execution needs, causing the call to fail (or behave unexpectedly), while the surrounding logic still treats the transfer as settled.

The closest reachable analog in this nearcore checkout is in the `near-wallet-contract`, which lets any relayer/caller submit an RLP-encoded Ethereum transaction that gets translated into a NEAR promise chain. The contract hard-codes several **fixed gas budgets** for cross-contract calls to token contracts / the address registrar, instead of scaling them to what the target contract may actually need: [1](#0-0) 

`NEP_141_STORAGE_DEPOSIT_GAS`, `NEP_141_STORAGE_BALANCE_OF_GAS`, and `REGISTRAR_LOOKUP_GAS` are all fixed at `5 Tgas`, unrelated to the amount of gas the receiving contract's method may actually require — directly analogous to the fixed `2300` gas stipend in `.transfer()`.

More importantly, in `inner_rlp_execute`, the **relayer fee refund is unconditionally dispatched before the underlying action is even attempted**: [2](#0-1) 

and the replay-protection nonce is incremented immediately as well (for the `ERC20Transfer`/base-transfer-without-address-check paths): [3](#0-2) 

Only afterward is the actual promise chain built, which relies on the fixed `NEP_141_STORAGE_DEPOSIT_GAS`/`REGISTRAR_LOOKUP_GAS` budgets: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

If the target NEP-141 token's `storage_deposit` (or the address registrar's `lookup`) implementation needs more than the hard-coded `5 Tgas`, the receipt runs out of gas and the whole action receipt (batched `storage_deposit` + transfer) fails atomically. Failure is only observed later in `rlp_execute_callback`, which is itself budgeted a fixed `RLP_EXECUTE_CALLBACK_GAS` and only refunds `caller_deposit` (not the already-paid relayer `fee`): [6](#0-5) 

Because the relayer fee (paid out of the wallet's own balance) is sent **before** the outcome of the action is known, and the nonce is already consumed, a relayer (or anyone controlling the predecessor account, i.e., an unprivileged caller of `rlp_execute`) is paid the fee even when the underlying transfer fails due to hitting the fixed, insufficient gas stipend on the `storage_deposit`/registrar call — mirroring the `.transfer()` issue where "funds can be lost... Transfer will revert" while the caller has already been compensated.

### Title
Fixed low gas stipend for NEP-141 `storage_deposit`/registrar calls combined with unconditional relayer fee payment allows fee extraction without a completed transfer - (File: `runtime/near-wallet-contract/implementation/wallet-contract/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
The wallet contract hard-codes `NEP_141_STORAGE_DEPOSIT_GAS`, `NEP_141_STORAGE_BALANCE_OF_GAS`, and `REGISTRAR_LOOKUP_GAS` at `5 Tgas` each — a fixed, non-adjustable gas stipend attached to a cross-contract call, analogous to Solidity's fixed `2300`-gas `.transfer()`. If the target contract's method needs more gas than this fixed budget, the call (and therefore the whole batched receipt containing the transfer) fails with an out-of-gas error. Separately, `inner_rlp_execute` unconditionally sends the relayer's `fee` and increments the replay-protection `nonce` *before* the actual transfer action is attempted, so this out-of-gas failure mode results in the relayer being paid for a transfer that never took effect.

### Finding Description
`NEP_141_STORAGE_DEPOSIT_GAS`/`REGISTRAR_LOOKUP_GAS`/`NEP_141_STORAGE_BALANCE_OF_GAS` are static constants (lines 34-36) chosen to match the "standard" NEP-141 `storage_deposit` cost, but any non-standard or heavier-than-typical `storage_deposit`/`lookup` implementation on the receiver side can legitimately require more compute than `5 Tgas`. When that happens, `promise_batch_action_function_call` for `storage_deposit`/the follow-up transfer runs out of gas and the receipt fails as a unit (lines 255-268, 433-458). Because `inner_rlp_execute` already (a) sent the relayer `fee` via `promise_batch_action_transfer` and (b) incremented `nonce` before this cross-contract call chain resolves (lines 358-385), the failure of the downstream action does not undo the fee payment or the nonce consumption. `rlp_execute_callback` only refunds `caller_deposit`, not the relayer `fee` (lines 296-316).

### Impact Explanation
A relayer (an unprivileged, ordinary NEAR account able to call `rlp_execute`) can collect the fee attached to an emulated ERC-20 transfer even though the transfer itself fails due to hitting the hard-coded gas ceiling on the `storage_deposit`/registrar lookup call. This is unauthorized value movement out of the wallet-contract owner's balance: the owner pays the relayer for a service that was not rendered, and the transaction's nonce is consumed so the user cannot retry with the same signed payload. This is a fee/value-movement bypass reachable purely by submitting a normal RLP-encoded Ethereum transaction through the existing `rlp_execute` entry point — no privileged or validator role is required.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: it requires a token whose `storage_deposit` (or the address registrar's `lookup`) implementation is heavier than `5 Tgas`, which is plausible for tokens with more complex storage-registration logic than the "reference" NEP-141 implementation the constant was tuned against. Any relayer routing a `fee`-bearing `ERC20Transfer` (or base-token transfer with `address_check`) to such a token/target is sufficient to trigger the condition; no cooperation from validators or the network layer is needed.

### Recommendation
- Do not pay the relayer `fee` (nor increment the nonce, where safe) until the wallet has confirmed the underlying action succeeded, e.g., by moving fee payment into `rlp_execute_callback` conditioned on `PromiseResult::Successful`.
- Replace the fixed `5 Tgas` budgets with a gas-weight based allocation (as already used for `action.gas()` elsewhere in the file) so the receiving contract's actual call gets a share of unused gas rather than a hard ceiling, reducing spurious out-of-gas failures.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy (or identify) a NEP-141 token whose `storage_deposit` method requires materially more than `5 Tgas` to execute (e.g., due to additional bookkeeping/storage writes).
2. As a relayer, submit an RLP-encoded Ethereum transaction to the wallet contract's `rlp_execute` representing an `ERC20Transfer` to an unregistered `receiver_id` on that token, with a non-zero `fee` for the relayer, from an account other than the wallet's own (`predecessor_account_id != current_account_id`).
3. `inner_rlp_execute` immediately creates and sends the relayer `fee` transfer and increments `nonce` (lines 358-385) before the transfer promise chain executes.
4. The promise chain calls `storage_balance_of` then, in `nep_141_storage_balance_callback`, batches `storage_deposit` (capped at `NEP_141_STORAGE_DEPOSIT_GAS = 5 Tgas`) followed by the actual `ft_transfer` (lines 255-268).
5. Because the token's `storage_deposit` needs more than `5 Tgas`, the batched receipt fails with an out-of-gas error; `ft_transfer` never executes.
6. `rlp_execute_callback` observes `PromiseResult::Failed` and only refunds `caller_deposit`, not the relayer's already-paid `fee` (lines 296-316).
7. Net effect: the relayer has been paid the fee, the nonce is consumed, but the intended token transfer never happened.
