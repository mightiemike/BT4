### Title
Smart contract owner can instantly change `consume_user_resource_percent` (energy-fee split) with no event and no timelock, silently shifting energy cost onto callers - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java`)

### Summary
Any account that deployed a TVM smart contract (the contract's `origin_address`) can broadcast an `UpdateSettingContract` transaction to change `consume_user_resource_percent` to any value in `[0,100]` at any time, with the change taking effect on the very next call. This percentage determines how much of the energy cost of a call is charged to the calling (unprivileged) account versus the contract owner, functioning exactly like the "fee" in the referenced Tracer finding: a value that is fully controlled by one party and directly determines what the other, unprivileged party pays per interaction. There is no event emitted and no timelock/delay before the new value takes effect.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute` unpacks the new percent and immediately overwrites the deployed contract's setting in `ContractStore`: [1](#0-0) 

`validate()` only checks that the caller is the contract's `origin_address` and that the new percent is within `[0, 100]`; it does not require any delay, prior notice, or emit any event: [2](#0-1) 

This value is consumed on every subsequent contract call in `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio`, which splits the total energy limit for a call between the caller and the contract's creator based on `consumeUserResourcePercent`: [3](#0-2) 

If the owner sets `consume_user_resource_percent` to `100`, `creatorEnergyLimit` collapses to `0` and the entire energy cost of every call is shifted onto the calling (unprivileged) account for its very next transaction, with no on-chain event and no notice period. This mirrors the `UpdateAssetContract` case too, where an asset issuer can instantly change `free_asset_net_limit`/`public_free_asset_net_limit` for TRC10 holders with the same lack of event/timelock: [4](#0-3) 

The `UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator` (governing `origin_energy_limit`) has the identical pattern — instantaneous, event-less, timelock-less update that changes how much energy the caller must front: [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
Deploying a contract (`CreateSmartContract`) or issuing an asset (`AssetIssueContract`) is a permissionless action available to any account — not a privileged system role like an SR/witness or committee member — so the "owner" here is exactly analogous to Tracer's unprivileged "market creator." Callers who interact with such a contract or asset have no way to observe, in advance, that the fee-equivalent parameter (`consume_user_resource_percent`, `origin_energy_limit`, or asset bandwidth limits) is about to change. A user can build and sign a transaction expecting a certain energy/bandwidth cost, and by the time it is broadcast/executed the owner may have already shifted the entire energy burden onto them, causing unexpected TRX consumption from their frozen energy or account balance. This is an economic/DoS-style griefing vector against unprivileged callers of contracts/assets they don't control, reachable purely by broadcasting ordinary transactions (`UpdateSettingContract`, `UpdateEnergyLimitContract`, `UpdateAssetContract`) — no special privilege beyond having deployed/issued the target contract/asset.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high: any account can deploy a trivial contract, get users to interact with it while `consume_user_resource_percent` is low, then front-run/change it to 100 right before a known-large call, or repeatedly toggle it. The actuators impose no cooldown, no maximum change per update, and no notification mechanism, so this is trivially reachable via standard `wallet/updatesetting`, `wallet/updateenergylimit`, or `wallet/updateasset` HTTP/gRPC endpoints and ordinary broadcast transactions.

### Recommendation
- Emit an on-chain event/log when `consume_user_resource_percent`, `origin_energy_limit`, or asset bandwidth limits are changed so wallets/dApps can detect and react.
- Introduce a timelock (e.g., N blocks delay before the new value takes effect) for these actuators, consistent with the "confirmed" recommendation in the referenced report for parameters that immediately affect unprivileged callers.
- Consider bounding the magnitude of a single update (e.g., max delta per epoch) to prevent abrupt shifts.

### Proof of Concept
1. Account `A` deploys a contract `C` with `consume_user_resource_percent = 0` (creator pays all energy) via `CreateSmartContract`.
2. Unprivileged users begin calling `C`, relying on paying little/no energy.
3. `A` broadcasts `UpdateSettingContract{owner_address=A, contract_address=C, consume_user_resource_percent=100}` (validated/executed per `UpdateSettingContractActuator.validate/execute`, `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java:40-113`).
4. The very next call from any user is processed by `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` with the new percent, forcing `creatorEnergyLimit = 0` and the caller to cover the entire energy cost (`actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/VMActuator.java:742-745`), with no prior event or warning having been emitted.
