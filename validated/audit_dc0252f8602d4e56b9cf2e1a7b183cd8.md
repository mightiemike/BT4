### Title
Unchecked non-existent `beneficiary_id` in `DeleteAccount` causes irrecoverable token burn - (File: runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs)

### Summary
`action_delete_account` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:330`) pays out the deleted account's remaining balance to `delete_account.beneficiary_id` via a system balance-refund receipt without ever checking that the beneficiary account exists. The only validation performed anywhere in the transaction/receipt pipeline, `validate_delete_action` (`runtime/runtime/src/action_validation.rs:447`), checks only that `beneficiary_id` is a syntactically valid `AccountId` string — not that the account is live. If the beneficiary does not exist (never created, previously deleted, or simply mistyped by the sender), the resulting `Receipt::new_balance_refund` fails at the receiver with `AccountDoesNotExist` (refunds are never allowed to implicitly create accounts, `implicit_creation_allowed`), and the runtime's "refund fails ⇒ burn" rule in `apply_action_receipt` (`runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:1047-1055`) permanently destroys the transferred deposit instead of returning it to the caller or any recoverable account.

### Finding Description
This is the NEAR analog of the reported Solidity bug class ("unchecked destination address leads to funds sent to an unrecoverable sink"). In Solidity's `CvgSdtBuffer.pullRewards`, failing to validate `processor != address(0)` sends rewards to the burn address. In nearcore, the equivalent unrecoverable sink is any nonexistent `AccountId`: NEAR has no implicit "zero address" but has the structurally equivalent concept of "an id with no live `Account` record," and refund receipts are hard-coded to never create one.

The call chain any unprivileged signer can trigger:
1. A transaction signer submits `DeleteAccountAction { beneficiary_id }` where `beneficiary_id` is any syntactically valid but non-existent account (e.g., a fresh unregistered name, or an account the signer previously deleted).
2. `validate_delete_action` only checks account-id string validity, not existence.
3. `action_delete_account` unconditionally queues `Receipt::new_balance_refund(&beneficiary_id, account_balance)` for the full remaining balance of the account being deleted.
4. When that refund receipt executes against the non-existent receiver, `check_account_existence`/`implicit_creation_allowed` rejects it (`is_refund => false`), producing `AccountDoesNotExist`.
5. Because the receipt's predecessor is `"system"`, the failure path in `apply_action_receipt` burns the deposit (`stats.balance.other_burnt_amount`) rather than returning it anywhere.

### Impact Explanation
Concrete, unrecoverable loss of user funds triggerable by a single, ordinary, unprivileged transaction: any account holder who deletes their own account (self-service, e.g. via the NEAR wallet contract's account-cleanup flows, or via a `DeleteAccount` batch action from a contract) and supplies (or is caused to supply, e.g. via a copy/paste or UI bug propagating an unchecked address) a nonexistent `beneficiary_id` permanently burns their entire remaining account balance instead of it being returned to them or refunded. This is a direct, protocol-level "value destruction reachable from a single tx" — matching the required impact bar (unauthorized value movement / permanently frozen or destroyed funds).

### Likelihood Explanation
High likelihood of accidental triggering (typo'd or already-deleted beneficiary id) and is also directly reachable by any signer intentionally, with no privilege required — it's a normal field on a normal action available to every account holder.

### Recommendation
Add an existence check for `beneficiary_id` before allowing `DeleteAccount` to proceed (or, at minimum, before emitting the balance-refund receipt), analogous to `require(processor != address(0))` in the referenced report: either reject the `DeleteAccountAction` outright with an `ActionErrorKind` (e.g. `BeneficiaryAccountDoesNotExist`) when `get_account(state_update, &delete_account.beneficiary_id)?` returns `None`, or route the balance to a recoverable fallback (e.g. refund to the deleting account's `actor_id`/predecessor) instead of a receipt that is guaranteed to fail and burn.

### Proof of Concept
1. Create account `victim.near` with balance `B`.
2. Sign and submit a transaction from `victim.near` to itself containing a single action: `Action::DeleteAccount(DeleteAccountAction { beneficiary_id: "totally-nonexistent-name.near".parse().unwrap() })` (a syntactically valid, never-created account id).
3. `validate_delete_action` passes (id is well-formed).
4. `action_delete_account` runs, account is removed, and `Receipt::new_balance_refund(&"totally-nonexistent-name.near", B)` is queued.
5. That receipt executes; `check_account_existence` rejects it as `AccountDoesNotExist` because it is a refund and cannot implicitly create the account (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:928-934`).
6. `apply_action_receipt` observes `receipt.predecessor_id().is_system()` and the failed result, and adds the full deposit `B` to `stats.balance.other_burnt_amount` (`runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:1047-1055`) — `B` is permanently removed from total supply with no recipient. This exact scenario (refund to nonexistent account) is already exercised by the existing regression test `refund_may_not_create_universal_account` (`runtime/runtime/src/tests/apply.rs:6883-6926`), which confirms the failure/no-account-creation behavior but does not test/guard against the preceding burn of funds from a `DeleteAccount`-initiated refund.
