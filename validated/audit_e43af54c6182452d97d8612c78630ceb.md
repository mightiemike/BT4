### Title
Spot-utilization interest rate is retroactively compounded over the entire stale accrual period, letting an attacker borrow to spike the rate and immediately redeem shares at an inflated price - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar])

### Summary
The vault's `interest-rate()` is a pure function of the *current* (spot) utilization, but `next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` apply that spot rate as a single compounding multiplier over `time-delta`, the entire elapsed time since the last `accrue()` call — no matter how stale that period is. Because utilization itself can be moved within the very same transaction (by borrowing against the vault) and `accrue()` is invoked as the first step of every state-changing entrypoint including `redeem`, an actor can spike the rate immediately before redeeming and have that spiked rate retroactively applied to the whole stale interval, inflating `total-assets-preview` and thus the shares' redemption price in a single transaction.

### Finding Description
`utilization()` is computed from live state: [1](#0-0) 

`interest-rate()` interpolates the rate curve using this spot utilization: [2](#0-1) 

`next-index()` and `next-liquidity-index()` take that spot rate and apply it as a compounding multiplier over the *entire* elapsed `time-delta = stacks-block-time - last-update`, with no time-weighting of intermediate utilization/rate values during that interval: [3](#0-2) 

This computed index feeds `debt-preview`, `total-assets-preview`, and ultimately the ERC4626-style conversion used for redemption: [4](#0-3) 

`redeem` calls `accrue()` first (which persists the manipulated index/lindex and updates `assets`/`last-update`), then immediately prices the caller's shares using the just-updated state via `convert-to-assets-preview`: [5](#0-4) 

The root cause mirrors the Notional finding: a value (interest rate) is read from manipulable spot on-chain state and used to discount/compound a financial claim, with the discounting model implicitly assuming the spot value reflects reality over the whole period being priced. Here that assumption is violated even more severely than in Notional, because the single spot sample is stretched over the *entire* elapsed real-time gap since the last accrual, not just an instantaneous trade.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who already holds `zft` shares (or acquires them cheaply beforehand) can, in one transaction:
1. Borrow heavily against the vault (via `market.clar`'s vault routing to `system-borrow`), which raises `total-borrowed`/`principal-scaled` and lowers `get-available-assets`, spiking `utilization()`.
2. Call `redeem`, which invokes `accrue()`. The spiked spot rate is applied via `calc-multiplier-delta` over the full stale `time-delta` since `last-update`, sharply increasing `index`/`lindex` and hence `total-assets-preview` in a single step — crediting far more "interest" than actually accrued in real time.
3. Redeem shares at this inflated price, extracting assets from the vault's real collateral, i.e., from other depositors.
4. Optionally repay the borrowed amount, reversing the utilization spike while keeping the extracted profit.

Because the extracted value comes directly out of `assets` (the vault's real underlying balance) backing all other `zft` holders, this is a direct transfer of principal from depositors to the attacker — not merely lost future yield — and can leave the vault under-collateralized relative to outstanding `zft` supply, i.e., protocol insolvency for that vault.

### Likelihood Explanation
The precondition — a meaningful gap since the last `accrue()`-triggering call (deposit/redeem/borrow/repay/`set-pause-states`) on a given vault — is common for lower-traffic vaults (e.g., `USDH`, `stSTXbtc`). The attacker only needs enough capital/collateral to move `utilization()` noticeably within one block, and the interest-rate curve is steep near its kinks (per `docs/vaults.md`'s 8-point curve, rates jump from single-digit % to 50-120% APR), amplifying the effect of even a modest utilization shift when compounded over a long stale interval. No cross-user interference is required — the same actor performs both the manipulating borrow and the redemption.

### Recommendation
- Do not apply the spot `interest-rate()` over the full elapsed `time-delta` as if it were constant for the whole interval. Either checkpoint/accrue on every utilization-changing event (borrow/repay) so `time-delta` between rate changes is minimal, or maintain a time-weighted average rate/utilization for accrual.
- Alternatively, cap the utilization/rate used in `next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` to values observed at the start of `time-delta`, or split compounding into sub-intervals bounded by borrow/repay events.
- Add same-block/same-transaction protection: disallow `redeem` (or cap its priced index) in the same transaction as a `borrow`/`repay` on the underlying vault by the same `tx-sender`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Vault `v0-vault-usdc` has `last-update` far in the past (no one has triggered `accrue()`); utilization has been low that whole time (real accrued rate should be near the low end of the curve, e.g., ~2-5% APR per `docs/vaults.md`).
2. Attacker already holds `zft` (USDC vault shares).
3. In a single transaction:
   a. Attacker (via `market.clar`) triggers a large `system-borrow` against `v0-vault-usdc`, driving `get-available-assets` down and `total-debt` up, spiking `utilization()` toward the curve's high-rate region (e.g., 80-120% APR per the 8-point curve).
   b. Attacker calls `redeem` on `v0-vault-usdc.clar`. Inside, `accrue()` computes `interest-rate()` off the just-spiked utilization and applies it via `calc-multiplier-delta` over the entire stale `time-delta`, producing a large one-shot jump in `index`/`lindex`.
   c. `convert-to-assets-preview` prices the attacker's shares against this inflated `total-assets-preview`, and `redeem` transfers out the inflated `inkind` amount of USDC.
   d. Attacker optionally repays the borrowed amount in the same or a follow-up transaction, restoring utilization to its prior level.
4. Net effect: attacker extracted assets from the vault corresponding to interest that never actually accrued over real time, funded by the remaining `zft` holders' backing.

(define-private (next-index)
  (let ((states (var-get pause-states))
        (idx (var-get index)))
    (if (get accrue states)
        idx
        (let (
            (rate (interest-rate))
            (time-delta (- stacks-block-time (var-get last-update)))
            (multiplier (if (is-eq time-delta u0)
                          INDEX-PRECISION
                          (calc-multiplier-delta rate time-delta true))))
          (calc-index-next idx multiplier)))))
