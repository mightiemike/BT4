### Title
Market order matching always rounds down (floor) when computing settlement quantities, causing makers to receive less than their fair proportional amount - (File: chainbase/src/main/java/org/tron/core/capsule/utils/MarketUtils.java)

### Summary
`MarketUtils.multiplyAndDivide` — the core arithmetic primitive used by `MarketSellAssetActuator.matchSingleOrder` to settle TRC10 market orders — always truncates (floors) the result of `a*b/c`, with no rounding-up path for quantities that represent what a party is entitled to *receive*. This mirrors the Bond Protocol M-7 bug class: a settlement/price formula that should round in favor of the party giving up value (the maker) instead always rounds down, silently shorting that party.

### Finding Description
`multiplyAndDivide` is implemented purely with `floorDiv`: [1](#0-0) 

It is used in `MarketSellAssetActuator.matchSingleOrder` to compute the exact amount of tokens each side of a trade receives when settling the on-chain order book: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

In particular, `makerBuyTokenQuantityReceive` — the amount of the counter-asset the resting **maker** order is paid when a portion of its `sellTokenQuantityRemain` is consumed by a taker — is derived as `floor(makerSellRemainQuantity * makerBuyQuantity / makerSellQuantity)`. Just like the whitepaper-specified `mulDivUp` in Bond Protocol's `BondBaseSDA.marketPrice`, this proportional-settlement calculation should round up (ceiling) to protect the maker (the party whose resting order is being executed and who is entitled to *at least* the value implied by their posted price) from being paid less than the exact ratio they committed to. Instead, `MarketUtils.multiplyAndDivide` always floors, so on every partial fill the maker is paid `floor()` of the exact fraction rather than `ceil()`, systematically underpaying makers by up to one unit of the buy token per fill (and, compounded across many small partial fills, materially more).

This is directly analogous to the audited bug: `BondBaseSDA._currentMarketPrice` used `mulDiv` (floor) instead of `mulDivUp`, causing the protocol to sell tokens at a price lower than the whitepaper-specified rounding rule, at makers' expense.

### Impact Explanation
Every maker order that is partially filled in the TRC10 exchange market (`MarketSellAssetActuator`) is settled using floor division for the amount it receives, meaning a resting maker can be paid strictly less than the exact proportional value of the tokens it gave up. This is a concrete, in-protocol accounting/settlement underpayment affecting any unprivileged user who places a maker order on the TRON on-chain order book — not a theoretical or mocked scenario, since `matchSingleOrder` is invoked on every `MarketSellAssetContract` execution that matches against resting orders.

### Likelihood Explanation
This triggers on essentially every partial fill against a resting maker order where the division `makerSellRemainQuantity * makerBuyQuantity / makerSellQuantity` (or the analogous taker-side computations) does not divide evenly — a very common case for arbitrary trade sizes and price ratios, since `sellTokenQuantity`/`buyTokenQuantity` are user-chosen `int64` values with no constraint that they divide evenly. No privileged role or special conditions are required; any two ordinary users submitting `MarketSellAssetContract` transactions with non-evenly-divisible price ratios exercise this rounding-down path.

### Recommendation
For quantities that represent what the maker (or more generally, the party whose committed order is being executed at its resting price) is owed, round up instead of down — analogous to Bond Protocol's fix of switching `mulDiv` to `mulDivUp` in `_currentMarketPrice`. Concretely, introduce a `ceilDiv`-based variant of `multiplyAndDivide` (or a parameter controlling rounding direction) and use it for `makerBuyTokenQuantityReceive` in `matchSingleOrder`, while carefully re-deriving the corresponding taker-side leftover quantities so token conservation invariants (`sell == buy` across all legs) still hold exactly.

### Proof of Concept
Given a maker order with `makerSellQuantity = 3`, `makerBuyQuantity = 2` (i.e., price of 2/3 buy-per-sell), and a partial fill leaving `makerSellRemainQuantity = 1`:
- Exact value owed to maker: `1 * 2 / 3 = 0.667`
- `multiplyAndDivide` (floor) returns `0`, meaning the maker forfeits its remaining sell-side inventory for zero payout in this branch, or in the general multi-fill case receives one fewer unit of buy-token than proportionally owed, per [5](#0-4)  combined with the call site at [4](#0-3) , versus the whitepaper/spec-consistent expectation (as demonstrated by Bond Protocol's `mulDivUp` fix) that such maker-owed quantities be rounded up.
