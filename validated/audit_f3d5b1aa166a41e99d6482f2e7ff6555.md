### Title
Front-Running Exhaustion of Fixed Asset Supply Blocks Other Users' `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` Purchases - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ParticipateAssetIssueActuator.java)

### Summary
`ParticipateAssetIssueActuator` lets any account exchange TRX for a TRC10 token that was issued with a fixed `TotalSupply`. The purchase succeeds only if the issuer's remaining asset balance can cover the requested `exchangeAmount`; otherwise the transaction reverts with "Asset balance is not enough !". Because this check is a simple "current supply vs. requested amount" comparison performed at execution time with no lock/queue and no minimum-fill guarantee, any user broadcasting a competing `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` transaction can be ordered ahead of a victim's transaction (via normal mempool/packing-order effects) to consume the exact remaining supply the victim intended to buy, causing the victim's transaction to revert while the attacker's transaction succeeds and mints them the tokens.

and the actual balance mutation (subtracting from the issuer's remaining pool, crediting the buyer) happens in `execute()`: [2](#0-1) 

There is no reservation, no per-block cap smoothing, and no fee beyond the transferred amount (`calcFee()` returns `0`): [3](#0-2) 

This mirrors the reported bug class: a global, monotonically-approached cap (`maxRaiseAmount` in the report, here the issuer's remaining `TotalSupply`) is checked at execution time and reverts the transaction that would push it over the limit. Because Tron's transaction ordering within a block is determined by the block producer packing order (not strict submission order), an attacker who wants to block a specific victim purchase can submit their own `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` for the remaining supply and get it packed ahead of the victim's transaction, causing the victim's transaction to fail deterministically during `validate()`/`execute()`.

### Impact Explanation
A victim who wants to buy up to the last unit of a fixed-supply, popular token sale can be denied that exact purchase by a front-running competitor, corrupting the intended "first-come, first-served, hard cap" distribution model of TRC10 asset issuance. Unlike the original report's variant, there is no way here for the attacker to costlessly reverse their purchase afterward (no "withdraw" primitive exists for `ParticipateAssetIssueContract`), so the griefing requires the attacker to actually spend TRX and permanently hold the purchased tokens — this materially reduces the practicality/repeatability of the attack compared to the original zero-cost sandwich, but the underlying capped-resource race condition and DoS-of-legitimate-purchase effect is the same class of bug.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: the attack requires the attacker to know the victim's pending transaction (observable pre-confirmation, e.g., via node RPC broadcast) and to have their own competing transaction packed first, which is influenced by block-producer ordering rather than guaranteed control by the attacker. It also requires the attacker to spend real TRX (no cost-free reversal), which limits repeated/automated griefing compared to the reported original bug.

### Recommendation
Consider adding an allocation mechanism that is fair under concurrent broadcast (e.g., partial-fill instead of all-or-nothing revert, or a per-block/per-account cap with pro-rata distribution) for `ParticipateAssetIssueContract`, so that the presence of a hard supply cap cannot be weaponized to selectively deny specific late-arriving purchasers within the same block window.

### Proof of Concept
1. Issuer creates an asset with `TotalSupply = 100` via `AssetIssueContract`.
2. Alice wants to buy the last `10` units and broadcasts `ParticipateAssetIssueContract{amount -> exchangeAmount=10}`.
3. Bob observes Alice's pending transaction and broadcasts his own `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` for the same asset that consumes the remaining `10` units, arranging (via fee/ordering incentives available to block producers) for his transaction to be packed before Alice's in the same block.
4. Bob's transaction succeeds in `ParticipateAssetIssueActuator.execute()`, reducing the issuer's remaining asset balance to `0`.
5. Alice's transaction then fails the `toAccount.assetBalanceEnoughV2(...)` check in `validate()` [4](#0-3)  and reverts with "Asset balance is not enough !", denying her the purchase she intended to make within the fixed-supply cap.
