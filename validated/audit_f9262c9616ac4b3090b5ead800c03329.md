## Title
Relayer fee is paid out unconditionally before the underlying transfer action is confirmed to succeed - (File: `runtime/near-wallet-contract/implementation/wallet-contract/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
The DODO `D3Trading.sol` bug pays out `toToken` to the caller and only afterwards verifies (via balance check) that the caller actually delivered `fromToken`, and the "payment" callback is not guaranteed to correspond to a completed swap. The NEAR Wallet Contract (`near-wallet-contract`) has the same root-cause pattern: in `inner_rlp_execute`, the relayer's fee is transferred out of the wallet's own NEAR balance *immediately*, as an independent receipt, before the actual emulated action (the native `Transfer` or the `ERC20Transfer`/`ft_transfer` chain) has even been attempted, let alone confirmed successful.

### Finding Description
`inner_rlp_execute` in [1](#0-0)  parses the signed Ethereum-style transaction and, for `EOABaseTokenTransfer`/`ERC20Transfer` kinds with a non-zero `fee`, immediately schedules a `promise_batch_create` + `promise_batch_action_transfer` sending `fee` yoctoNEAR to `predecessor_account_id` (the relayer). This receipt is created as a **separate, unchained** promise — it is not `.then()`-attached to the promise that performs the actual action (`action_to_promise(...)` at [2](#0-1) , or the multi-step ERC20 flow in `nep_141_storage_balance_callback` at [3](#0-2) ). Consequently the fee-payment receipt executes and finalizes independently of whether the follow-on action later fails.

By contrast, the contract *does* correctly track and conditionally refund the caller's own `attached_deposit` via `CallerDeposit` only on failure, in `rlp_execute_callback` at [4](#0-3) . The asymmetry — refunding `caller_deposit` on failure but never clawing back (or conditioning) the `fee` payout on failure — mirrors the DODO bug exactly: value is disbursed to an external party (relayer / `msg.sender`-equivalent) speculatively, before the corresponding service (a successfully executed transfer to the intended recipient) is confirmed.

Also note that the nonce is incremented unconditionally once parsing succeeds (see the nonce-increment logic right before the fee-transfer block, [5](#0-4) ), so the signed transaction is consumed (cannot be resubmitted) regardless of whether the transfer ultimately succeeds.

### Impact Explanation
Any unprivileged party who obtains and submits a validly signed RLP transaction (the "relayer" role requires no special permission — it is simply whoever calls `rlp_execute`, reachable by any transaction sender) can cause the wallet contract to pay out the relayer fee even when the underlying `Transfer`/`ft_transfer` fails for any external reason (destination account doesn't exist, NEP-141 token contract reverts/pauses/blacklists the receiver, insufficient gas provided in the signed tx, insufficient token balance, etc.). This is unauthorized value movement out of the wallet owner's NEAR balance with no corresponding service delivered, and because the nonce is already advanced, the user cannot simply resubmit; they must sign and pay a new fee for a new attempt. A relayer who can predict or induce such failures (e.g., by choosing to relay transactions targeting receivers known to be unregistered/blacklisted) can systematically extract fees without delivering value.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable directly by any account submitting a `FunctionCall` to `rlp_execute` with a validly signed Ethereum-style transaction containing a non-zero `fee` — no validator, node, or operator privilege is required. The only precondition is that the follow-on action fails for reasons outside the relayer's control (or that the relayer can arrange), which is plausible in normal operation (e.g., NEP-141 receiver token pauses, blacklists, or insufficient balance at execution time due to a race).

### Recommendation
Chain the fee-payment promise to the outcome of the underlying action instead of firing it independently — e.g., only initiate the fee transfer inside `rlp_execute_callback` after observing `PromiseResult::Successful`, or make the fee-transfer itself part of the same promise batch/chain so that a failed action also fails (or explicitly reverses) the fee payment.

### Proof of Concept
1. User signs an RLP `ERC20Transfer` (or base-token transfer) transaction with a non-zero `fee`, targeting an NEP-141 token/receiver known to reject the transfer (e.g., a receiver blacklisted by the token, or one with a `ft_on_transfer` that always fails).
2. A relayer submits this transaction via `rlp_execute` (see `inner_rlp_execute`, [6](#0-5) ).
3. The fee-transfer receipt is created and executes immediately, crediting the relayer.
4. The subsequent `storage_balance_of` → `ft_transfer` promise chain executes and fails (`PromiseResult::Failed` observed in `rlp_execute_callback`).
5. Result: the relayer has been paid the fee, the wallet's nonce has advanced (transaction consumed), and the intended token transfer never happened — the wallet owner has lost the fee amount in NEAR for no delivered service.
