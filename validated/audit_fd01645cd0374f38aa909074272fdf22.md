### Title
Instant, timelock-free change of contract energy-cost split via `UpdateSettingContractActuator` harms unprivileged contract callers - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java`)

### Summary
The Sandclock finding flags `BaseStrategy.setPerfFeePct` for allowing a privileged role to instantly change a fee percentage that affects unprivileged depositors, with no timelock or grace period. The same pattern exists in java-tron's `UpdateSettingContractActuator`, which lets a smart contract's owner instantly change `consume_user_resource_percent` — the percentage of TVM execution energy that is charged to the *calling* (unprivileged) account rather than to the contract's own frozen resources — with no delay, no rate limit, and immediate cache invalidation.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` unpacks the `UpdateSettingContract`, writes the new `consumeUserResourcePercent` directly onto the `ContractCapsule`, and immediately evicts the contract's cached repository entry so the change is visible to the very next transaction: [1](#0-0) 

`validate()` only checks that the caller is the contract's `origin_address` (deployer/owner) and that the percent is within `[0, 100]`; there is no cooldown, no maximum delta per update, and no time-delayed activation: [2](#0-1) 

This percentage directly determines how TVM execution energy cost is split between the contract owner's frozen resources and the calling account's own resources/TRX burn (`VMActuator`/`Program` consume this value at execution time). Because the change takes effect on the next block with no delay, any unprivileged user who calls the contract — expecting the previously-observed resource split (e.g., 0% so the contract pays for execution) — can be charged up to 100% of the energy cost on their very next interaction, with zero warning.

### Impact Explanation
This is a direct accounting-impact analog to the reported bug class: a role with elevated privilege *over a specific object* (contract owner, analogous to Sandclock's strategy manager) can unilaterally and instantly alter a cost-sharing parameter that unprivileged third parties (any account calling the contract) rely on. Unlike protocol-level parameters (which go through the `ProposalService`/committee voting flow with witness consensus, effectively acting as a timelock — see `ProposalService.process`), this specific per-contract setter has no such governance delay, letting the contract owner front-run or ambush users of their own contract with sudden, unbounded energy-fee shifts.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any contract deployer can invoke `UpdateSettingContract` at will for their own contract; no special network privilege beyond ordinary account/contract ownership is required, and the change is effective immediately (next block), making the "bait-and-switch" pattern trivially reachable by any dApp operator against its unprivileged users.

### Recommendation
Introduce a delay (timelock) before a `consume_user_resource_percent` change takes effect (e.g., activate at the next maintenance cycle or after N blocks, mirroring the built-in one-cycle delay already used for witness brokerage updates in `DelegationStore`/`MaintenanceManager.doMaintenance`), and/or emit an on-chain event so calling contracts/users can detect and react to pending changes before they apply. [3](#0-2) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Account A deploys a contract and sets `consume_user_resource_percent = 0` (contract pays all energy).
2. Unprivileged user B observes this and begins interacting with the contract relying on zero personal energy cost.
3. Account A submits `UpdateSettingContract` setting the percent to 100.
4. `UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` writes the new value and calls `RepositoryImpl.removeLruCache(contractAddress)` immediately: [4](#0-3) 
5. In the very next block, user B's transaction to the same contract is charged 100% of the energy cost from B's own account/frozen resources, with no prior notice or delay — a direct, unmitigated accounting-shift attack against an unprivileged caller.
